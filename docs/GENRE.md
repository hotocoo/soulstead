# The Genre: Successor-Sim

**Working name of the genre:** *Successor-Sim* (store-facing shorthand: **"Settlement Souls"**).
**Reference title in this repository:** *SOULSTEAD*.

## One-line definition

> A weighty action-RPG where you never respawn — you are inherited. Every death hands the
> blade to a simulated person who knew the last holder, and the settlement, the world, and
> your own corpse all remember what happened.

## The three genres being merged

1. **Soulslike action** — commitment-based attacks, poise, dodge windows, readable enemy
   tells, corpse retrieval, mastery over stat inflation.
2. **Colony / life-sim** — a settlement of simulated villagers with relationships, jobs,
   moods, memories, and grief.
3. **Emergent-narrative roguelite (successor line)** — death advances a lineage instead of
   resetting a run.

Nothing on the market fuses all three (see `RESEARCH.md`). The nearest neighbours own exactly
one pillar each.

## The five load-bearing mechanics

### 1. Death is inheritance, not a reset
There is no respawn. When the wielder dies, the settlement holds a **Succession**: you choose
from real villagers, each with their own stats, traits, and — critically — their own
**relationship to the person who just died**. A grieving spouse starts with high Resolve and
low Composure. A rival starts cold and efficient. The successor is a person you have already
seen doing chores in your settlement.

### 2. Your corpse becomes the enemy: the Echo
The body stays where it fell, carrying your gear and your skills, and it re-animates as an
**Echo**. Reclaim it to recover your loadout. Ignore it and the Echo *levels with the world
clock*, wanders, corrupts the region, and eventually **raids the settlement** wielding the
build you spent hours assembling. Corpse-runs stop being a tax and become the game's best
antagonist generator: the hardest boss you fight is always your own last build.

### 3. Grief is a resource, not a debuff
Each death raises **Grief** across the settlement, which suppresses work output and unlocks
nothing on its own. Grief is spent at **Vigils**: funerals, memorials, story-telling, or
burning the dead's gear. Each rite converts grief into a permanent settlement **Legacy** —
a passive that every future successor inherits. Playing badly does not soft-lock you; it fills
the settlement with material to build a legacy from. This is the direct answer to the
punishment-fatigue complaint: the stake stays real, the outcome stays generative.

### 4. Composure replaces the stamina bar
Combat stamina is renamed and re-sourced: **Composure** is a per-character psychological
budget set by who the successor is and how they relate to the dead. Fighting near a memorial,
near a friend's Echo, or in a region your lineage has repeatedly failed in changes the
Composure economy. The souls feel (commit to a swing, pay for it) survives; the number behind
it is now simulation output, not a flat stat.

### 5. The world clock never rewinds
Regions have a decay state driven by your failures: unreclaimed Echoes, abandoned quests,
dead NPCs. Nothing respawns to full. A region that flips is not a game-over — the map
reshapes, routes close, new factions move in. Emergent story from simulation, at indie
content cost.

## What this is explicitly NOT

- Not permadeath roguelike: the settlement, its Legacies, and the world persist.
- Not Rogue Legacy: heirs are simulated people with histories, not stat rolls.
- Not a colony sim with combat bolted on: you are one body, in real time, with a weapon.
- Not difficulty-as-content: difficulty stays high, but failure produces *new content* instead
  of a run-back.

## Why the market should want it

| Player complaint (2026) | Successor-Sim answer |
|---|---|
| "Soulslikes are all the same and punish my time" | Keep the weight, delete the reset; failure generates content |
| "I want story and character, not just bosses" | Every successor and every Echo is an authored-by-simulation story |
| "Indie games can't afford narrative production value" | Narrative is emitted by the sim, not hand-authored per hour |
| "Colony sims have terrible combat" | Combat is the primary verb; the colony is the memory layer |
| "Nothing I do in an RPG world sticks" | The world clock never rewinds |

## The elevator pitch

> *Dark Souls' weight. RimWorld's memory. You die, and the widow picks up the sword.*
