# Market Research — Where the Gap Is (September 2026)

Research pass run before any design decisions. Sources are listed at the bottom.

## 1. Soulslike fatigue is now an explicit discourse, not a hunch

Destructoid argues "soulslike fatigue" deserves to be a real term: the complaint is not
quality, it is volume, price, and the amount of gaming time the genre demands. Their stated
ask is for someone to arrive with an evolutionary formula that is "neither friendslop nor
soulslike."

ComicBook frames 2026 as the year action RPGs overtake soulslikes, because developers are
moving away from difficulty-as-the-hook toward faster combat, character-driven stories, and
player expression instead of punishment.

**Read:** players still want the *feel* (weight, mastery, atmosphere, meaningful death). They
are tired of the *contract* (punishment as the only stake, bonfire reset, run-back tax).

## 2. Hybridisation is already the survival strategy inside the genre

Current soulslike releases are bolting on other structures: PvPvE extraction loops,
first-person flintlock combat, cooldown-instead-of-stamina action RPGs. Remnant 2 is
routinely described as "soulslike with guns." The genre is bending, but every bend so far is
still *combat-shaped* — nobody has bolted soulslike combat onto a **simulation** layer.

## 3. Systemic/emergent design is the affordable answer to a rising production bar

Steam Q1 2026 data (6,169 games in 3.5 months, ~3.8% of the whole library) shows RPG (17%)
and Story Rich (17%) demand is strong but the production-value barrier is rising. Simulation
(26%) and Strategy (24%) demand higher complexity but command a more loyal, higher-spending
audience. Casual and 2D flood the market and fail most often.

Meanwhile 50% of 2026 releases earned under $290, and under 0.5% of indies take 80% of
revenue. Content-volume competition is lost before it starts for a small team. Systemic,
emergent content is the cheap-per-hour answer — RimWorld and Dwarf Fortress generate
narrative from simulation rather than from hand-authored assets.

## 4. The specific hybrid does not exist

Searching directly for "colony/life-sim management fused with soulslike combat where death
permanently alters the world" returns no title that does it. The nearest neighbours each
hold only one half:

| Game | Has | Missing |
|---|---|---|
| RimWorld | AI-storyteller emergent consequence, colony memory | No weighty, skill-expressive combat |
| Dwarf Fortress | Deepest "death changes the world" simulation; Adventurer mode | Combat is not a skill-expression layer |
| Ascent of Ashes | Colony sim + tactical squad combat | Tactical, not action; no successor identity |
| Rogue Legacy | Successor heirs after death | Heirs are stat rolls, not simulated people you knew |
| Deathbound | Souls combat themed around absorbing the dead | No settlement, no persistent world memory |
| Valheim / Sons of the Forest | Base-building + stamina combat | Death is a chore, not content |

## 5. Underserved sub-audiences (positioning, not just genre)

Game Oracle's demand map over 62,000+ Steam developers flags anime RPGs, idle sims, and
atmospheric horror as trending, plus fragmented shooter subgenres that indies underserve.
The recurring practical conclusion across sources: the gap is rarely an empty genre, it is an
**underserved sub-audience inside a crowded one**, reached by a hybrid that cannot be
cleanly categorised.

## Conclusion that drives the design

> Keep soulslike weight and mastery. Delete the punishment contract. Replace the bonfire reset
> with a **simulation that remembers every death** — of your characters and of the people who
> knew them. Death stops being a fail state and becomes the content generator.

That is the design brief for `docs/GENRE.md`.
