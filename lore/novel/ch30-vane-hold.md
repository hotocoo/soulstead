# Chapter 30: Vane Hold

> *Dominion, fifty-one. Mercy, minus twenty-three. Memory, nine. Communion, minus
> forty-four. Drifting toward: The Long Hold.*
> Recorded: the board at Vane Hold, still legible, copied on the ninety-eighth day

**Hand:** Asha Vane, third hand, who is not related to it and is tired of being asked
**Days:** 97 to 99
**Place:** the Vane Road

I will deal with the name first because she did, at the top of the page, in the fast angry
hand, before she wrote anything else.

*I am a Vane out of Brack. There are twelve family names in this valley and eleven of them
are on that wall in some order and if a man asks me again whether they were mine I will
put him in the mere.*

Twelve names. Eleven of them on the wall at Vane Hold in some order. That is the thing
about a valley that has run out of names, and I have written about it in the appendix and
will not repeat it here except to say that Asha Vane was correct, and that four of my own
are on that wall too, and that nobody has ever asked me.

·  ·  ·

The Vane Road runs east out of the Vale along the old river cut, and there is a wall at the
end of it.

You see it from about two thousand paces, if the light is behind you, and what you see
first is that it is straight. Nothing in the Vale is straight. The palisade at Ashfall is
stone for two thirds and timber for the rest and it wanders where the ground wanders,
because it was built by people who were also doing three other things.

This is straight for four hundred paces and turns a corner and is straight again.

It is stone all the way and the courses are level and the corners are set with relic and
you can put a hand on it and not feel a seam. Nobody now living could build that. I want
to be exact: nobody now living in this valley or in Holt Vale or in Verren Hold or in any
of the eleven holds whose boards I have copied entries from has four Carvers to spare for
a season, or knows how to draw a course that long true, or would be permitted to try.

It is the work of giants, which is the phrase the Wayfarers use and which they did not
invent either; it comes out of one of the copies, from a hand that had it from somewhere
further off, about some other wall.

Where has it gone, the hand that set those corners. Where has it gone, the quarry rota, the
lime, the four Carvers and the man who kept their tally. Where has it gone, the argument
about whether to bring the courses round the north side or leave the north side timber,
and who won it, and what the winner said in the yard afterwards, and who laughed.

The stone is there. The wall stands. It will stand after Ashfall Hold is a shape under
grass and I have written that sentence in the knowledge that Ashfall Hold is where I am
sitting.

·  ·  ·

The gate is barred from inside.

Asha Vane and two Foragers went out on the ninety-seventh day, on a Wayfarer's directions
and against Colm Grey's advice, which he gave once and did not repeat, and it took them
most of a day to walk it and most of an evening to get in, and they got in over the wall
at the north-east corner where a beam had failed inward, and not through the gate, because
the gate could not be opened.

Two arms' worth of timber. That is her measure and I have kept it. Two arms, laid across
the gate in brackets set into the stone, in courses, the way you would lay up a wall if
your wall were made of trees.

The brackets are iron and they were made for it. Nobody improvises iron. Somebody at Vane
Hold stood at a fire and made brackets to hold two arms of timber across the inside of a
gate, on a day when the gate was still being opened, in anticipation of a day when it would
not be.

The timber is scored. She noted the scoring and did not count the marks. Grieve Mourne
counted them on the two hundred and tenth day and there were a great many and they were all
recent, which is the only sentence in this book that I am going to leave without any
commentary at all.

·  ·  ·

There are no Echoes at Vane Hold.

None. Not one. Three people walked that hold for two days and a night and there is not a
ring of bent grass anywhere inside the wall, nor outside it within a morning's walk, and
the Foragers are Foragers and would have found one.

Their board records four wielders dead in thirty days. Four, in a month, which is a rate
no hold in any copy I have has matched, and every one of those four died outside, and every
one of them was left, and their rings would have been in the fields east of the wall,
where the fields are ash now, and their rings are not there.

They roamed. That is what happens at the third growth, and past the third growth they keep
going, and a thing that keeps going long enough leaves its region and then leaves its
valley. There are four somewhere. There have been four somewhere for a long time, carrying
four Vane names and four Vane loadouts and the skill of four people who died badly in one
month, and they have been fed by every piece of ground they have crossed since.

On the hundred and ninety-sixth day, ninety-eight days after Asha Vane wrote this page,
Orrin Dunn died at the palisade of Ashfall Hold against a fourth growth that belonged to
nobody we could name, in plate we did not recognise, with a name on it we could not read
because we do not have the hand.

I am not going to claim it was one of the four. I have no way to know and I will not
pretend to. I will write down that the possibility has been raised, by the Foragers and by
me, and that nobody in this Hold has ever wanted to discuss it twice.

·  ·  ·

What is inside the wall is one hundred and sixteen Husks.

Asha Vane counted them on the ninety-eighth day. She counted them twice and got the same
number twice and wrote it down once. The Foragers refused to help with the counting and she
has not held it against them in anything she wrote.

They are not in a heap. That is the part I have the most trouble putting on a page.

They are at the looms. They are in the doorways. One is at a table with both hands flat on
it, sitting on a bench, and the bench has been sat on for a long time and has taken the
shape. One is at the well with the handle. Two are in the lane between the byre and the
workshop, facing each other, about three feet apart. Eleven are in what was plainly the
Hearth, which at Vane Hold is a stone room with a chimney and benches round three sides,
and the eleven are on the benches, and there is a cask.

A Husk walks its last chore. Everybody in the Vale knows that. What the Vale has not had to
look at, before the ninety-eighth day, is a settlement in which everybody died of the same
thing at about the same time and every single one of them was performing an ordinary duty
when the grip closed.

They were not fighting anything. There is nothing there to fight. There is no mark on the
wall, no breach, no burning, no bones in the lanes. One hundred and sixteen people were
weaving and drawing water and sitting on benches and then they were not, and then the
grip closed round them and set them walking, and they have been at the looms since.

Asha Vane's note: *they are still working. Nobody is left to be fed by it.*

·  ·  ·

Here is the reconstruction and I offer it as a reconstruction. Three people spent two days
in a hold that had been shut for longer than anyone can date and came back with a count, a
board, and a theory, and I have copied the theory out of her hand and have not improved it.

The clock runs on whoever is outside.

That is the oldest observed fact in the Vale. Bran Holt worked it out on the twentieth day
of our Hold and we have steered by it for two hundred days: nothing advances while every
body is inside the wall, and a settlement with nobody in the field is a settlement in which
the bread does not rise and the pot does not cool and the ground does not go one state
worse.

Vane Hold lost four wielders in thirty days. They conscripted nine people off the rota into
Warden kit, which is nine sets of shoulders out of the fields and nine holes in the work,
and their board has all nine entries and the Dominion climbing five at a time. They culled
their own sick, twice, and both entries are there, and that is where the Mercy went.

And then somebody stood in that stone Hearth with a board on the wall and did the
arithmetic that anybody in any hold could do and nobody else has ever had the nerve to
finish, and said: **the decay comes from us being out in it.**

So they stopped going out. They laid two arms of timber across the gate in brackets made
for the purpose.

And they were right. That is the thing. That is why this chapter is in the book and why I
have given it three pages of weather. They were not wrong about the mechanism. The ground
outside Vane Hold did not go one state worse from the day that gate was barred to the day
Asha Vane came over the north-east corner, and it has not gone one state worse since, and
the fields east of the wall are ash for reasons that predate the barring, and everything
past the ditch line is exactly as it was.

They solved it. Nothing came. Nothing decayed. Nothing grew a growth.

What they did not solve is that a Keeping costs grief, and grief is only spent at a rite,
and a rite has to be held by somebody, and one hundred and sixteen people inside a barred
gate with nothing to do are one hundred and sixteen people whose grief has nowhere to go
and whose fields are outside.

Their board shows it. The last legible dozen entries are `vigil_skipped` and
`vigil_skipped` and `vigil_skipped`, and no deaths recorded between them, which means
either that they stopped entering the deaths or that the skipping was the only verb left
that described anything they were doing.

Asha Vane's last line on the theory, and it is the line the Hold has taken from her and
uses now about all sorts of things:

*They were not wrong. They were only finished.*

·  ·  ·

The board is in the stone Hearth on the north wall, out of the weather, and it is legible.

I have copied it in full and it is in this book in another chapter and I am not going to
put it twice. Four numbers and a line. Dominion fifty-one, which is the highest figure any
hold in any copy has ever reached. Mercy minus twenty-three. Memory nine, which is the part
people do not expect and which I find the saddest of the four: they were not an Unnaming
hold. They kept cutting panes. Nine is a hold that was still trying.

And Communion minus forty-four.

The last line still says what it said. It has said it for however long it has been saying
it, in a room with eleven Husks on the benches and a cask that is still there, and the
letters are cut deep because whoever cut them cut all their letters deep.

Bran Holt's note on the copy, made the following spring, is four words: *the board does not
lie.* And underneath it, in his same dull complete hand: *it also does not help.*

·  ·  ·

They held a Burial on the ninety-ninth day.

Three people, two days, and a hold with one hundred and sixteen in it. Asha Vane does not
say how many they managed and I have asked two of the three and neither will give a number
and both of them said the same thing in almost the same words, which is that they stopped
when they stopped.

Eight of Memory. Four of Mercy. Five of Communion. One rite, for a hold of a hundred and
sixteen, by three people from a valley away, one of whom had four of her own names on the
wall and did not mention it.

It is the only rite ever held at Vane Hold that anybody can prove was held.

Dominion stood at eleven in Ashfall Hold on the ninety-ninth day, which was the highest we
ever went, and which we reached four days earlier by pulling a Carver out of her shed on
her wedding morning, and which is forty short of the number cut into the board she was
copying.

I do not think she noticed the comparison while she was making it. I have thought about
that a great deal and I think she was too busy counting.

## The Other Roads

### Stripping it

There is good iron in those brackets and there is dressed stone in four hundred paces of
wall and there is relic set into the corners, and a hold that is short of relic is a hold
that cannot raise a Monument, and a hold that cannot raise a Monument cannot hold ground.

A hold three valleys south did this. I have their copy. They took the corner relics out of
a barred hold over two seasons and set them under stone at home and their board climbed on
`relic_spent` eleven times, five of Memory and three of Communion a time, and they will
tell you, and they are not lying, that it was the most pious season they ever had.

What is not in their copy, and what a Wayfarer told me, is that the wall came down the
following winter at the corners, and that the Husks came out of it.

`relic_spent` ×11 · Memory +55 · Mercy 0 · Communion +33 · Dominion +11

**Pressure:** toward **The Wake**, on the numbers, and that is exactly why this road is in
the book. The board cannot tell the difference between a relic spent on a Monument for
your own dead and a relic prised out of somebody else's corner. Eleven times five is
fifty-five either way.

### Barring it back up and cutting no pane

The cheapest road and the one most holds take, and there is no shame in it that I can find
and I have looked.

Three people cannot bury a hundred and sixteen. A hold a morning's walk away cannot
maintain a Memorial for a hold a day's walk away. Bar the corner, walk home, enter nothing,
because there is no verb for having looked at something terrible and left it alone.

`vigil_skipped` · Memory -3 · Mercy 0 · Communion -2 · Dominion +2, if the hold is honest
enough to enter even that. Most enter nothing, and nothing is not a neutral act on a board
that only knows what a hand put in it.

**Pressure:** toward **The Quiet Field**, which is where most roads go, and which this
book has been instructed to treat with more respect than the other four.

### The road-shrine

The Wayfarers proposed it on the hundred and first day, two days after the Burial, at our
crossing post, and Colm Grey refused, and his reason was that the Hold had three Carvers
and a wall to finish.

Their proposal was this: panes at Vane Hold, cut by anybody, for anybody, from any valley,
and the pearl for the glass brought in by whoever is walking, and a hold that is a day from
everywhere made into the place you stop.

It would have cost Ashfall nothing but Carvers.

`foreign_echo_honoured` and `vigil_held` in an ongoing series, six or eight of Memory and
seven or nine of Communion at a time, indefinitely, for as long as the road has traffic on
it.

**Pressure:** hard toward **The Open Hearth**, and this is the road Holt Vale took in a
different form eighty days later and got the ending for. Ashfall refused it because of a
wall, and got a different ending, and I have written elsewhere in this book that the
board's order of questions decided between two valleys that had reached the same number,
and that I find it intolerable.
