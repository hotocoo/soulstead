# Chapter 14: The Tellers

> *A death recounted aloud in full. The Teller holds the graver. The room is not the hold.*
> Recorded: the second pane in the east range, cut by Grieve Mourne at twelve, who got the K wrong

**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Day:** 21 · **Place:** the Memorial

The Telling was held at the Memorial on the evening of the twenty-first, with the panes behind us and the ash pit to the east and every working person in the Hold standing in a half circle with their backs to the open air. Twenty-two of them, which is everybody, because the Healers had said that anybody who could stand had to stand and that was the only standing law anyone in the Hold had yet written down.

The Telling is one of the five Vigils the Hold has. It costs twenty-five of grief, which is more than a Burial and less than a Pyre, and what it buys is eight in a hundred more of Composure on every wielder who comes after, forever, which is the Legacy. The numbers are on the board and the numbers are the only reason a Hold of twenty-two working people on a hill above the fields would give up an evening and a cask and a third of the grief pool that should have gone to the next dead and not to the dead already laid down.

What a Telling is, in the body, is this: one person speaks, in front of everyone, all of one death, from the start of the day to the end of the day, and the room holds still for it. That is the rite. There is no cut. There is no pane. There is no vessel. The graver is held, and it is the only implement, and it is held so that what is said can be recorded if the Teller can keep up, and if the Teller cannot keep up the graver is set down and the Telling is told again by somebody who can.

We held it on the twenty-first because we owed Rin's name a Telling and we owed it before we owed it anything else, and because the grief pool stood at fifty on the morning of the twenty-first and twenty-five of it was a price a Hold of twenty-two could pay without the bread going thin.

·  ·  ·

Maren Verren told it.

She stood at the centre of the half circle and the graver was in her hand and she did not look at any of us and she did not look at the panes and she looked past us into the open air behind us and she told the fourteenth of the Hold out loud in the order it happened.

She told the morning cold and clear and the ash with a crust on it from the night. She told Rin at breakfast standing up, and Maren at the table with her hands round a cup, and Bran Holt coming in with the tally board and saying the long grass had put on a foot and things were coming out of it. She told the cutting grass in short savage strokes and the stopping with her hands on her knees. She told the first Stalker coming out of the grass on the left and dying in one swing because she was already mid-stroke, the second from the right in the recovery, twelve, the third and the fourth together out of the loose ash at the foot of the cut bank.

She told the body going down on one knee from the ash and not from a blow. She told the slow sidestep. She told the coulter's old curve catching the third one under the jaw. She told the fourth one.

She stopped.

The room held still. She told it again from the part where she could.

Here is what she got wrong, and I am writing this down because Bran Holt's hand is the one being kept and I am Bran Holt and the wrong is mine to record, and because Grieve Mourne has asked me to record it and I am doing it once and I am not doing it twice.

The cup on the table from breakfast was hers. She said it had been on the table from breakfast, which is right. She said nobody had moved it, which is wrong. The Healer who was in the room on the evening of the fourteenth has told me, and I have asked her twice because I wanted to be sure, that she saw Maren move the cup herself. She put it down on the night of the nineteenth, when the cup had been on the table for five days because nothing in the Hold had moved. The Healer is sure. The Healer is also sure that Maren does not know that the Healer saw.

The slope on the west side. She said the Kettle had been before it changed its mind, and that is right. She said the cut bank was the good ground and the good ground was why there was loose ash under the grass at the foot of it. She said the Forager who would have told Rin about the loose ash was at the kilns that morning, burning shell. That is what Bel Brack heard from the palisade frame, and I have it in my notes, and she is right about the kilns.

She is wrong about the direction Rin was facing when she went past the fence. She said she was facing east. Sila's entry on the fourteenth says the middle of the morning and does not say which way Rin was facing. The palisade frame faces west, and Bel Brack was on it, and Bel Brack says she went past facing south, with the cut bank on her left. I have asked Bel Brack about this and Bel Brack is now seventeen and has not been asked anything by me in a year and was happy to be asked and gave me the same answer he gave me the first time and gave me one extra detail that he had not told me before, which is that she stopped at the fence and put her hand flat on the post. I am recording that here because it is a piece of the day and I did not have it when I kept the hand, and because the post is the same post the doorstone is on the other side of and the hand on the post and the hand on the doorstone are not the same hand and were not at the same time of day and I am not going to be the one who says they were.

Two details wrong out of an afternoon of about four hours. The cup. The direction at the fence.

I have written both the telling and the corrections into the Chronicle and I am not going to choose. I have been asked to choose and I have refused.

·  ·  ·

The Telling took about an hour.

I have graver cuts of an hour of speech and they are not easy to read. The lines are uneven. Some of them are not lines. I have sat at the table with the wax under the lamp and read the cuts by the way they catch the light and I have written down what I can, and what I cannot I have left blank, and I have put the word *said* in the margin where the blank is so that a reader knows the Telling was fuller than what I have.

Maren told it without looking up. The graver was in her right hand and her left hand was at her side and she did not move the left hand for the whole hour, which is the kind of detail Bran Holt records and Bran Holt's readers resent him for. Her right hand made three mistakes, two of them in the second half. The first mistake was a verb in the wrong tense and I have corrected it in the margin. The second was a name that should not have been there at all, the name of a man who was not at the Hold on the fourteenth, and I have marked that with a single question mark in the margin. The third was the cut at the bottom of the hour when her hand slowed down on a long word and the knife ran a hair out of the line and I have let that stand because the cut is true to the moment and the line was going where the line was going.

What she said about Rin after the telling was over was nothing. She handed the graver to me and she walked to the gate of the Memorial and stood with both hands on the top bar and did not move for a long time, and then she came back and sat on a bench and Bel Brack brought her a cup of water and she drank it and that was the end of it.

·  ·  ·

The Tellers' doctrine is older than the Hold and the Hold has not been the only place to argue it.

It is this. A bond between two people does not end when one of them dies. It changes shape. The dead person is no longer in the room, but the dead person's grip is, and the room that continues to hold that grip has more in it than the people standing in it. The dead person is held by the Telling the way a pane holds a name: not by being alive, but by being called. The Hold that carries its dead forward has, in its daily work, the hands of people who have known loss, and the hands of people who have known loss work differently from the hands of people who have not.

This is not a fancy. It is on the board. Eight in a hundred more of Composure, on every wielder who comes after, forever. A wielder with the Telling's Legacy spends less of their purse when they swing and gets more of their purse back when they stop, and the difference is the eight, and the eight is the difference between a person who walks home from a fight with nothing left and a person who walks home from a fight with enough left to do it again.

The Telling is therefore not only a funeral. It is a piece of inheritance. The Legacy is the inheritance. The room at the Memorial is where it is decided.

This is the doctrine. It is also the honest weakness in the doctrine and the reason a Hold will hold a Telling for a name when the settlement needs the grief spent on something faster.

Ketil Brack said so at the Hearth the next morning. He said it to me and not to Maren and not in front of the Hold, because Ketil Brack is a careful man when he is being careful and a blunt man when he is not and this morning he was being both. He said the grief pool stood at fifty on the morning of the Telling. The Telling spent twenty-five of it. The other twenty-five is what the next death has to bury itself with, and the next death is not a year off, the next death is the death that is going to happen because they always are, and the twenty-five in the pool is not enough for a Pyre and is not enough for a Monument and is just enough for a Burial if the Burial is somebody who is not well loved, and the Telling was held on the eve of an expedition because Rin was well loved and the twenty-five spent on her Telling is gone.

He is right. I have not written down what he is right about because I have been keeping this hand for two days and I have not been able to put it into words that do not make the Telling sound like a mistake. The Telling is not a mistake. The Telling cost the Hold a quarter of its grief on the eve of a season in which the grief was needed.

That is the whole of the argument and it has not been settled and I am not going to settle it.

·  ·  ·

I am putting on the Chronicle tonight what I have, which is the Telling and the two corrections and the graver cuts and the cup of water and the hour. Grieve Mourne, who is nine and who is apprenticed to nobody yet, is not in the room. He is on the ladder at the palisade frame where he is not supposed to be and he is looking through the rails at the Memorial and he has the look on his face that he is going to have for the rest of his life when he is looking at a thing he wants to write down. I record what was said.

He will be on this hand one day and he will know about the cup and the direction and the name that should not have been there. He will know about it because I am writing it here and because somebody is going to give him this Chronicle to keep and he is going to read it before he keeps it and he is going to keep both the wrong details and the corrections and he is going to do the thing I am doing now, which is not to choose.

He will do it because I did it.

I am Bran Holt and I am the second hand of the Chronicle of Ashfall Hold and I have recorded a Telling and I have refused to choose and I have spent twenty-five of grief and the Hold is twenty-five lighter than it was and I am going to bed.

---

Grieve Mourne, compiling. I am writing this in the fourth hand and I am thirty-two and the two wrong details are still on this page in the second hand and I have read them nine times and I do not know which was wrong. Bran Holt knew. Bran Holt left it to me.

The cup was hers. The direction was south. The name that should not have been there was a name Bran Holt would not have known because Bran Holt was not at the Hold on the fourteenth, and Bran Holt recorded it because Maren said it. Maren said it because the Telling was about a person she had slept next to for six years and the name she said instead was a person she had slept next to before that and the Saying was not a mistake, it was the Telling doing what a Telling does, which is letting the bond show its shape.

I have not written that in the body of the chapter. I have written it here.

I will not write it in the body of the chapter.
