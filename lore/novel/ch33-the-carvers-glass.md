# Chapter 33: The Carver's Glass

> *Pearl from the pyre of the named. The glass will run at a heat a wood fire can reach.*
> Recorded: the Smithy's hot end, with Grieve Mourne at twelve cutting his first pane

**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 72 to 74 · **Place:** the Smithy, the Memorial

A pane is glass. The glass has a name in it. The name is fluxed into the glass with pearl, and the pearl is pot-ash, and the pot-ash is lye boiled dry, and the lye is pyre-ash steeped and settled and drawn off clear, and the pyre-ash is the ash of the body whose name is in the pane.

A pane is a body and a name and a piece of glass and a smelter and a smith and a fire, and the pane is not a pane until the smith has put the pearl into the sand at the hot end of the fire and the fire has been hot enough to run the glass at a heat a wood fire can actually reach, and the wood fire is the fire the Smithy has, and the Smithy has had the wood fire since the founding, and the wood fire is what the wood fire is.

This is the material chapter. The Chronicle has been kept for seventy-two days and the Chronicle has not yet said how a pane is made. The Chronicle has been about the panes, and about the smears, and about what the panes do for the dead and what the panes do for the Hold, and the Chronicle has not yet said what the panes are made of, and the panes are made of what the panes are made of, and the what is what I am spending this chapter on.

·  ·  ·

The ash is the start.

A pyre is a fire. A pyre is a body on a fire. The body is gear and bones and flesh and the gear and the bones and the flesh are the body's hold, and the body's hold is what the Loosing took and the Loosing took it and the body is on the fire and the fire is taking the body's hold and the fire is making the body's hold into ash, and the ash is the body's hold in a different posture, and the posture is the one the Smithy can use.

The ash is gathered. The ash is gathered by the Carvers, who are the people whose job it is to gather ash, and the gathering is done with wooden shovels and wooden buckets because iron takes the ash's hold and the holding is what the ash is for.

The ash is steeped. The ash is steeped in water, in the wooden vats by the Smithy door, for three days. The water takes the ash's hold into the water. The water is lye, and the lye is what the ash is in the water for, and the lye is drawn off clear and put into the wooden casks that hold it for the rest of its life.

Lye is what soap is made of. Lye is what the Hold sells, and soap is the only thing the Hold has ever sold to anyone, and soap is what the soap-makers make, and the soap-makers are the people whose job it is to make soap, and the soap-makers are mostly the Field-hands who have lost a finger or a hand and who cannot field-work any more.

Lye is also what pot-ash is made of. Pot-ash is lye boiled dry, in the iron pots over the Smithy's hot fire, and the boiling takes the water out and the lye becomes a powder and the powder is grey and the powder is pot-ash and the pot-ash is what the Carvers want.

Pot-ash is what pearl is made of. Pearl is pot-ash refined again, in a smaller iron pot, with more care, and the care is the Carvers' care, and the Carvers' care is what the Carvers are for. Pearl is a finer white powder and the powder is what the smith puts into the sand at the Smithy's hot end, and the smith puts the pearl into the sand at a measured rate and the measured rate is what makes the glass run.

Glass is sand melted. Sand needs a heat a wood fire cannot reach. Sand with pearl melts at a heat a wood fire can reach. Sand with pearl is what glass is in the Vale, and the glass is what the panes are.

·  ·  ·

A pane is glass with a name in it.

The name is cut into the glass with a graver. The graver is a piece of iron with a sharp end and the sharp end is what cuts the glass and the cutting is the cutting and the cutting is what the Carvers do and the Carvers are the people whose job it is to cut.

The name is fluxed with pearl. The pearl is pot-ash refined again, and the pearl is pot-ash refined from the pyre of the person whose name is in the pane, and the pearl is what the pane is fluxed with, and the fluxing is what puts the pearl into the cut of the graver, and the pearl fills the cut and the pearl makes the cut white and the white is the white of the name, and the white of the name is the white of the body that was burned to make the pearl, and the name is written in the substance of the named.

I am going to say this once and I am not going to press it.

A pane is a name written in the substance of the named.

The substance is pearl. The pearl is pot-ash. The pot-ash is lye. The lye is pyre-ash steeped. The pyre-ash is the body burned. The body burned is the person named. The person named is in the pane in the substance the person named was burned to make. The pane is glass. The glass holds the substance. The substance holds the name. The name holds the person. The pane holds the name.

This is the most important physical fact about the Memorial and I am going to state it plainly once, early, and never explain it again.

·  ·  ·

The problem is on the board.

The Hold has buried more than it has burned. The Hold has buried nine dead for every one the Hold has burned. The buried dead yield no pearl. The buried dead are in the ground whole and the ground is what is keeping the buried dead's hold, and the ground does not give the hold back for glass-making and the ground does not give the hold back for pane-making and the ground does not give the hold back for anything except the rite the buried dead was buried with, and the rite was the Burial and the Burial is a machine that moves hold from the grip of the dead and into the grip of the living and the Burial does not move hold into the Smithy's pot.

The Holden glass shortage is nine panes deep. The Holden Memorial has nine names on the board that have not been cut into panes. The nine names are waiting for glass. The waiting is on the board and the board reads the waiting in the backlog and the backlog is nine panes and the nine panes are the names of nine people whose bodies are in the ground and whose hold is in the ground and whose pearl is not in any pot in the Smithy and whose pane is not in any frame in the Memorial.

The Holden backlog is the reason the Stranger's Echo was buried with a pane on the seventy-first day. The Holden backlog is the reason the Carvers had to be argued into cutting the pane. The Holden backlog is what the Unnaming has been pointing at for a season, and the pointing is the part of the Unnaming's argument that has weight, and the weight is on the board.

A relic is what was spent on the seventy-fourth day.

The relic is the unnamed relic from the Gravewarden Maren killed on the twenty-seventh day of the Hold. The relic sat on the Smithy shelf for forty-seven days. The relic was unbound and the relic was not bound into any blade because the relic was the relic that was going to be the relic that decided whether the relic from the Stranger's Echo was going to be bound into something or spent on something, and the Stranger's Echo had not arrived and the relic had nothing to decide and the relic sat.

The relic was spent on stone on the seventy-fourth day.

The stone is the second Monument in Brackwood, set in the third stand from the south, with the relic under it, and the relic is unnamed, and the stone is unmarked except for the stone, and the stone holds eleven paces of Quick ground around itself in ground that had been Greying since the season before the Hold came, and the eleven paces are Quick now and the eleven paces were Quick before the seventy-fourth day but the eleven paces were Quick because of the relic under the first Monument and the eleven paces on the seventy-fourth day are Quick because of two relics and the two relics are what is holding the second stand and the second stand is what is holding Brackwood.

The board read what the board read on the evening of the seventy-fourth. Memory plus five. Mercy zero. Communion plus three. Dominion plus one.

The reliquary was spent on stone. The stone was raised over a piece of ground the Hold had already pushed back from Greying once. The pushing back was the eleven-day work of the forty-seventh day and the relic is the stone's defence of the work and the work is what the eleven days of the forty-seventh day were for.

The reliquary is not bound to a blade. The reliquary is bound to a stone. The reliquary is the relic of a person the Hold did not know and the person is somebody and the somebody is on the second Monument in Brackwood and the somebody is not on a pane.

·  ·  ·

Grieve Mourne is twelve years old.

He has been apprenticed to the Carvers for three weeks. He is small for twelve and he has the forearms for cutting glass and not much else, and the Carvers put him on the cutting bench on the morning of the seventy-third because the Carvers were short a hand and the Carvers had nine panes of backlog and the Carvers needed a pane cut and the pane was Bel Kettle's and Bel Kettle had not been a pane until today because the pane had not had glass until today and the glass had not had pearl until today and the pearl had not had a body until today, and the body was Bel Kettle's and the body had been in the lake for forty years and the body was not on a pyre.

The pearl for Bel Kettle's pane was not from Bel Kettle.

The pearl was from a stranger's pyre. The stranger's pyre was the stranger's Echo the Hold buried on the seventy-first day, and the stranger was Sila Verren, and the stranger's flask had been at the stranger's belt and the stranger's flask had been full of a stranger's ash-water and the stranger's ash-water had been the stranger's pyre-water and the stranger's pyre-water had been the pearl the Carvers were refining on the seventy-third day.

The pane was cut on the morning of the seventy-third. Grieve Mourne held the graver. The graver was an iron with a sharp end and the sharp end was not steady in his hand. The pane is Bel Kettle's pane and the pane is in the eastern range and the pane has the K wrong.

The K is wrong because the K is not the K. The K is the K's first stroke and the first stroke is a vertical line and the vertical line is the line a person of twelve makes when the hand has not learned the K yet, and the K's second stroke is a diagonal and the diagonal was a hair out and the diagonal ran into the first stroke and the K is a closed shape with a diagonal in it that does not belong to a K, and the shape is not a K and the shape is not a letter and the shape is what a person of twelve makes when he is cutting a pane for a person whose body he has never seen and whose name is on a board and whose board is on the wall.

Grieve Mourne knows the K is wrong. He has not been told the K is wrong. The Carvers have not been told the K is wrong because the Carvers did not look at the K because the Carvers were not at the cutting bench when the K was cut. The pane is on the frame and the pane is in the eastern range and the K is wrong.

The K is going to be wrong for the rest of the book's life and the book's life is the life of the pane and the pane is going to be in the Memorial for as long as the Memorial is in the Memorial, and the K is going to be wrong, and the K is going to be the wrong of a boy of twelve who cut his first pane with the graver in his right hand and the right hand shaking because the right hand had never held the graver before.

Grieve Mourne is at twelve. He has the forearms for cutting glass and not much else. The pane is the first pane he has ever cut. The pane is for Bel Kettle. Bel Kettle is a child whose body has been in the lake for forty years and whose Echo has been in the shallows for forty years and whose Echo was reclaimed by Maren Verren on the thirty-eighth day of the Hold.

The pane is in the eastern range. The K is wrong. The pane is the only pane in the Memorial that has been cut by a person of twelve and the pane is the pane the Hold will show to whoever asks what the Memorial looks like.

The pane is going to be there for the rest of the book.
