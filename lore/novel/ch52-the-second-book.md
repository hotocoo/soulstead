# Chapter 52: The Second Book

> *Whoever cuts the line under this one, write down what it cost.*
> Recorded: the first page of the Chronicle of Ashfall Hold, in Sila Carver's hand,
> dated the first day and cut on the ninth

**Hand:** Grieve Mourne, and one other
**Days:** not entered
**Place:** the Hearth

The book is full.

That is not a figure of speech and it is not the end of anything. It is a fact about a
shelf. The Chronicle of Ashfall Hold is four hundred and six leaves of vellum and
eleven waxed boards, and the boards have been scraped to the grain and cannot take another
course of letters, and the vellum is finished because there is no more vellum, because
vellum is skin and skin is the byre's business and the byre has other calls on it.

There is also the pearl. A Teller does not only cut a Chronicle; a Teller cuts panes, and
a pane wants pearl, and the pearl comes out of the pyres, and this Hold buries more than
it burns and always has. The backlog stood at nine panes on the seventieth day and stands
at four now, which is the best it has been, and four is still four names in wax waiting
for glass.

So: a second book.

Nobody in the Vale has begun one. Verren Hold's copy runs to three volumes and their
third stops mid-entry. Holt Vale keeps loose leaves in a chest and has done since Tove
Rowan, which is a system and not a book. The unsigned fragment is seven pages and was used
as packing.

Beginning a second book is a rite. It costs nothing, it moves no number, there is no verb
for it, and every person in the Hold turned up.

·  ·  ·

We did it on an afternoon. I am not going to give the day.

I have given every day in four hundred pages, including the days I would rather not have,
including the hundred and twenty-eighth and the hundred and thirtieth, and I am withholding
this one, and the reason is the only piece of sentiment I have permitted myself: a day
that is entered can be counted, and a thing that can be counted goes on a board, and I did
not want this on a board.

Whoever reads this and wants the date can work it out. It is between the two hundred and
tenth and the end of me, and the range is narrower than it used to be.

Eleven people in the Hearth and the rest of them coming in and out. The old board on the
north wall with four numbers on it, locked. A new board, planed that morning, blank, with
nothing on it but four words at the top and a fifth line ruled and empty.

And the argument, which took the whole afternoon, and which was the Four Readings one more
time, in one room, in miniature, and each of them won something, and I am going to set down
what each of them won because that is the whole of what I have learned in seventy days of
compiling.

·  ·  ·

**What the Tellers wanted, and got.**

Every name. All of them, copied forward in full, wielders and villagers and the four
Brackwood sick who lasted a hundred and sixty-two days past the quarantine, and the boy at
the fire, and the stranger.

They wanted the deaths recopied with their days and their subjects and their four numbers
intact, and they got that, and it took the new Carver and me nine days, and the nine days
are not in either book.

Their argument was the one they have always made and it has never been improved: the bond
does not end, it changes shape, and a Hold that carries its dead forward has more in it
than the living could manage on their own. They can now point at a wall. Every Legacy this
lineage owns came from a rite, and every rite came from a name somebody insisted on
keeping, and a Teller in the Hold on that afternoon could say out loud that a man of
thirty-two with a graver's forearms and no training had gone out and come back because
nine people had been properly kept, and nobody in the room could answer it, because it is
on the board and anybody can check.

**What the Unnaming wanted, and got.**

The wax.

I want to be precise about what happened, because it has been misreported already and I
have been in the room and the room had eleven people in it.

Sila Grey stood up, at sixty-one, with the same hands, and said that she was not going to
make the argument again because everybody had heard it and nobody had ever demonstrated
against it or for it, and that she wanted one thing, which was that the eleven waxed
boards be scraped and not copied.

The boards hold the ledger. The vellum holds the writing. Every entry exists twice, once
as a cut line on a board and once as a sentence in a book, and the boards are what a Hold
reads at the turn of a season and the book is what a Wayfarer carries.

She said: copy the book. Scrape the boards. Let the Hold have to open a book to say a
name, instead of the name being on the wall at eye height where it is said forty times a
day by people walking past it on their way to the byre.

She was not asking for anybody to be forgotten. She was asking for the difference between
a name that is kept and a name that is called.

We scraped the boards. The new board went up with four words and a ruled line and nothing
else, and the names are in the second book, which lives on a shelf, and a person who wants
to say one has to go and get it and open it and find the leaf.

I do not know whether she was right. I have written four hundred pages and I do not know.
The Hold's low rows are Greying as I write this and were Quick in the spring, and Sila Grey
would say that is because we have said eleven names a thousand times each in the last
seventy days, and a Teller would say it is because nobody has walked out to the low rows
since the Wake, and both of them can produce a reason and neither of them can produce a
season with the other thing held still.

**What the Hearthkeepers wanted, and got.**

The failures.

There is a section in the appendix of this book listing the rites this Hold has attempted
and failed, and it includes both attempts on Kettlemere, and the second attempt cost a
Healer three fingers and gained nothing, and the Healers wanted that copied forward exactly
as it stands, failures intact, with the dates and the fingers.

Their argument: a hold that copies only its successes is a hold that will try the same
thing again in eleven years with somebody else's hands.

Nobody opposed it. It is the only motion of the afternoon that nobody opposed.

**What the Cull wanted, and got.**

The numbers on the sick.

They wanted the Brackwood ledger copied with the labour figures beside it. Nine out of the
pool plus two Healers, on a Hold of twenty-something, for a month, and four of the nine
dead anyway, and the output figure for that month set next to the output figure for the
month before it.

They did not want the mercy struck out. I have been asked this by two Wayfarers and the
answer is no, they never asked for that, and I have never heard the Cull of this Hold ask
for anything to be removed from a board in twenty years.

What they asked for is that the cost be copied next to the choice. They got it. It is on
leaf forty-one of the second book, in two columns, and the columns are what they are.

The Cull of Ashfall Hold are the reason there is a Hold. I have written that in three
places and I am writing it in the last chapter so that it cannot be skipped.

**What the Open Road wanted, and got.**

A second copy.

Not carried. Kept, here, on a different shelf, in a different room, so that if the Hearth
burns the Chronicle does not.

They have been asking for this since the twenty-fifth day and they have been refused for
two hundred days on the grounds of vellum, and this afternoon they were not refused,
because the room had just spent an hour discussing what a thing is worth when it exists in
only one place, and because everybody in it had read the unsigned fragment.

I copied that fragment into this book in full, on the seventieth leaf, so that a hold with
no name would be in two places instead of one. That is the only rite available for a hold
with no name and I performed it and I have said elsewhere in this book that it is the thing
I am least ashamed of.

The Open Road got their second copy and a Wayfarer is carrying the third, and if you are
reading this in a valley I have never walked in, that is why.

**What the Long Hold wanted, and got.**

Nothing.

There was no advocate in the room. I have looked for one in this Hold for twenty years and
I have never found a person who would stand up and make the case, and I have found a great
many who would make it sitting down, in a byre, about a particular gate, on a particular
evening.

They got nothing on that afternoon and they will get everything eventually, because the
Long Hold is not a doctrine anybody holds. It is what a hold becomes after it has been hurt
inside its own lanes once. It is waiting in the second book already, in the blank ruled
line under the four words, the same way it was waiting in the first.

**What the Gravewardens wanted.**

There was nobody to ask.

**What the Yielding wanted, and got.**

The chapter about the orchard, copied whole, with the argument in it and no answer after
it.

I wrote that chapter and I wrote no answer after it because there is not one, and the
Healer who went north on the hundred and second day made the best case anybody in this Vale
has ever made for anything, and her case is that the rite is a theft and the ground is owed
and they leave no Echo.

Nobody has to go out with a maul for them. That is the sentence. It has been in this book
since the eighteenth day and it has been unanswered for four hundred pages and it is in the
second book now, on leaf ninety, unanswered, and I am the one who copied it there.

·  ·  ·

What did not carry: the grief.

There is none. A Wake takes sixty and then it takes everything else, and we held one on the
two hundred and tenth day, and the pool has been at nothing since. The second book opens on
a Hold that owes nothing and is therefore a Hold that cannot afford a rite, and every
Teller before me would have found that funny, and Asha Vane would have written something in
the margin about it.

What did carry: the Legacies. All of them. They cannot be lost. That is not a rule anybody
made; it is the only thing in the Vale that has never once behaved otherwise.

Six of health for every Burial. A flask for the Pyre. Eight in a hundred of what comes back
when you stop swinging, for every Telling. A tenth of the arm for every Monument standing
with a relic under it. And whatever a Wake leaves, which is five in a hundred and a yard
that stays green.

A successor in the second book starts where Rin Ashfall could not have reached in a year.
She had nineteen years and four days and a sword made out of a plough. Whoever picks it up
under the second board will start with nine people's deaths in their arms and will not have
met any of them.

I have written the inversion once, flatly, in the chapter about the Wake, and I am not going
to dress it up here. They are stronger because those people died. I do not know what to do
with that. Neither did the three hands before me. It is the first thing in the second book
that has no entry and no verb, and it is the reason there is a second book at all.

·  ·  ·

The new board went up with four words and a ruled line.

Memory against Oblivion. Mercy against Ruin. Communion against Isolation. Dominion against
Surrender. All four at nothing, which is not the same as at peace, and the fifth line ruled
and empty, because the board does not say a thing until somebody has done something.

Nobody has ever accounted for that line. Not Sila Carver, not Bran Holt, not Asha Vane,
not me. It has been right about four other valleys and I have copied all four of their last
pages into this book and I still cannot tell you who put the words on the wall of a
settlement that was nine days old.

I planed the board myself and I ruled the line myself and I could have left it off.

I did not leave it off.

·  ·  ·

The knife went in with her. That was the reasoning on the ninth day and nobody has improved
it: a grip closes round things, so the things go too, and Sila Carver put Oda Ashfall in the
ground with her knife because the knife was part of the grip.

Two hundred and ten days later a man who had never carried anything went out to the ninth
ring in the Grey Ash with four flasks and a sword with a plough's curve still in it, and
came back, and put the sword on the hook, and it is on the hook now.

The hillside north of the ninth ring has nothing on it. Ash to the ankle, nine rings, a dead
orchard, and the shoulder of the Mourne behind. I walked out there in the spring to set the
last of the stone and I stood in it for a while with the lime on my back.

It is worth looking at. I do not know whether that is courage or quotation and I have
stopped thinking the distinction is worth what I used to think it was worth.

·  ·  ·

The first page of the second book is cut and I have put it here, whole, in the letters it
was cut in.

```
D1 of the second book. 31 of us. Pool at nothing.
Board planed, four words up, fifth line ruled and empty.
Legacies carried: all. Names carried: all. Grief: none.
Sila Carver asked what it cost. It cost nine of us, and it bought
the rest, and I have written down both halves so that neither can
be quoted without the other.
Whoever cuts the line under this one: the same.
```

There are two hands on that page.

I cut the first four lines and I know I cut them. The fifth and sixth are not mine, and
the seventh is, and the eighth I am no longer certain about, and I have held the leaf to
the fire and to the light, which is what Bran Holt's scraped line taught me to do, and the
letters are better than mine.

I am not going to settle it.

I record what was said. I have done that for seventy days and four hundred pages and nine
of my own people and one stranger and four other valleys, and I have not settled one thing
in any of it, and the instruction on the first page of the first book has now been answered
on the first page of the second.

Somebody will pick up the blade tomorrow.
