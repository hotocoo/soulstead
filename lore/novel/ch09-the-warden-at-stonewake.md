# Chapter 9: The Warden at Stonewake

> *It said a name four times and it said it at the top of every swing. It was not my mother's name. I have asked everybody.*
> Rin Ashfall at the Hearth, thirteenth day, in Sila Carver's first hand

**Hand:** Sila Carver, with a marginal note in Bran Holt's hand
**Days:** 13
**Place:** Stonewake Scarp

The errand was lime.

Sila Carver wanted mortar. She had four stones cut for the Memorial and nothing to set them in, and she had been saying so at the Hearth for three evenings in the voice she used when she had already worked out that nobody was going to give her anything. Ketil Brack wanted to know whether the quarry would give him a block big enough to stand the anvil on, because the anvil was standing on the cart's axle bed and moving a thumb's width every day. Bran Holt wanted to know how far it was.

Three wants and one pair of legs. The rota does that the way water goes downhill.

Rin Ashfall went out on the thirteenth day with the blade on a proper strap, because Ketil had seen about the strap, and with the two-fingers-wide cut above her left hip packed again that morning by Wren Ashfall, who had told her a second time to walk and not run.

She put two fingers in the dip on the doorstone on her way out.

·  ·  ·

Stonewake is rock. That is the first thing about it and almost the whole of it.

It comes up out of the east side of the Vale in one piece, grey and dry, with the quarry cut into the foot of it where somebody before the Hold had taken out stone in squares and left the squares stacked. The kilns are three, round, shoulder-high, built of the rock they stand on, and two of them were sound on the thirteenth day and have been sound every day since, and the third had gone in at the crown and has never been rebuilt.

There is no grass on the scarp. There is no tall grass anywhere on it. That is worth putting down beside what happened, because it means she had it wrong in her body before anything came, and she said afterwards that she had been watching the wrong thing all the way up.

And her arm was her own up there.

She noticed it at the kilns, doing nothing, only standing and looking into the sound one, that whatever it was that had made the swinging post cheap on the Memorial ground and heavy in the barley past the fourth stake was not doing either on the scarp. She had four of the hard ones and she had all four of them. Nothing of hers had ever stopped on that rock. Nothing in that rock had ever known her.

There is no word in the Vale for what that is worth and the Vale has had two hundred days to find one.

·  ·  ·

The barrows are above the quarry, on the shelf, two hundred paces up a track that was cut for carts and is wide enough for two.

There are nineteen of them. She counted nineteen and every count since has been nineteen. They are low, longer than they are wide, turfed over and going grey at the crowns, and each of them has a door in the end that faces the Vale: two uprights and a lintel and a slab across, all of the scarp's own stone, set by somebody who was better at it than anybody now living.

Eleven of the slabs were down or leaning. Six were shut and could be moved.

Two were shut and could not. One of those two is not a door at all. It is a wall built into the doorway, coursed and mortared, the depth of an arm, and whoever built it built it from the outside and then went away. Nobody opened it on the thirteenth day. Nobody opened it for a long time.

Rin Ashfall did not go into any of them. She said so at the Hearth and two people said afterwards that she had said she went into one, and both of those accounts are in the Chronicle and neither of them was written by somebody who was on the scarp.

She was standing on the track between the fourth and fifth barrow, looking up at the shelf and not at her feet, when the one that was standing in the fifth barrow came out of it.

·  ·  ·

It was upright before she saw it and it had been upright a long time.

It wore plate, which is a thing the Hold had heard of from Colm Grey and had never seen. Not one suit of plate. Plate over plate over plate, lapped and strapped and pinned, down the arms and over the chest and round the thighs, so much of it that there was no shape of a person in it anywhere, and every piece of it grey, and none of it rusted.

Thirty of reach. It carried something long in both hands and the Chronicle has four descriptions of it and they do not agree about the head of it.

Thirty of speed, which is less than half of what had come out of the grass the day before, and that was the only part of the whole business that was kind.

She went to twenty-six and hit it.

Nothing happened.

She hit it again, low, at the hip, where she could see a strap, and nothing happened. The blade arrived, and the sound was the sound of iron going onto iron, and the thing did not step and did not turn and did not stop doing what it had been doing, which was coming towards her at thirty.

That is poise. It is the word Sila Carver wrote in a column on the eleventh day for the packing a body wears. A body with enough of it on is not a body you can argue with. What is on a Gravewarden is fifty-five, and the Hold did not know that number on the thirteenth day and has known it since the twenty-seventh, and fifty-five is more than four times what came out of the grass in Wrenfield.

She learned the rest of it in the next half-minute and it is all in her own telling of it at the Hearth, which Sila took down at the time and did not tidy.

She was twenty-six. It was thirty.

There was no place to stand. She went round it, and it turned, and she was inside thirty the whole time she was anywhere she could reach it from, and she was outside twenty-six every time she was anywhere it could not reach her from. Four of something. She had no word for the four and she said so twice.

·  ·  ·

Then it swung, and while it was swinging it said a name.

Sixty-two hundredths.

That is one full swing of a lead bob on a hand and a half of waxed cord, out and back, which Ketil Brack proved afterwards, standing in the Smithy doorway with the string in his hand, saying nothing, for as long as it took him to do it eleven times.

You can see it coming from the other side of a barrow. That is what sixty-two hundredths buys you, and it is a great deal, and the Hold has buried people who had it.

The voice was not breath. There was nothing in the plate that was breathing. It came out of it the way the sound of iron comes out of iron, at the top of the swing, one word, in the flat way a man counts sacks.

It said *Halde.*

Rin Ashfall had been ready since the barrows came into sight. She had been ready for her mother's name. She had walked two hundred paces up a cart track being ready for her mother's name and she had not told anybody she was doing it, and she told everybody afterwards.

It did not say her mother's name. It said *Halde*, and it said it again at the top of the next one, and then twice more.

There was nobody in the Hold called Halde.

·  ·  ·

Here is what sixty-two hundredths does to a nineteen-year-old who swings first, set down in numbers because she set it down in numbers herself and there is no better way to keep it.

It went up. She had it all: she saw the arms come back and she saw the weight change feet and she had the whole of it in front of her. So she went.

She went at forty.

A roll is forty-two hundredths of being a thing that is being carried, and for eighteen of the forty-two nothing in the world can reach you. She went sideways and down at forty hundredths into a sixty-two hundredth swing. Her eighteen ran out at fifty-eight. The thing arrived at sixty-two.

Four hundredths. She was on the ground and coming off it and a hand could have picked her up, and what picked her up was thirty of reach with the whole of that plate behind it.

Eighteen. It put her four paces across the cart track and took the wind out of her and broke something across the back that Wren Ashfall found on the fourteenth day and did not have time to do anything about.

That is the other face of the long tell and it is the one that kills people. A quarter of a count of warning kills you because you have no time in it. Sixty-two hundredths kills you because you have too much, and you spend it, and you cannot get it back.

She got up.

·  ·  ·

The second time it went up she counted.

She stood inside thirty with her hands down and she counted a plumb line she could not see, all the way out and all the way back, and she did not move at forty and she did not move at fifty and she went at fifty-eight, and it arrived at sixty-two into her eighteen, and nothing reached her.

Then she was behind it, and behind it there was a door.

Because it is slow going up it is slow coming back. Everything the packing buys it, the packing charges it. It stood on the cart track with the long thing out at the end of its arms and the whole weight of the swing gone past it, and for as long as that took, which was longer than she had, she had it.

Light. Ten up, ten in the meat, twenty to get her hands back. Fourteen of poise into fifty-five.

Light again. Twenty-eight of the fifty-five gone.

Then the hard one, and this is the part that has been told in the Vale more than any other part of the thirteenth day. Twenty-eight hundredths to bring it up, in front of a thing with thirty of reach, at a range where it did not need to take a step to have her. Fourteen in the meat. Thirty-eight to get her hands back.

She agreed to it. She kept it. Thirty-four of poise, and thirty-four onto twenty-eight is sixty-two, and fifty-five is what was on it.

The packing opened.

·  ·  ·

There is no good way to write what that is like and Sila Carver did not try. Her hand for it is one line: *she says the plate came loose on it and it was only a man in there for a moment and she went in.*

Break the packing and what is left is a person, for a little while, and a person is a thing you can argue with. She put three light swings into it inside that while, sixteen of power in each, and the third one turned it, and then the plate took hold of itself again and shut, and she was back on a cart track in front of thirty of reach with two of the hard ones gone out of her and one of them spent on opening a door that had closed again.

She had eight lights and four heavies in her at the top of the track. She had spent one heavy on the break, one heavy going nowhere at the start, and better than half the lights.

She drank one of the three flasks at the foot of the fourth barrow with the thing coming at her at thirty.

It burned going down and it kept burning. That is the whole of what anybody in the Vale will tell you about it and it is accurate. Her right hand closed. The twist came back into the rope, not all of it, enough, and she went up the track and did the same three things again in the same order: light, light, and the hard one, and fifty-five opened for the second time, and she put three more into it.

Ninety. Nobody knew that number on the thirteenth day either.

It went down across the doorway of the fifth barrow and it did not get up, and it did not stop being upright in the way a person stops being upright. The plate stayed in the shape it had been in. It went down the way a stack of cut stone goes down when the bottom course goes.

She was colder for the rest of that day than the day was. She said so and nobody wrote down what they thought it meant.

·  ·  ·

What came out of it came out all at once.

The Hold has language for this now and had none then. When a thing that has been holding on is put down, what it has been holding comes out of it in one go, and it goes through whatever is standing there, and it does not ask. There was one person standing there.

She could not say what it was like. She was asked at the Hearth by four people in turn and each time she said less than the time before, and the fourth time she said it was like being handed something in the dark, and then she stopped answering.

In the middle of the plate, where a chest would have been, there was a thing left.

It is about the size of a closed hand and it is heavier than that. It is dense all the way through, the way lead is dense and unlike the way stone is dense. It is warm, and it stays warm, and it was warm on the fourteenth day and it was warm on the nineteenth. Holding it is slightly wrong and nobody who has held one has ever improved on that, including the four hands of this Chronicle.

It is not shaped like anything. It is not shaped like a knuckle or a stone or a seed or a tooth, though all four of those are in the record. It is shaped like nothing that anybody can put a name to, and the reason that is worth writing down is that everything else in the Vale is shaped like something.

She carried it down the track in her left hand because her right hand had the blade in it, and she carried it across Wrenfield, and she did not put it in her coat.

·  ·  ·

Ketil Brack took it off her at the gate and held it for about as long as it takes to say a short name and then held it for a good deal longer than that, and then he asked what he was supposed to do with it.

Nobody answered him.

He put it on the shelf over the quench tub in the Smithy, at the back, and he put a cloth under it and not over it.

It sat there for six days.

It was still there on the fourteenth day when Rin Ashfall was carried in through that gate. It was still there on the fifteenth when the Hold argued about who would take up the blade and on the sixteenth and the seventeenth and the eighteenth, and every one of those days Ketil Brack went past it and did not touch it and did not move it.

On the nineteenth day it came off the shelf.

·  ·  ·

There was the question of what to call the thing on the scarp, and it had to be settled that night, because a thing has to be called something before you can cut a line about it.

Sila Carver wrote *a Warden* and scratched it out.

She scratched it out because the Hold had had Wardens since the third day, and one of them was standing at the back of that room with his arms folded, and there was going to be a column on the board with the word in it, and she was not going to have the same word do both. She said that out loud and there is no record of anybody disagreeing with her and there is a record of people going quiet.

She wrote *gravewarden*, as one word, small, because it had come out of a grave and it had plainly been the warden of something.

She was wrong about the one word. Everybody afterwards has written it as one word anyway, and it is still the same word with a grave put in front of it, and the Hold has never found another, and the Hold has gone on calling its own people by the word it uses for what they go out to put down. That was noticed on the twenty-seventh day and it has been noticed on and off ever since, and it has never once been changed.

·  ·  ·

Then the four columns.

Sila cut the line and the second column went down by one, and Bran Holt, who had been standing at the board doing sums since the ninth day and had not been asked to, said the thing he said.

He said the second column had gone down by two the day before for two animals in the grass, and it had gone down by one that day for whatever had been on the scarp, and he wanted to know why. He said the columns had gone down for a thing that had come out of a barrow already dead, that had been standing in that barrow since before anybody in this Vale had been born, that had tried to kill the Hold's Warden twice and had got eighteen and something across the back into her, and that had said a name nobody knew four times while it did it.

He said he was not saying the board was wrong.

He said he was saying that the board thought something about that thing and it was not what he thought about it, and that he would like to know which of them was going to be right about it in a year.

Nobody answered him. It was late. The line stayed as it was cut and no line has ever been cut differently since.

She went out again in the morning.

·  ·  ·

Three things are written in the margins of this page in the second hand, none of them asked for.

*The blade is twenty-six. The thing at the quarry was thirty. There is nowhere to stand. I have cut both numbers into the board above the verbs, on the left, where a Warden will pass them on the way out.*

*I have asked Wren Ashfall three times what she steeps for the flasks and she has not answered me three times. Rin Ashfall drank one at the foot of the fourth barrow on the thirteenth day and it worked. I am writing that down because nobody has explained it and because in a year somebody will say it was always understood.*

*Halde. I have read every page there is. I have asked every person in this Hold and I have asked the Wayfarers twice since. There is nobody in this Vale called Halde and there never has been. I record what was said.*
