# Chapter 22: The Cull at Brackwood, Two

> *Four of seven, plus six, plus two. The best single entry this Hold ever wrote.*
> Recorded: Brackwood, the eleven-day push, with Asha Vane's annotation seventy days later

**Hand:** Bran Holt, then Asha Vane's later annotation · **Day:** 47 · **Place:** Brackwood, the Hold

Maren Verren walked into the quarantine on the morning of the forty-seventh and sat down in it.

I was at the fence. The Hearthkeepers were at the fence. The Cull were at the fence. The vote had been called for the morning and the morning had come and the morning was not a morning for a vote. The morning was a morning for a person to do a thing, and Maren did it, and the vote was not held, and the argument the Hold had been having for a fortnight was settled in the way arguments in this Hold are settled, which is by a person walking into the place the argument is about and sitting down in it.

The nine sick were in three huts. The huts were on the north side of Brackwood, away from the burners' camps, on ground the charcoal pits had been pulled off of because the ash in the seams of the wood was in the seams of the air and the sick had stopped being able to hold a cup for a full minute. One of them had stopped being able to hold a cup at all. His name is on a pane and I am not going to put it here because the pane is enough.

She sat down in the third hut, on the bed of the man who could not hold the cup, and she put her hand on the side of the bed and she did not move. She had the Widow's Maul across her knees. She did not have it across her knees to fight with. She had it across her knees because she always had it across her knees when she sat, and she had walked into the quarantine with it and the walking was not a violation of the Healers' law because the law is about who fights in the field and not about who sits in a hut, and the Hearthkeepers had argued this for an hour on the night of the forty-sixth and the Hearthkeepers had won it and the Healers had accepted it because the Healers were not at the vote either, the Healers were at the fence.

I was at the fence.

The Cull were at the fence.

The Cull had signed a list. The list had four names on it. Every name on the list was a name of a person who had signed it, because the Cull's defence, which is a good defence and which has held in three other valleys I have copies of, is that the person who signs the list is the person who has lost somebody on the list, and the Cull is a Hold's way of saying that the grief of losing a person is the only grief that ought to weigh in the decision. The four signers were Orrin Dunn, who had lost a sister, Tarn Stonewake, who had lost a brother, Yeva Holt, who had lost a father, and Nix Ember, who had lost a child. I am writing the names because the names are part of the record and because the Cull has been right three times and wrong twice in this book and the Cull deserves to be named when the Cull is right.

The Cull was wrong this morning. Maren sat down in the third hut and the Cull was wrong and the Cull knew it, and the Cull has never held that against her because the Cull is a doctrine and not a grudge.

·  ·  ·

The nine were kept.

The arithmetic on the board is the arithmetic of the kept. Six on Mercy, because a body was walked past. Two on Communion, because the body walked past was a sick person in a quarantine and not a Husk at a fence, and the Communion is the smaller of the two because the person was already inside the Hold and the gate was not opened to get her there. Three off Dominion, because the wielder spent the morning sitting down and did not hold any ground.

The arithmetic on the body is not on the board. Maren spent the day in the third hut, and she came out at dusk, and the Healers went in, and the Healers came out, and the nine were on the same beds they had been on, with the same grey in the seams of their hands, with the same wrong with their sleep, and four of them died anyway over the following month and were buried, and the Chronicle records that too, in the same voice, with the same day-number on the pane, with the same four numbers on the board.

Oda Rowan died on the fifty-third. Sila Dunn died on the fifty-ninth. Rin Kettle died on the sixty-first. Tove Mourne died on the seventy-first. Their graves are in the eastern range at the Memorial, in a row, and the row is the row I walk when I am cutting the fifth line on the board at the turn of the season and I want to remember what the four numbers cost.

Five survived. Colm Brack is the one who can no longer hold a cup for a full minute. He is alive in the Hold today and he has been alive in the Hold for every chapter after this one. He has never been on the board and he has been on every pane in the eastern range.

·  ·  ·

The Cull's argument was not answered by Maren's sitting. The Cull's argument was answered by what happened after, which was that Brackwood itself was pushed back from Greying, and Brackwood is the second half of this chapter and the half that is on the board in a way that the sitting is not.

Eleven days. The whole Hold, excepting the five Healers and the five surviving Brackwood sick and three children who could not lift a spade. The day was spent in Brackwood, with the Carvers setting relic-stone and the Foragers turning lye and bone into the burnt stands and the Wardens walking the perimeter with whatever weapon was on the wall. Maren was there. She was there every day. She was the wielder and the wielder was the work and the work was the pushing back, and the pushing back was eleven days of the most expensive piece of ground any settlement in any valley I have a copy of has ever pushed back.

Four on Memory. Seven on Mercy. Six on Communion. Two on Dominion.

The numbers are on the board. The numbers are the best single entry the Hold ever wrote, and the best single entry is the entry that moves all four axes in the same direction by the largest total that any single verb can move, and `region_saved` moves all four axes by nineteen, and nineteen is the largest movement of any single verb in any direction.

The relic from the Gravewarden on the twenty-seventh was spent on stone. I cut the entry. The stone is at the second stand from the south, set into a low cairn with the relic under it, and the relic is unnamed and the cairn is unmarked except for the cairn, and the cairn holds eleven paces of Quick ground around itself in ground that had been Greying since the season before the Hold came, and the eleven paces are Quick now and they are Quick because the relic is under the stone and the stone is holding the ground the way a Monument holds the ground.

Eleven days. Twenty-two people. Four, seven, six, two.

·  ·  ·

After the eleven days the board read what the board read.

Memory at fifty-five. Mercy at thirty-seven. Communion at thirty-nine. Dominion at eight.

Thirty-nine on Communion.

I am going to put on this page the number and the position, because the number and the position are the most important fact about the forty-seventh day of this Hold, and the fact is that Communion was one short of the gate for six days, and during those six days nobody in the Hold knew.

The gate that Communion opens is The Wake. The Wake requires Communion at or above forty. The Wake also requires Memory at or above forty-five and every Echo reclaimed and the lineage not empty. Memory was at fifty-five, which was past forty-five. The lineage was not empty, which it never was. The Echoes were at zero, which they had been since the nineteenth. The Wake was one point of Communion away.

For six days, the Wake was one point of Communion away, and nobody in the Hold looked at the board for it. Nobody in the Hold looked at the board because nobody in the Hold had reason to look at the board, because the Wake was a thing that happened at the end of a Hold's life and the Hold was forty-seven days old and was not at the end of its life.

I am writing this because the Wake is what happened at the end of the Hold's life, and the end of the Hold's life was the two hundred and tenth day, which is a hundred and sixty-three days after this morning, and the Wake was one point of Communion away for six days of those hundred and sixty-three, and if any person on any of those six days had looked at the board and counted the Echoes and seen that the Echoes were at zero and seen that the lineage was not empty and seen that Memory was past forty-five and seen that Communion was at thirty-nine, that person would have known that the next time somebody walked past somebody, the Wake would lock.

Nobody looked. The board does not announce. The board is a piece of wood with numbers on it and the numbers do what the numbers do and a number that is one short of a gate is a number that is one short of a gate and a number that is at the gate is a number that is at the gate and the difference is one point of Communion and one point of Communion is what you get from a single Telling or a single Echo reclaimed or a single `ally_revived` or one of three other verbs, and the person who walked past the body on the fifty-third day did not know she was locking the gate when she locked it, and the gate stayed locked for six days and opened again on the fifty-fifth when somebody died and the dead was lying out.

I have spent twenty years thinking about this and I am not going to spend the chapter on it. The Hold was one point of Communion away from its own ending for six days and the Hold did not know, and the knowing was not on the board and the board did not warn and the warning was not what the board was for.

·  ·  ·

This page has been annotated, seventy days later, in the third hand, by Asha Vane, who was at the fence on the morning of the forty-seventh and who was not at the fence because she wanted to be at the fence but because the Hold had put her there as a Warden the day before and the Warden had been told to stand at the fence and the Warden stood at the fence and the Warden was twelve days from being conscripted into the kit and forty-eight days from being conscripted into the raid and the Warden did not know either of those facts.

She wrote in the margin, in her own hand, in handwriting that is faster than mine and shorter than mine and angrier than mine:

*Maren sat. The Cull signed. Nine were kept. Four died. Five lived. Brackwood was pushed back. The board moved nineteen on four axes and the Hold did not know it was one point away from the Wake. The Hold has never known what the Hold is one point away from. That is what the board is for.*

She wrote this seventy days later, on the one hundred and seventeenth day, when she was inside the kit and had not been outside the kit for six days and had written nothing for six days because a Teller writes down what happened and for six days she was one of the things that was happening. She wrote it in the margin of this page and she wrote it in pencil because she had run out of ink and the pencil was a piece of lead from a Wayfarer's bark and the lead was grey and her hand was grey and the writing is hard to read.

I have copied it in ink because the page was already ink. I have left her pencil under the ink because the page is the page and the pencil is hers and the ink is mine.

## Other Roads

### The cull performed

The Cull's road. The list is read out, the names are called, the sick are put down at the fence with the rest of them, and the day after the Cull is shorter by nine sick and the day after that is shorter by one Warden who could not do the day after and went out the gate and did not come back.

I have a copy from Verren Hold of this. The copy is on three boards and the boards are stained and the stain is not water and the stain is not ash and I am not going to write down what the stain is. The Cull's road is the fastest road in the book to `region_lost`, because when the Cull is performed the people who signed the list stop being healers and start being what the Cull makes of them, and what the Cull makes of them is a person who has done the day's work and is going to do it again tomorrow, and tomorrow's work is the same work, and the work eats the ground.

### Bringing them inside the palisade untreated

The Healer road. The sick are carried inside, put in the byre, the rest of the Hold works around them, and the Hold's labour pool drops by nine overnight and the output drops by more than nine because the sick are not the only ones who cannot work, the well ones who are watching the sick also cannot work, and the watching is a kind of work and the kind of work is grief.

The grief pool goes up by what the nine brought with them, and the nine had six bonds each, and six times three times nine is a hundred and sixty-two of grief spent on nobody because the nine are alive and the alive do not move the board, and the grief sits in the pool and the pool suppresses the output and the output is the floor.

### The Unnaming's variant

A variant I have not seen done and a variant the Hold has argued about. The sick are unnamed while still alive. The pane for the sick is not cut. The pane for the sick is left blank.

The argument for it is that the pane is what is keeping the hold closed, and the hold is what is making the sickness, and the sickness is the grey in the seams of the hands, and the grey is the hold drawn into the body, and the uncut pane does not call the name and the name not called does not close the grip and the grip not closed does not hold the drawn-ash and the drawn-ash does not stay in the seams of the hands.

The argument against it is that the pane is not the only thing that calls a name, the room does, the Hearth does, the Teller does, the wielder does when she touches the doorstone on her way out. The argument against it has been held for two hundred days in the Hold of Ashfall by a person I am not going to name in this chapter because the chapter has had enough of her.

Nothing entered · the pane stays blank · the names stay spoken by the room

**Pressure:** nobody knows. This is the road the board cannot read. It is the road I have spent a whole hand arguing about and a whole hand refusing to settle.
