# Chapter 45: Ash Inheritance

> *The lineage forgot itself. Your own Echo walks the fields now, and the villagers call it
> by your name because it is the only name they have left.*
> Recorded: the fifth line of the board at Verren Hold, two valleys east

**Hand:** four hands of Verren Hold becoming one, copied whole by Grieve Mourne
**Days:** Verren's own count, day 1 to day 240
**Place:** Verren Hold

I have read this copy nine times.

It came to us in three parts, carried by three different Wayfarers across a long stretch of
calendar, and the third part arrived after the Wake, which means I read the ending of
Verren Hold with our own board already locked and nothing left to do about anything.

It is the most complete document in this book that is not ours. Two hundred and forty days
of their count, four hands, and not one leaf missing. I want that noted. Vane Hold gave us
eleven legible leaves out of a hold that stopped. Verren Hold gave us everything, in order,
kept to the end, by a Teller who never missed a morning.

That is the thing about this chapter and I am going to say it here at the top and then let
the document do it: **Verren Hold did not stop keeping the board.** It was kept perfectly.
It is the best-kept Chronicle I have ever handled.

·  ·  ·

## The first hand. Tove Verren. Their days 1 to 61.

A hold like any hold. Wall, quarry, four Carvers, a Memorial on a south rise with a range of
panes going up at the ordinary rate, which is one pane behind every pyre and three panes
behind every burial.

Their first thirty days are our first thirty days with different weather. Two wielders. One
`region_saved`. One Monument. A Telling on their twenty-second day that the hand describes
in a full page, warmly, and which raised their Memory to thirty-one.

Then their thirty-fourth day.

```
D34. Argument at the Hearth, all evening, whole roll present.
     Motion: that the panes be shaved, oldest first.
     Reasoning as given: the dead did not walk before we wrote
     them down. An Echo grows because it is being called. The
     oldest names have been calling longest. Shave the oldest
     first and the oldest grips open first, and we will know
     within a season whether it is true.
     Motion carried, 71 to 44.
     There is no verb for this. Entered in words.
```

Seventy-one to forty-four. That is not a cult and it is not a coup. It is a hold of a
hundred and fifteen working people voting, at the end of a long evening, on a testable
proposition, and the losing side was forty-four strong and stayed.

Our Hold had the same argument on our fifty-eighth and fifty-ninth days and our
fifty-ninth ended with eleven panes recut and a Telling held for all eleven at once, and
the woman who had shaved them holding the graver. Seventy-one to forty-four the other way,
and I have spent a great deal of my life being glad of that and no part of it being certain
we were right.

Sila Grey has read this copy too. She asked me for it and I gave it to her and she had it
for a season and gave it back and said one thing about it, which was: *they did it in the
wrong order.*

I asked her what the right order was.

She said: the book. You copy the book and you scrape the boards. They scraped the panes,
which is the part a person walks past, and they kept every name on the board at eye height
in the Hearth where it was read aloud at the turn of every season. She said that is not
unnaming. That is taking the glass off the wall and leaving the calling where it was.

I have no way to test that and neither has she and she is sixty-one.

·  ·  ·

## The good sixty days. Their days 34 to 94.

It worked.

I am going to give the entries because the entries are the argument and because no summary
I could write would be honest about them.

```
D37. Pane shaved: the founder's mother. Mem -6. No rite. No grief entered.
D41. Pane shaved: two of the first wall party. Mem -12.
D44. Ring at the north rows, first growth, four days standing.
     Nobody sent. Watched from the fence.
D46. Ring at the north rows: opened. Nobody went out to it.
     Grass standing. Root colour returning.
     There is no verb for a grip that opened by itself.
     Entered in words. Whole roll came out to look.
D48. Pane shaved: four. Mem -24.
D52. Output at full. First time since D9.
D55. Pane shaved: six. Mem -36.
D58. Ring at the east ditch: opened. Nobody went out to it.
D61. Grief pool at nothing, and no Wake held for it.
     I do not know how to enter this and I have entered it in words.
```

Their first hand ends there, eight days later, of something ordinary.

Read it again. On their forty-sixth day a ring in the north rows opened by itself with
nobody within two hundred paces of it, and a hold of a hundred and fifteen people walked
out of their gate to stand and look at grass.

Nothing in our Chronicle has ever done that. Not once in two hundred and ten days. Every
grip that ever opened in the Vale of Ashfall was opened by a body walking out with a weapon
and paying for it.

Their grief pool went to nothing on their sixty-first day without a Wake, because grief is
hold that has been released and not yet given, and they had stopped taking any in. No
names entered, no bonds counted, no ten and no three apiece. Their fields ran at full for
two seasons. Their second hand's opening leaves are the most cheerful pages in any
Chronicle I have copied.

The Wayfarers went there. That is in the entries too, on both sides: for a stretch of
calendar Verren Hold was the hold you walked to, because it had bread, and because it had
worked something out.

## The second hand. Ketil Rowan. Their days 61 to 138.

This is the hand that finds the bill.

It takes him seventy-seven days of their count and I do not think any of us would have
found it faster, and I am certain Bran Holt would not, and I am not sure I would have found
it at all until it had already happened to me.

```
D96. Tove Rowan of the Holt road asks at the post how many
     Legacies we hold. I could not answer her. Went to the board.
     Counted. Six, all from before D34.
     Every one of the six came out of a rite.
     Every rite came off a name.
     We have cut no rite since D31.
D97. Went back through the leaves. There is no Legacy in this
     Chronicle that did not come off a name.
D99. Put it to the Hearth. Told that a hold with full output and
     no dead walking does not need Legacies.
     I said that a hold with no Legacies has nothing to hand a
     successor but a blade and a floor.
     Told that we have not needed a successor since D54.
     That is true and I have entered it.
```

Six Legacies, all from before their thirty-fourth day, and none since, and they were right
that they had not needed one, and they were right for another forty days.

The arithmetic is very simple and it is the whole of this chapter and I will set it out
once in my own voice and then get out of the way.

A Legacy comes only from a rite. A rite costs grief. Grief comes only from a death that
has been entered with a name and a count of bonds. So a hold that stops entering names
stops accruing grief, which is why their fields ran full, and stops being able to hold
rites, which is why they gained nothing, and every successor they ever took after their
thirty-fourth day started exactly where their first wielder started: a hundred of health, a
hundred in the purse, three flasks, and a blade.

Our tenth wielder went out against a Gravewarden at the ninth ring with a graver's forearms
and no training, and came back, because he was carrying nine people's deaths in his arms.
Their eleventh went out with a hundred and three flasks and did not.

## Their days 139 to 190. The third hand, Bel Mourne.

Wielders, in order, with their reigns in their own days.

```
D139  Yeva Verren        11 days
D150  Ketil Brack        7 days
D157  Sila Verren        14 days
D171  Bran Rowan         5 days
D176  Asha Verren        9 days
D185  Tove Brack         3 days
D188  Colm Ember         2 days
D190  Nix Verren         13 days
```

Eight wielders in fifty-one days. We had ten in two hundred and ten.

They were not worse people. That is the thing I cannot get anybody in our Hold to hear
when I read this aloud. They were a bigger hold with better ground and four Carvers and a
finished wall, and they went through eight wielders in fifty-one days because each of the
eight went out with nothing on them but themselves.

And every one of the eight was left where they fell, because a rite needs a name, and a
name was the one thing that hold had voted not to keep.

```
D163. Sila Verren fell at the west ditch. Mem +2 Dom -3.
D165. Left the Echo of Sila Verren to grow. Mem -5 Mrc -3 Com -2 Dom -1.
D171. The Echo of Sila Verren has left the ditch.
D178. The Echo of Sila Verren is not in the region. Foragers have
      walked the line and it has gone east.
      There is no verb for an Echo that has left the valley
      and was not sent.
```

She came to us.

Our seventieth day. An Echo walked into the Vale of Ashfall carrying a name nobody here had
ever met, and our Hold argued about it for two days with nine panes of backlog and the
Carvers against it, and Maren Verren buried it, and cut a stranger's name into Ashfall
glass fluxed with pearl out of a stranger's own ash, and put it in the west range where it
is now, between Sila Carver and Tarn Stonewake.

Plus six, plus seven, plus nine, minus one. It is the single best entry in our whole
Chronicle by the Communion figure, and it was for a woman none of us could have described.

And a Wayfarer carried the line east, and it reached Verren Hold, and their third hand
entered it, and here it is.

```
D186. Report from the Ashfall road: the Echo of Sila Verren was
      buried at Ashfall Hold on their D70. Their Teller cut her a
      pane. Mem +6 Mrc +7 Com +9 Dom -1.
      Entered. I do not know what to do with it.
      There is a pane of her in a valley that never met her and
      none of her here, and I shaved the blank that was waiting
      for her myself on D55 on a motion carried 71 to 44.
```

*I do not know what to do with it.*

That entry moved their board six in the direction they had spent a hundred and fifty days
moving it away from, and it moved it because somebody in another valley did something
nobody in Verren Hold voted for.

What crosses between holds is entries. Not ground, not goods, not maps. Lines. A Teller in
one valley writes a sentence and a board two valleys east moves for it, and there is no
gate in any hold anywhere that has ever stopped that.

## Their days 192 to 240.

Here is where I stop being able to describe this document and start having to show it.

There are four hands in this Chronicle. Tove Verren to their sixty-first day. Ketil Rowan
to their hundred and thirty-eighth. Bel Mourne to their two hundred and third. Asha Ember
after that.

I have handled the original leaves. I am a Carver by trade and I have cut wax and glass
since I was twelve and I know what a hand is: the pressure at the top of a stroke, the
depth in the bowl of a letter, where a person stops to breathe.

Bel Mourne's hand is heavy at the top and shallow in the bowls and he breaks a line after
about nine words to rest his wrist.

Asha Ember's hand is light, even, and she does not break lines at all.

From about their two hundred and tenth day, the two are the same hand.

Not similar. The same. Same pressure, same breath, same break at nine words, and it is not
Bel Mourne's hand and it is not Asha Ember's hand, and it is not Ketil Rowan's or Tove
Verren's either. I have laid all four against it under a lamp and it is a fifth hand that
has arrived at something all four were reaching for.

It is better than all four of them. I have to put that down because it is the thing I
noticed first and it is the thing I have never been able to stop noticing. The letters are
better. The lines are level. The arithmetic is correct on every leaf and I have checked
every leaf.

And from their two hundred and third day the entries are signed.

```
D203. Nix Verren fell. Mem +2 Dom -3.
D205. Board kept. Output at four parts in five. Two rings standing
      in the north rows, neither entered, both watched.
      Kept by N. Verren.
D209. Board kept. Kept by N. Verren.
D214. Board kept. One ring opened by itself at the west ditch.
      Whole roll came out to look. Kept by N. Verren.
D226. Board kept. Kept by N. Verren.
D240. Board kept. Kept by N. Verren.
```

Nix Verren fell on their two hundred and third day. It is the entry directly above the
first of the signatures and it is in the same hand as the signatures.

I am not going to tell you what that means. I have nine readings and I can support four of
them and I am going to give two.

The first is that a hold which has stopped saying names ends up with one name left in it,
and uses it for everything, and that the last Teller of Verren Hold signed a dead wielder's
name to her own entries because it was the only name still cut anywhere in that Hearth and
because after a while that stops being strange.

The second is what the fifth line of their board says, and I have put it at the head of
this chapter, and I am not going to put it twice.

·  ·  ·

There is no last entry.

Their two hundred and fortieth day is not the end of the leaf. There are four inches of
clean vellum under it and no damp on the leaf and no fire mark and no tear. The book is
whole. The hand simply does not come back.

Their board is still on the wall in the stone Hearth at Verren Hold, and a Wayfarer read it
to me from her own copy of a copy, and the four numbers are there and the fifth line is
there and it says what it says.

And in the fields, by every account anybody has carried out of that valley, there is
something at the second or third growth walking the north rows, and the people who are
still in that hold call it by a name.

They call it correctly. That is the detail every Wayfarer brings back and it is always the
same detail. They are not frightened of it and they do not go out to it and they have not
gone out to anything in a long time, and when it is in the north rows they say the name it
is owed, and it is the name on the bottom of the last thirty-seven entries in the best-kept
Chronicle in any valley I know of.

·  ·  ·

Half a page of mine.

I have read this copy nine times and the ninth time was after our Wake, with our board
locked at a hundred and a hundred and a hundred and one, and I read it as a man who had
won the argument.

I did not feel as though I had won the argument.

Sila Grey shaved eleven panes in our Memorial on a night in our fifty-fifth day and was
found out by a boy of nine who looked at glass from the wrong angle, and our Hold recut
them and held a Telling for all eleven at once and put the graver in her hand, and that is
why there are Legacies in our arms and that is why a Teller with no training walked home
from the ninth ring.

And on their forty-sixth day a ring in the north rows of Verren Hold opened by itself, with
nobody near it, and a hundred and fifteen people came out to look at grass, and no
Chronicle in this valley has ever recorded anything like it, and nobody in this Hold has
ever offered me an explanation for it that does not require the Unnaming to be right about
the mechanism and wrong only about the price.

The handwriting in the last forty pages is better than the handwriting before it.

I have said in four hundred pages that I record what was said and do not settle it, and I
have held to that with two culls, two wakes, a barred gate, a drowned village and nine of
my own dead.

I am holding to it here. I am aware of what it costs me to hold to it here.
