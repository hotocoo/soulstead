# Chapter 26: The Long Grass Road

> *Lifted Tove Rowan off the ground. Plus two, plus eight, plus nine, minus two. The gate
> was opened on the sixty-third day and this is what it bought.*
> Recorded: the board, in Bran Holt's hand, the sixty-sixth day

**Hand:** Bran Holt, second hand
**Days:** 63 to 66
**Place:** the crossing post; Wrenfield, past the last fence; the palisade

Four Wayfarers came to the crossing post on the sixty-third day and asked for passage
through Wrenfield.

Bran Holt names two of them. Tove Rowan and Sable Dunn. The other two are in this book as
*two others*, and they carried packs and cut wood and one of them could read, and that is
the whole of what four hundred pages have to say about two people who walked into this
valley and out of it again, and there is nothing I can do about that now. I have been
angry about it for years. The second hand recorded what he was asked to record and nobody
asked him to record a name.

It was a Turning season. The grass past the last fence was standing chest high and going
pale at the root, and the birds in it were thinner than they had been, and everybody in the
Hold could see both of those things from the palisade without going anywhere.

·  ·  ·

What the Wayfarers wanted was the short line.

The ridge west of us is crossed in one place. To reach it from the crossing post you go
through Wrenfield, past the last fence, along the old fence line and up through the long
grass to the cut bank, and that is a morning. The way round is the mere road and then the
edge of the Grey Ash, and that is four days, and two of them are in ash to the ankle in
country where nothing grows and the routes are closed.

They had a route-mark to cut and copies to leave. That is what they carry. Not goods and
not maps. They carry lines out of other valleys' boards, and they will sit down at your
Hearth and read you a stranger's afternoon, and if the stranger did something your Hold has
never thought of doing, your Hold now knows it.

They also carry soap.

I want that in early, because every hand that has written about the Open Road has written
about it as a matter of doctrine and it is at least half a matter of freight. Ash steeped
in water and drawn off clear gives lye and lye gives soap, and soap is the only thing
Ashfall Hold has ever sold to anybody. It goes out on Wayfarers' backs. There is no other
way for it to go. A Hold that shuts its post has a shed full of soap and a valley with no
market in it.

·  ·  ·

The Wardens were against it and their case was better than the ruling.

Bran Holt has it from Orrin Dunn, who was a Warden then and was twenty-four and had the
eastern furlong off his father, and it runs to about a page.

Turning is the state where things start coming out of the grass. Quick ground is loud with
insects and you can hear it being empty. Turning ground is quiet in patches. Everybody who
works Wrenfield knows where the patches are and nobody can tell you how they know.

A Stalker is not a person and was never a person. It is a Vale animal that the drawn-ash
got into and that learned that hold is food. It stands about knee high and it is thin and
it carries almost nothing on it, so there is no packing to break and one clean swing ends
it, and Orrin Dunn's whole objection is in the next sentence. They do not come alone. And
the warning it gives you before it commits is a little over a quarter of a second, which
is not long enough to decide anything, which means you have to have decided already, which
means you have to know the ground.

Four strangers do not know the ground.

Then he made the argument that matters, and it is the reason this chapter and the next one
are next to each other in the book.

He said: if one of them goes down out there, we have a stranger standing in our field in
four days with a name we cannot cut and a build we have never seen, and there is no rite in
this Vale that anybody has ever held over somebody else's dead, and no law that says we owe
one, and it will be in our grass either way.

Bran Holt wrote that down on the sixty-third day.

On the sixty-eighth day something walked into Wrenfield carrying a name out of a valley two
removes east and the Hold argued about it for two days. Orrin Dunn was not on that
argument. He was on the mere road. I have never been able to establish whether anybody
reminded him what he had said five days earlier, and I have asked.

·  ·  ·

Maren Verren opened the gate on the sixty-fourth day.

She gave two reasons and Bran Holt entered both and did not rank them, and I am going to
put them in his order and then say which one I prefer, because the Chronicle does not rank
and I am allowed to and he was not.

The first reason was the soap.

The second reason was that there is no verb for it.

The board has eighteen verbs and it has had eighteen since the ninth day and not one of
them is for letting somebody through. There is a verb for pulling a villager off their work
and putting a spear in their hands. There is a verb for walking past something without
drawing and there is a verb for putting it down. There is no verb for a bowl of food and a
place by the fire and a bed of straw in a byre for four people who are going somewhere
else.

Maren Verren's line, which is nineteen words and is the only thing she ever said that got
copied into other valleys' books: *there is nothing on that wall for this, so it costs us
nothing on that wall, so open it.*

She was wrong about the arithmetic and right about the gate. What happened on the sixty-
sixth day put twenty-one on the board across four numbers, which is the largest single
entry in this Chronicle, larger than any Monument and larger than pushing a region back,
and none of it would have been available if she had kept the post shut. The absence of a
verb is not the absence of a consequence. It only means the consequence arrives under
somebody else's heading.

I prefer the soap.

·  ·  ·

They went through on the sixty-fifth and they came back on the sixty-sixth, which is the
part nobody plans for. The ridge crossing was shut with water. So they came back down
through the long grass a day after they had gone up it, and the difference between those
two mornings is that the first time they went behind Maren Verren in single file and the
second time they were coming home and were spread out and were talking.

Maren Verren had the Maul and two Foragers and Orrin Dunn with a spear. She had three
flasks. Three was the Hold's ration and the Healers had settled on three by then and there
was a fourth in the Hold off the boy's Pyre and it was not hers.

They came out of a quiet patch about two hundred paces inside the last fence, and there
were three of them, and Bran Holt's entry for the fight is four lines and I have not added
to it.

She put the first one down with a light swing before it closed, which is a twentieth of a
second of decision and a fifth of a second of edge, and she had been carrying that hammer
for fifty days. The second went past her at the Foragers and Orrin Dunn met it with the
spear and it went off him and came back and he got it the second time and took its teeth
through the outside of his calf doing it.

The third one had Tove Rowan on the ground before anybody had turned round.

·  ·  ·

Twelve is what a Stalker does, and twelve on a body that is not wearing anything packed and
has been walking since first light is not a wound anybody would write home about.

What put her on the ground was not the twelve.

I am going to be exact about this because it is the single piece of knowledge in this
Chronicle that has done the most good in the world, and because two valleys used it on the
hundred and eightieth day and one of them is not this one.

A body that has spent everything it has and is then opened does not finish at once. The
grip goes slack in stages. There is an interval. The Healers of this Hold put it at about
as long as it takes to bring a small pot up, and they will not be held to that figure and
have hanged nobody who disagreed with it. Inside the interval the hold is still hers, and
still closed, and coming open, and if it finishes coming open then she is dead and in four
days she is standing in Wrenfield doing whatever she was doing last.

Inside the interval, another body can hold it shut from outside.

Not from nearby. On her. Hands on, skin, and the Healers say over the ribs because the ribs
are where you can feel it going and because you need to feel it going in order to know how
much to spend. And what you hold it shut with is your own. There is no other kind. You
cannot lend somebody else's, you cannot lend the Hold's, and you cannot do it at all if you
have just been swinging, because a body that has been swinging has nothing in it to give
and both hands full of nothing.

And it does not come back to you. It goes into her and it stays in her. She gets up with a
purse that is partly somebody else's and she keeps it.

Maren Verren had been swinging.

So she went down on her knees in pale grass with her hands under a stranger's ribs and she
drank her second flask kneeling, which is not what a flask is for. Pyre-ash steeped and
settled three days and drawn off clear and cut with hearth-water until it will not strip
your throat. It burns going down and it keeps burning and what it does is hand you back,
for a short while, the hold you have already spent.

She was handed it back and she gave it away.

That is the whole of the mechanism and it took the Vale sixty-six days to find out and
about a hundred and fourteen more to work out what it was worth.

·  ·  ·

The Healers' account, given at the Hearth on the sixty-sixth day and copied whole by Bran
Holt, has three rules in it and all three have held for a hundred and forty-four days
since.

You cannot do it from a distance. You cannot do it for somebody who has finished. And the
person who can do it is almost never the person who wants to, because the person who wants
to is the one who has just been fighting.

The Hearthkeepers had said the third part of this out loud since before there was a board.
Their line is older than the Chronicle and it comes in six words and it is the reason two
of them have gone over the palisade in a temper about the Flask ration and come back.

*We will lift a stranger off the ground.*

Nobody in the Hold had ever seen it done. Nobody in the Hold had ever needed to, because in
sixty-five days nobody had gone down in reach of anybody who had anything left.

·  ·  ·

The board took two off Dominion.

Bran Holt could not account for it and said so in the margin, which he did twice in
seventy-six days. Two off, for the best thing anybody in this Hold had done since the
nineteenth day.

I can account for it now and it took me most of a life. Dominion is the question of whether
the Vale is a thing you hold or a thing you are in. A woman who kneels in her own field and
gives away part of what she is holding, to somebody who is leaving in the morning, has
answered that question, and she has answered it in the direction of being in it, and the
board is not insulting her. It is agreeing with her.

Two off Dominion, eight on Mercy, nine on Communion. Nine is the largest single movement on
that axis anything in the eighteen verbs can produce. There is nothing in this Hold's
history to that point that comes near it, and I have checked, and the Brackwood entry on the
forty-seventh day comes closest and it took eleven days and twenty-two people.

This one took about as long as it takes to bring a small pot up.

·  ·  ·

Tove Rowan copied the board on the evening of the sixty-sixth day.

She could not sit up. She was on her side on a bench in the Hearth with the north wall in
front of her, and she had loose leaves and a stick of lead, and she copied the four numbers
and the drift line and every entry since the last copy any Wayfarer had taken out of this
valley.

Holt Vale, two ridges east, keeps its Chronicle as loose leaves in a chest and has done so
since Tove Rowan. It is a system and not a book. I have said that to her face and she has
said worse to mine.

She asked Bran Holt what the fifth line was for.

The board is four words with a signed number against each of them and then one line
underneath that says what the whole thing is drifting toward. Nobody put it there. Nobody
has ever accounted for it. Sila Carver did not, Bran Holt did not, Asha Vane did not, and I
have planed a board and ruled that line myself and I could have left it off.

Bran Holt told her he did not know. Then he read her the line, because she could not turn
her head far enough to read it herself, and the line on the sixty-sixth day said **The
Wake**, and she wrote it down.

His entry names her, because the verb needs a subject and the board will not take an entry
without one. It says nothing else about her. Not her age, not where she was from, not what
she did for the four days she lay in our byre, not one word about the woman herself. He
gave her a subject and not a line.

She had Ashfall's numbers in a bundle under her arm when she walked out of the gate, and
that is how a valley two ridges east came to know what this valley's board said, and it is
the reason that a hundred and fourteen days later two boards could move on the same
afternoon over the same field.

The same thing happened again on the hundred and eightieth day, in Wrenfield, between two
different people, and it went the other way round: a Warden of this Hold with a shoulder
that had been wrong for seventy-one days knelt down in the grass and lifted a wielder of
Holt Vale off the ground, and she was the same woman, and it is the same entry, plus two,
plus eight, plus nine, minus two, and both boards took it. I have written that chapter. She
has written it too, and her account differs from mine in three particulars, and both stand.

What crosses between holds is entries. It is worth saying plainly the first time it happens
rather than the second. Nothing crossed the ridge on the sixty-sixth day except a bundle of
leaves under a stranger's arm. No ground moved, no grief moved, no Legacy moved. A line
moved, and a line is enough, and everything this valley eventually became is downstream of
a woman who could not sit up copying a number off a wall.

`ally_abandoned`. Minus two, minus eight, minus ten, plus three. Ten off Communion is the
largest single subtraction on any axis in the eighteen verbs, and the board is not being
squeamish. It is being accurate.

almost these words, and what their board records afterwards is nothing at all for a
season, which is the point.

shed. No copies arrived, so no argument arrived, so for eleven years their Hearth heard

their own order, and they are correct entries, and there is not one name in them from

### Robbing them

Four packs, four sets of boots, a lead stick, copies out of eleven valleys, and about
nine days of food. It is not a large prize. It has been taken.

The Teller of that hold had eighteen verbs and no honest one, and he entered `enemy_slain`
four times on the same day with four subjects, and every hand that has copied that page
since has copied the four names, which means the four people are better recorded in the
book of the hold that killed them than they were anywhere else in the world.

The route-marks came off the posts for three valleys around. Not defaced. Cut out, so the
post reads as a post. A hold that is not on anybody's road has a board nobody copies, and a
