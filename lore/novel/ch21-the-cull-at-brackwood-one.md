# Chapter 21: The Cull at Brackwood, One

> *Oda Rowan. Sila Dunn. Rin Kettle. Tove Mourne. Ketil Stonewake. Nix Holt. Yeva Ember.
> Sable Vane. Colm Brack.*
> Recorded: the Cull's roster, in Orrin Dunn's hand, read out at the Hearth on the
> forty-sixth day

**Hand:** Bran Holt, second hand
**Days:** 41 to 46
**Place:** Brackwood, the burners' clearing; the Hearth

Nine people came back out of Brackwood on the forty-first day and stood in the yard and
were grey in the seams of their hands, and that is the whole event, and it took the Hold
six days to understand that it was an event at all.

This chapter has no entry in it. Nothing was done between the forty-first day and the
forty-sixth. The board is blank across the whole of it, which is the only blank stretch of
that length in the second hand, and Bran Holt was not a man who left things blank.

·  ·  ·

A charcoal burn is six days of standing over a smothered stack.

You cut cordwood and stack it in a dome and turf it over and open a flue at the top, and
then you do not leave it. Somebody is on it at every hour, because the burn has to smoulder
and must not catch, and when it starts to catch you shovel earth on the place where it is
catching, and the place where it is catching is under the turf and the only way to find it
is by what comes off the top. So you stand over it and you breathe what comes off the top,
for six days, in shifts, and the shifts overlap because nobody wants to be the one who
handed over wrong.

The Hold needed the charcoal and there is no argument about that in any hand. Iron does not
come out of a wood fire. Glass comes out of nothing at all without a hot end, and the hot
end runs on charcoal, and the Memorial is a wall of glass, and the lime-kilns at Stonewake
want it too, and lime goes into the furrows. The Smithy had been running short since the
thirty-first day because the man who used to keep its stock was dead and the man who taught
him had spent two days on a bench.

Brackwood was at Greying. Everybody knew it was at Greying. Greying means ash in the seams
of everything and the colour going out of it, and it is the second of the four states, and
it had been at Greying since the twenty-sixth day and nobody had done anything about it,
because there had been a boy at the fire and then there had been the mere.

Nine people went into a Greying wood on the thirty-fifth day and stood over a stack for six
days and came out on the forty-first.

·  ·  ·

Here is what was wrong with them, as Bran Holt set it down, and he set it down well, which
he did about once a fortnight and never on purpose.

The grey was in the seams of their hands. Not on the skin. In the seams, in the knuckle
creases and under the nails and in the lines of the palm, and it was the colour of the
floor of that wood. They washed. Bran wrote down that they washed, which is the kind of
detail later Tellers have called him dull for keeping, and it is the detail that settles
the question, because lye takes charcoal off a plank and it took nothing out of a knuckle.
They washed with the Hold's own soap, which is made out of the Hold's own dead, and it did
nothing at all.

There was something wrong with the sleep. They slept. They woke and had not been given
anything back by it. Oda Rowan, who was the eldest of them and had been burning charcoal
in three valleys since before the Hearth, put it in four words at the Hearth on the
forty-third day. She said sleeping had stopped counting.

None of them was short of breath sitting down. The Healers said that on the first day and
kept saying it and it kept being true, and it is the reason the Hold spent five days
looking for the wrong thing.

What was actually wrong was the other one.

Sila Dunn carried a basket of shell twenty paces up the bank from the mere road on the
forty-second day and put it down and sat on the bank. Nix Holt could draw a bow. He could
not draw it twice. Yeva Ember got up from a table, walked to the door, and stood in the
doorway holding the frame for a while before she went through it, and she was thirty-four
and had been on the rota every day of her life.

The three Wardens watched that for two days and then said the word out loud, because it is
their word, and they say it flatly the way a carpenter says plumb.

Composure. What you spend before your grip opens. It comes back when you stop, and it
comes back slowly, and it does not begin coming back at the moment you stop.

What was wrong with the nine was that it was not coming back. Not all of it. They emptied
like anybody empties and they filled a little overnight and they did not fill up, and
whatever did not come back on the first night had not come back on the sixth and has not
come back since in the four of them who are still alive.

Orrin Dunn, who was a Warden then and would carry the Reaper Pike a great deal later, said
the thing nobody wanted said. He said every wielder in this Hold does that in a field for a
quarter of an hour and then walks home. He said these nine are doing it standing still, all
day, and they are not walking home from it.

·  ·  ·

It was not a plague. The Foragers established that on the forty-second day and nothing has
ever contradicted them.

Nobody took it off anybody. Two of the nine had been sharing a hut with two people who were
not on the burn and those two were well, and stayed well, and one of them is alive now. It
does not pass between bodies. It came out of the top of a stack in a wood at Greying.

Fall-ash grows barley. Drawn-ash is what ground turns into where hold has been pulled out
of it and never put back, and it grows nothing and never will, and the two of them are the
same colour, and telling them apart is a Forager's whole craft. A wood at Greying has
drawn-ash in the seams of it. A smothered stack takes what is under the turf and sends it
up through the flue, and nine people stood in what came up.

It was in their lungs. That is the Foragers' word for it and they gave it on the
forty-second day, and they were the only people in the argument that followed who had ever
been right about anything before.

And what drawn-ash does is exactly what drawn-ash has always done. It takes hold out of
whatever is holding it and does not give it back. A field. A wood. Nine sets of lungs.

There is nothing new in this chapter at all. That is what makes it the worst one so far.

·  ·  ·

Colm Brack had stopped being able to hold a cup.

He could pick it up. He could not keep his hand shut on it for as long as it takes to
drink. His hand opened.

He was thirty-one. He had been the best axeman in that crew for four years and he had cut
about a third of the cordwood that went into the stack that did this to him. He put a cup
down on the table at the Hearth on the forty-sixth day by opening his hand over it, and
the cup did not fall over, because he put it down carefully, with the other hand under it,
because he had already had six days of practice.

Everybody in that room watched him do it.

The roster was read out about ten minutes later.

·  ·  ·

It was Orrin Dunn's hand. He gave it to Bran Holt and Bran Holt read it out, all of it,
including the two lines under the names, because reading out what is put in front of him is
what a Teller is for and he had never once declined to do it.

```
BRACKWOOD. The burn of the thirty-fifth day. The sick.

Oda Rowan
Sila Dunn
Rin Kettle
Tove Mourne
Ketil Stonewake
Nix Holt
Yeva Ember
Sable Vane
Colm Brack

Proposed verb: enemy_slain.
Proposed day: the forty-seventh, at first light, inside the palisade, and kept the same day
    on the flags.

Orrin Dunn, Warden
Tarn Stonewake
Yeva Holt
Nix Ember
```

Bran Holt refused one thing in the whole of that evening, and it was not the reading. He
was asked, afterwards, which verb he would cut if the vote went that way, and he said he
would cut it when there was something to cut and not before. It is the only recorded
occasion on which the second hand declined to answer a direct question, and I have thought
about it for a long time, and I think he was not being brave. I think he had looked at the
list of eighteen and could not find the one, and would not say so in front of the room.

·  ·  ·

The Cull's case was made at that Hearth and it was made well and I am going to put it down
the way it was given, because this book has an obligation and because nothing in the case
is stupid.

Orrin Dunn spoke and Tarn Stonewake did not, and what Orrin Dunn said came in three parts.

The first part was the arithmetic. Ten to begin with and three for every bond. He had gone
through the nine with Bran Holt's own board and the nine had between four and seven bonds
each, because they were burners and burners are old families and everybody in a Hold of
twenty-two knows everybody. He made it a little over two hundred, and the pool was standing
at forty-one, and at two hundred a settlement works at a third of itself and cannot build
its way out of the hole it is standing in. He said the Hold would not get that all at once.
It would get it over a month, one at a time, in the order they went, which is worse, because
a Hold can spend forty on a Monument and cannot spend twenty-six nine times in thirty days
on anything.

The second part was the Holding. A person who dies four hours outside the gate, in Greying
ground, in a hut, does not get kept the same day. There is a walk out and a walk back and a
rite to pay for and nine of them coming. A body that is not kept stands up on the second
day and a thing that stands up in Greying ground has soil to draw on, and the third growth
leaves the ring, and the fifth comes to the palisade. He said nine, and he said it in a Hold
that had never yet seen one at the fifth and would see one on the hundred and thirty-fourth.

The third part was the flags. There is a pyre floor on the Memorial ground now. It was laid
in an afternoon on the thirty-first day and what it means is that this Hold can burn
somebody on the day they stop. No Holding at all. No ring. No growth. No purse sitting in a
clearing storing up what the ground draws. The grief arrives when the Hold chooses, and it
arrives once, and the Hold can spend it.

That is the case. Put down early, put down cheap, put down inside the wall where they can be
kept the same hour. He said the word cheap himself and he did not soften it and the Cull
have never softened it in any valley I have a copy from. They say it out loud. It is the
only faction in this Vale that has never once lied about what it wants.

And they are the reason this Hold was still standing at the hundred and fifty-second day,
and they know it, and so do I.

·  ·  ·

Here is the thing about that roster that nobody in this valley has ever given the Cull
credit for, and I am going to give it to them now, in one paragraph, and then go on.

Orrin Dunn signed it, and Sila Dunn is on it.

Tarn Stonewake signed it, and Ketil Stonewake is on it.

Yeva Holt signed it, and Nix Holt is on it.

Nix Ember signed it, and Yeva Ember is on it.

Four signatures and four of the nine are theirs. There was no other way for it to be, in a
Hold of twenty-two with twelve family names in the whole valley, and that is exactly the
point: they knew before they wrote it down that every name on that list was somebody's, and
they wrote it down, and they put their own names under it where the room could read them.

Tove Mourne was my father's sister.

·  ·  ·

The Healers answered and there were three of them on the roll and I can name one.

Bran Holt entered all nine names of the sick and entered one Healer and did not enter the
other, and this is the single most resented omission in the second hand, and I have gone
through every page of it twice looking for her, and she is not there. He recorded what he
was asked to record. I do not know who asked him, or what she asked him, and I have
stopped guessing.

Wren Rowan spoke. She was Oda Rowan's granddaughter, which the Chronicle mentions once and
does not make anything of.

The Hearthkeepers' position is not softness and it has never been softness. It is that a
Husk is a person who was failed, and that what a person is owed is a rite and not a blade,
and that the nine were not Husks. They were nine people who were ill and who were standing
in the room. She put that first and she put it once.

Then she gave the part of it that has teeth. She said the arithmetic was right and that she
had checked it against Bran's board herself, and that the two hundred would arrive, and
that she was not going to pretend otherwise in front of people who could count. She said
the Cull's sum has a term in it that the Cull will not write down, which is what a
settlement is for. She said if the answer to nine people who cannot hold a cup is to put
them down at first light and burn them by noon, then the Hold has become a thing that keeps
a pyre floor for its own convenience, and that the floor was laid for a boy of seventeen
in one afternoon by four Carvers, two of whom spent the afternoon arguing about the fall of
the surface, because that is what people do.

Then she said she would go out and stand in it with them.

That is not an argument. Bran Holt wrote it down as though it were, and it is not, and it is
what she did.

·  ·  ·

The quarantine huts are the burners' own huts in the clearing, because there were huts
there already and nobody was going to build new ones.

Nine people in Brackwood. Two Healers with them. Eleven, out of a roll of twenty-two, four
hours from the gate.

Bran Holt wrote the rota for the forty-seventh day before the vote had been taken, because
somebody had to write two rotas and he wrote both, and this is the one he wrote for the
mercy.

```
On the roll, twenty-two.
Out: nine in the huts, two Healers with them, and the wielder, who is not on the rota.
Left on the rota, ten.
Field-hands, six on the roll, three left.
Foragers, four on the roll, one left.
Wardens, three on the roll, two left.
Carvers, four on the roll, two left.
Healers, three on the roll, one left.
Smith, one on the roll, one left.
Pool at forty-one. Work at four parts in five.
Relic-stone wants four Carvers.
```

One Forager. That is the line to stop on. Four on the roll and one left standing, and the
Forager's craft is telling fall-ash from drawn-ash by feel and by what grows, and it is the
one craft in this valley that cannot be halved, because half of it is a man walking into a
wood and guessing.

And the last line. Relic-stone wants four Carvers. Bran Holt wrote that on the evening of
the forty-sixth day as a complaint about a rota, and nobody in that Hold knew what the line
was going to be worth on the morning of the forty-seventh, and I have left it exactly where
he put it.

·  ·  ·

And there is the cost that has no answer, and Orrin Dunn gave it in nine words, and I will
give it in rather more because I am not him.

Brackwood is past the last fence.

The clock belongs to whoever is outside. Inside the Hold nothing moves and inside the fence
line nothing moves, and the Vale decays only while there is a body out in it, and this is
the oldest observed fact in the valley and the one everything else is built on. Eleven
people in a clearing in Brackwood is eleven bodies outside, continuously, for as long as
the quarantine stands. Not an expedition. Not a morning. A standing arrangement.

So for every day the Hold keeps its sick, the world moves. And the wood they are being kept
in is at Greying, and Greying goes to Ashen if nothing is done to it, and in Ashen the trees
stand up dead, and what is being kept alive in that wood is nine people whose lungs have
drawn-ash in them because they stood in that wood.

Orrin Dunn said it and sat down. Nobody answered him. Wren Rowan did not answer him, because
there is no answer, and because she had already said the only thing she had, which was that
she would go and stand in it.

I have read four Wayfarer copies from holds that faced this and three of them culled. I am
not going to pretend I do not know why.

·  ·  ·

Bran Holt called the vote for first light on the forty-seventh day. He read the roster
again at the end of the evening because two people had come in late and asked him to, and he
read it the same way both times.

Wren Rowan went out through the mere gate at dusk with the Healer whose name is not in this
Chronicle. They carried bread and lye and two blankets and a pot. Nobody told them to go and
nobody stopped them. The gate was shut behind them. The mere road was dry and the water was
still down a hand, and they were in Brackwood by dark.

There is no entry for the forty-sixth day.

## The Other Roads

There are none in this chapter, and saying so is part of the chapter.

Every other side road in this part of the book carries its branches here, because every
other side road had already been taken by the time the Teller sat down to it. This one had
not. The Hold went to bed on the forty-sixth day with a roster on the table, two rotas in
the second hand, nine people in a clearing, eleven bodies past the last fence, forty-one in
the pool, and the board blank.

This road forks in the next chapter and not in this one. The branches belong there and I
have put them there, all three of them, and one of them is the road that three holds out of
four took.

What I will put here instead is the only thing this chapter can carry without lying, which
is that on the evening of the forty-sixth day nothing had been decided and nobody in that room
was arguing in bad faith. The Cull had done the arithmetic and the arithmetic was correct.
The Healers had checked the arithmetic and agreed with it and refused it anyway. The Teller
had written both rotas. And Maren Verren, who had the blade and had carried it for
thirty-one days and would carry it for another thirty-eight, had not spoken once in six
days.
