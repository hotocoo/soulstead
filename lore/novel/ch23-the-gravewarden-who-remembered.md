# Chapter 23: The Gravewarden Who Remembered

> *It said Oda Ashfall and then Rin and it was right about both of them.*
> Recorded: the seventh barrow from the east end, Stonewake Scarp, opened from the inside

**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 50 to 53 · **Place:** Stonewake Scarp, the sealed barrow

I put this on the page of a Chronicle that has been kept for fifty-three days by a hand that was not raised to keep it, and I am writing it because the page is the page and the page is what the Vale will read when the Vale reads what happened on the scarp on the morning of the fifty-third, and the page needs to be true and the truth of the fifty-third day of the Hold is the most expensive truth the Hold has paid for.

The sealed barrow opened on the morning of the fifty-third.

I did not open it. I want that on the page first because the page has been argued about for eighteen days and the argument has been about who opened the barrow and the answer is that nobody in the Hold opened the barrow. The stone in front of the opening, which had been there for as long as the barrow had been a barrow, was on the scarp at first light, and the opening was open at first light, and the stone was inside the opening, set back into the barrow about a body's length, leaning against the far wall.

It was not broken. It was not chipped. The seams that had been packed with the dark stuff that is not ash and not lime were disturbed, and the disturbance was not from the outside. The packing had been pushed in from the inside. Whatever was in the barrow had moved the stone from the inside, on its own, without breaking it, and had set the stone down inside the opening in a posture that a person standing in the barrow would have set the stone down in.

I went up the scarp at first light with three Wardens and two Foragers. I went up because I had been past the barrow three times this season and had written about it in the Chronicle and had said in the Chronicle that the barrow was going to be opened by somebody and the somebody was going to be a person the Hold had not yet decided to be afraid of.

The person the Hold had not yet decided to be afraid of was the barrow.

·  ·  ·

It came out at the middle of the morning.

I want the morning described properly. The scarp in late autumn has a wind that comes up the quarry face and bends the long grass flat against the rock and brings the dust of the lime-kilns with it and the dust of the lime-kilns is white and the dust is what the Wardens taste first. The sky was the colour of a piece of slate held up to the sun. The Foragers had said the morning would not rain and the morning did not rain.

I was at the opening. The three Wardens were behind me at twenty paces, which is what I told them to do because a Gravewarden in plate is not a thing you stand in front of. The two Foragers were above us on the scarp edge, where they had gone to watch because the Foragers are not Wardens and the Watchers are not Healers and the Hold has rules about who stands where when something is coming out of a hole.

It came out slow. The plate made the sound plate makes against stone, which is a sound like a heel on a flagged floor but slower, and it came out into the light the way a person comes out into the light when they have been in a room a long time and have to adjust.

It was a Gravewarden. It was wearing the same kind of plate as the one Maren killed on the twenty-seventh, packed, one piece over another, every joint seamed with the dark stuff, the seams old and the plate old. It was ninety of what a body has, and eighteen of damage, and fifty-five of packing, and thirty of reach, and six tenths of a second of tell. It was at the scarp edge of the opening, with the morning behind it, and the morning was not behind it for very long.

It said a name.

The name was *Oda Ashfall*. It said it the way a Gravewarden says a name when it fights, which is when the plate opens enough for the plate to speak, and the plate was not open and the plate was speaking anyway, and the speaking was not in the mouth, the speaking was in the plate, the speaking was the name being spoken by the relic that was the name, and the name was being spoken because the relic was in the plate and the relic was the name.

Then it said another name.

The name was *Rin*.

It said *Oda Ashfall* and then it said *Rin* and the two names were correct and the two names were in order.

I am going to write this down because it has to be on the page. It said the name of the woman the Hold buried on the ninth day of the Hold, with her knife, in the ground. It said the name of the woman the Hold could not bury because she died in the long grass and the woman who walked in the long grass was not a woman any more and the woman who walked in the long grass was holding the Ashblade. The two women are not buried in the same place. The two women are not buried at all if the barrow has them, and the barrow has them, and the barrow has been on the scarp longer than the Hold has been on the rise.

I am Bran Holt and I am the second hand and I am a man of fifty-one with a bad hip and a tally board and I have just been told by a thing in plate that the Hold's dead are not in the ground the Hold put them in, and the thing told me by saying their names, and the names are correct, and the names are in order, and the names are in the plate.

·  ·  ·

The plate contains relics. Some of the relics are the Hold's dead.

I went down to the Wrenfield Monument the same morning, with two Wardens and the graver and a lamp, and the Monument is intact. The relic under it is intact. The stone is in place. The pane in the Memorial has Rin's name in it and the pane is not disturbed. The pane for Oda Ashfall is cut into the sheet of glass she carried across two valleys out of a house that was not hers, and the pane is not disturbed. The pane is the oldest pane in the valley and the only one with room left on it, and the room is in the corner where the name is cut small, and the rest of the glass is empty, and the empty is the right shape for what is in the plate.

I have written that sentence and I am going to write it again because it has to be on the page twice.

The pane is intact. The Monument is intact. The relics under both are intact. The Hold's dead are not under what the Hold put them under.

This is not the same Hold's dead.

The barrow is older than the Hold. The plate is older than the Hold. The relics in the plate are older than the Hold. The names the plate is speaking are the names of people who died in this valley, in the same fields the Hold has its fields in, in the same ground the Hold has its ground in, and the people who died are not the Hold's dead. The people who died are the dead of a settlement that was on the rise before the rise was a rise, and the settlement is gone, and the settlement's dead are in the plate, and the plate is on the scarp, and the scarp is where the plate has been since the settlement was gone.

The Hold came to a valley with a barrow on the scarp and a grave on the rise and a lake over a village. The Hold did not come to an empty valley. The Hold came to a valley that had been emptied, and the emptying had a barrow at the top of it and a cairn at the bottom of it and a pane in the middle of it that nobody had cut yet.

I am going to spend the rest of the chapter on what Maren Verren did about it.

·  ·  ·

She did not draw.

The Gravewarden was on the scarp and the Gravewarden was speaking the names and the Wardens were at twenty paces and the Foragers were above us and the Widow's Maul was on Maren's back and Maren did not take it off her back. She did not take it off her back for the eighteen days the argument was held. She did not take it off her back for the moment on the morning of the fifty-third when the Gravewarden came out of the barrow and stood in the light and said the names and a Warden behind me said the word *Maren* and Maren did not move.

The argument was held for eighteen days. The argument was held in the Hearth, in the Memorial, on the scarp, on the Mere road, in the byre, at the kiln, on the palisade, in every place a Hold of twenty-six people can hold an argument, and the argument was held by every person in the Hold who could stand, and the argument was about whether to kill the thing in the plate that knew the names of the Hold's dead, and the argument was held by a Hold that could not decide.

The Cull argued to kill. The Cull said the thing was an Echo in plate and the plate was its Echo and the Echo was at the fifth growth and the fifth growth was at the palisade and the palisade was the Cull's reason for being. The Cull said the thing had spoken the names of the Hold's dead and the speaking was a calling and the calling was what the Echoes do. The Cull said the relic under the plate was somebody's and the somebody was a person who had been reclaimed and the reclaiming was the Hold's work and the Hold's work was being worn by a thing that was not a person.

The Hearthkeepers argued not to kill. The Hearthkeepers said the thing was a person who was failed and what the thing was owed was a rite and not a blade. The Hearthkeepers said the names the thing was speaking were names the thing had been given to keep, and the keeping was the thing's work, and the work was what the Hearthkeepers' doctrine was for.

The Tellers argued both sides. The Tellers said the names were on the board if the names were on the board and the pane was not disturbed and the pane was the pane and the pane said what the pane said.

The Unnaming argued neither side. The Unnaming said the plate was speaking because the plate had been bound with relics and the relics were names and the names were being called and the calling was what kept the grip closed and the grip closed was the plate's hold on its own plate and the plate's hold on its own plate was what made the plate a person.

The Long Hold argued to bar the gate. The Long Hold said the thing on the scarp was what the Long Hold was for.

The Open Road argued to read the thing the names of every settlement the Wayfarers had a copy of, and to wait for the names that matched.

The Yielding argued to walk north. The Yielding said the argument was the answer and the answer was the road and the road went north.

Maren Verren argued nothing.

·  ·  ·

On the eighteenth day she walked up the scarp alone, with the Maul on her back, and stood in front of the Gravewarden, and the Gravewarden said *Oda Ashfall* and said *Rin* and said no other name, and Maren did not draw, and she stood there until the light went out of the scarp, and she walked back down.

I was at the fence. I have a copy of what she said when she came back, which I have been asked not to put on the page and which I am not going to put on the page, and what she said is enough for the Hold to know that she did not draw and that she is not going to draw and that the Gravewarden is to be left on the scarp with the names in its plate and the relic under its plate unspent.

The Cull never forgave it. The Cull have not said so in twenty days of days and they have not said so in the pages of the Chronicle, but the Cull never forgives a Mercy that is unpaid, and the Mercy here is unpaid, and the Cull's defence is a good defence and the Cull has been right three times in this book and is not going to be told it is wrong by a Teller who has held the graver for fifty-three days.

The Gravewarden is on the scarp. The Gravewarden is not roaming. The Gravewarden has not come down the scarp. The Gravewarden has not spoken the name of a person the Hold has buried since the two it spoke on the morning of the fifty-third. The Gravewarden says *Oda Ashfall* and *Rin* and Maren and the Cull and the Hearthkeepers and the Tellers and the Unnaming and the Long Hold and the Open Road and the Yielding have all been up the scarp to look at it and the Gravewarden has not changed.

The name the Gravewarden had, as far as the Hold can establish, was Halde. The name was on the plate, in a hand I have not seen before, and the hand was cut into the plate when the plate was new, and the hand had been hidden by the seams. Maren held the plate up to the light on the nineteenth day and read the name and the name was *Halde* and Halde is the unidentified spoken name from the Gravewarden Maren killed on the thirteenth day of the Hold, in the same scarp, in front of the same barrows, and the spoken name was Halde then and the spoken name is Halde now and the spoken name is the same name and the Hold does not know what that means.

The name does not solve anything. The name is a piece of plate.

·  ·  ·

The board read what the board read on the evening of the fifty-third.

I was at the Hearth when I cut it. The numbers were Memory fifty-five, Mercy forty-three, Communion forty-one, Dominion five. The Wake was a floor away on Memory and a floor away on Communion. The Wake was gated on every Echo reclaimed and the lineage not empty, and the Echoes were at zero and the lineage was not empty and the Wake was within reach of a single `echo_reclaimed` or a single `ally_revived` or a single Telling or a single `enemy_spared`.

Maren had spared the Gravewarden on the scarp. The entry on the board was `enemy_spared`, with the subject *Halde, a Gravewarden*, and the four numbers were zero on Memory, plus six on Mercy, plus two on Communion, minus three on Dominion.

I cut the entry. I read the line under it.

The line was *The Wake*.

It was the first time the board had ever said The Wake. I read it out loud. Maren was at the table. The Cull was at the door. The Hearthkeepers were at the kiln. The Unnaming was in the yard. The Tellers were at the Memorial. The Long Hold was at the gate. The Open Road was at the post. The Yielding was not there because the Yielding was not in the Hold.

Nobody knew what The Wake was. Nobody had ever seen The Wake on a board. The board had been cut for thirty-three days and the board had said The Quiet Field every evening of those thirty-three days, and the board said The Quiet Field because The Quiet Field is what the board reads when nothing else matches, and something had matched this evening, and the something was a wielder who had walked past a thing that knew her dead's names.

I read it out loud and I said: this is what the board says. I said: I do not know what it means.

Maren said: it means the Echoes are at zero.

The room was quiet. The Cull at the door was not quiet. The Cull at the door said what the Cull says when the Cull is not being a Cull, which is that the Mercy on the board was paid for by something the Cull had signed a list to put down.

I cut the line. I left the room.

The Wake was on the board for six days. It was off the board on the fifty-fifth, when somebody died and the dead was lying out and the Echoes were not at zero. The Wake came back eleven times after this and the Wake went away eleven times after this, and the Wake was on the board for the eleventh and last time on the two hundred and tenth day of the Hold, when a Teller who had been a boy on the morning of the fifty-third day walked out of the gate with the Ashblade and came back with the Echo of the woman the Gravewarden had spoken second and the Wake was paid for and the Wake locked.

That is in another chapter.

The Gravewarden is on the scarp. The Gravewarden is named Halde. The Gravewarden has not been put down.

The pane for Oda Ashfall is intact. The pane is the oldest pane in the valley and the only one with room left on it.
