# Chapter 18: The Gravewardens and the Yielding

> *They bound relics into their own plate. They were right. That is the problem.*
> Recorded: the barrow above Stonewake Scarp, six paces from the quarry edge

**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Day:** 27 · **Place:** Stonewake Scarp, the Grey Ash

The barrows above the quarry are the oldest thing in the Vale. They are not ours. They are not the Mourne's. They are older than the Mourne being a fire-mountain, which is to say they are older than the ash, which is to say they are older than the Hold and the Kettle and every reason a person has ever had to come to this valley.

There are eleven barrows. I have counted them three times and got eleven each time. They run along the upper scarp in a line that is too straight to be accident, with the openings all facing south, and the openings are blocked with stone that fits and has been fitted by a hand that knew what it was doing. Eight of the eleven are broken into, by people I will not name in this Chronicle because the names are not on any board, and what was inside is what I am going to spend this chapter on.

The thing inside the barrow was a person, once.

It is not a person now. It is the person's grip, with the person gone out of it. It is wearing plate. The plate is packed, one piece over another, every joint seamed with something dark that is not rust and not leather and not paint, and the seams are old and the plate is old and the plate fits as if it had been grown on.

The thing inside the plate is what the Hold calls a Gravewarden.

The word is the same word the Hold uses for the enemy archetype. That is deliberate. The people who bound relics into their own plate were the old order of the Wardens, and the order became what it bound, and the order is still doing what it did, and the people who come to the barrow to put one down are using the same word to mean both the enemy and the order, and the word is doing both jobs, and characters in this chapter will be visibly uncomfortable about it and I am one of them.

·  ·  ·

The relics in the plate are people.

I am going to say that again because it is the only piece of doctrine in this chapter that the rest of the chapter is built on.

The relics in the plate are people.

When an Echo is put down, the grief it had stored comes out all at once, and in that release a little of the hold hardens. What is left is a relic: a dense knuckle of matter, warm, slightly wrong to hold, shaped nothing like anything. A relic is hold that will not go back to being anybody's. The Smithy can bind one into steel, and the steel then remembers a motion the dead knew. The Carvers can set one under a stone, and the stone then holds ground against drawn-ash. The Gravewardens of the old order bound them into plate.

Plate, one relic at a time, one plate at a time, over years, on the theory that a body carrying enough borrowed hold cannot be opened by anything. A blow spends itself against the hold. A grip cannot find the seam. The plate holds, and the body inside the plate holds, and the body that holds is the body that put the relics there, and the body is not anybody any more, and the body has been holding for longer than anyone can remember.

The reliving is wrong. A relic is somebody who died and was reclaimed. The somebody is in the plate. The somebody is being held by the body that bound them, and the body is the one that killed them and could not let them go, and the body is still wearing the relic and the relic is still being worn and neither of them is at rest.

This is the doctrine of the old order. They were right. A body in packed plate cannot be opened. A body in packed plate cannot be put down. The body will speak the names of the people in the plate because the names are what the plate is made of and the names are what the plate is calling, and a Gravewarden at the barrow says a name when it fights because the name is the only thing it has left to say.

They were right. That is the problem. They are still standing.

·  ·  ·

Maren Verren killed one on the morning of the twenty-seventh, on the scarp, in the long grass above the quarry, where the Gravewardens are thickest.

I am not going to write the fight out long. There is a fight in this book already, on the nineteenth day, and the reader who has come this far does not need to be told what a heavy swing costs and what the breaking of packing sounds like and what a body does when it has spent everything on a parry it did not mean to make. The numbers are on the board and the numbers are the only thing the board can hold.

Ninety of what a body has. Eighteen of damage. Fifty-five of poise, which is the number that matters, because fifty-five of poise is the packing, and the packing is what you have to break before any of the rest of it matters, and the breaking is what the hold's whole doctrine of the heavy swing is for. Thirty of reach on a thing that moves at thirty, so it cannot chase you and does not need to. Six tenths of a second of tell.

She broke the packing on the third heavy swing. The packing broke the way packed plate breaks, with a sound like a seam giving in wet timber, and the person inside the plate was a person for about a tenth of a second, and the tenth of a second is when you are in, and she was in.

The relic it dropped was somebody's. She put it in her belt pouch without looking at it. She did not say who the relic was. The relic is on the Smithy shelf as I write this and nobody has held it and nobody has named it and the Hold has decided that the relic gets to be unnamed for as long as the relic is on the shelf, which is a long time, which is going to be forever.

The Hold has not yet decided what to do with the plate. The plate is on the scarp. The barrow is open. The body is in the long grass where it fell and it is not getting up because it is not a person any more and there is no grip to close and no hold to thicken and no Echo to walk out of the grass. The body will stay where it fell for as long as the scarp stays where the scarp is, which is also forever.

This is what the Gravewardens leave behind when they are put down. Plate and a body and an unnamed relic and a hole in the ground where a person used to be standing. The relic can be spent on stone that will outlast the Hold. The body cannot be spent on anything. The body is what it is.

·  ·  ·

There is another road out of this chapter and the road goes north.

I am going to spend the rest of the chapter on the road that goes north because the road that goes north is the strongest argument anyone in the Vale has ever made for anything, and nobody in the Hold is going to answer it, and I am writing it down because it is on the page of a Wayfarer copy I got from a leaf a woman who has been in this book three times carried out of the Hold on a leaf I did not give her.

The Yielding are people who walk north.

They walk into the Grey Ash, where the fall-ash and the drawn-ash are mixed together past the telling apart, and the trees stand dead upright in it, and the ground is ankle deep in ash that has been there longer than the Hold. They walk until they find a place they like, and they sit down, and they do not get up, and the ground takes the hold back early and unclosed, on the argument that the Vale is owed it and the rite was always a theft.

A rite is a machine that moves hold from the grip of the dead and into the hold of the living. A Burial spends grief to give a Legacy. A Pyre spends grief to give a Flask. A Telling spends grief to give Composure. A Monument spends grief and a relic to give ground held. A Wake spends grief and clears grief. Every rite is a conversion, and every conversion moves hold from one posture to another, and every conversion costs something.

The Yielding's argument is that the hold should not be moved at all. The hold should be given back. The grip should open because the grip was always closed wrong, and the closed-wrong was the Hold's doing, and the unclosed-wrong is the Yielding's giving it back. They leave no Echo. There is no purse in the field for the Hold to spend. There is no grip on the ground for the Hold to break. There is no Echo to walk out of the grass. There is no tier three to leave its ring and no tier five to come to the palisade. The dead is given back and the dead is gone and the ground does not feed and the ground does not put on drawn-ash and the ground is, for the first time since the Loosing, not Held.

I have walked into the Grey Ash once. I went with two Foragers and I have written the account in another chapter and I am not going to repeat it here. What I will say here is what the Foragers said to me on the way out, which is that the Yielding is the right answer to a question the Vale has not yet finished asking, and the question is whether the rite is owed or whether the rite is owed because the Vale has decided that the rite is owed, and the Yielding says the second is true and the first is not, and the Yielding might be right.

I do not think the Yielding is right. I think the rite is owed. I think the rite is owed because the rite is the only machine the Vale has for moving hold from one posture to another, and without the rite the hold stays where the dead put it and the dead put it in the ground and the ground feeds on it. I think the Yielding gives the hold back early but does not stop the hold from being there, because the hold was there before the dead and the hold will be there after the dead, and the ground is the same ground and the ground will hold it the same way.

I have not said this to a Yielding. I have not met a Yielding. There are no Yielding in the Hold and there have never been any and I am not predicting there will be.

The Cull have said it better than I have. The Cull say: the Yielding is the cleanest version of the Cull's position and it ends with the person who chose it, and the person who chose it does not get to choose for anybody else, and a settlement that has Yielded has stopped being a settlement. The Cull do not hate the Yielding. The Cull have said they would Yield themselves if they thought it would work, and they have said they do not think it would work, and they have said they will not say this twice because saying it twice is the Cull's version of saying it once too often.

I am Bran Holt and I am the second hand and I have written the strongest argument against everything this book is built on and I have not answered it and I am not going to answer it.

·  ·  ·

There is a barrow above the quarry that has not been opened.

It is the seventh from the east end, and the opening faces south, and the stone in front of the opening is fitted tighter than the stone on any of the eight that have been broken into, and the seam between the fitted stone and the opening is packed with something that is not ash and not lime and not anything the Smithy knows. It is the only barrow on the scarp that has not been touched, and I have walked past it three times this season and I have not touched it either, and the Hold has agreed that the barrow is not to be opened by a Hold decision, and the barrow has been on the scarp longer than the agreement.

I am putting it on the page because I want it here for whoever comes after me, and because the seventh barrow is going to be opened by somebody, and the somebody is going to be a person the Hold has not yet decided to be afraid of.

I am Bran Holt and I am the second hand and I have written the heaviest chapter in the book so far and I have written the heaviest line in it on the page about a road that goes north and I have not argued against the road and I have not argued for it and the road is going to be argued about for as long as the Vale has Tellers and the Vale has Tellers for as long as the book is kept and the book is kept for as long as there is wax.

The relic from the Gravewarden is on the Smithy shelf. The relic is unnamed. The relic is somebody.

---

Grieve Mourne, compiling. The relic stayed on the shelf for six days and was bound into the Ashblade on the thirty-third day by Ketil Brack and the binding is the first piece of weapon-art in the Vale, and the weapon the Ashblade became is the weapon Grieve Mourne carries out on the two hundred and tenth day. I have not put that on the chapter. The chapter does not need to know it yet.

The Yielding is going to come back in this book. They will be in Chapter 31 and they will be in Chapter 47 and they will be in Chapter 49, and they will be the only argument in the book that nobody in the book ever answers, because the argument does not have an answer. It has a road, and the road goes north, and the road does not come back.
