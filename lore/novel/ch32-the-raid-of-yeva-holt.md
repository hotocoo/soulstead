# Chapter 32: The Raid of Yeva Holt

> *Day one hundred and thirty-four. The Echo of Yeva Holt reached the palisade. Minus
> eight, minus six, minus four, minus five. It came at the timber.*
> Recorded: the board, in Asha Vane's hand, written eleven days late

**Hand:** Asha Vane, third hand, from inside a Warden's kit
**Days:** 128 to 136
**Place:** Brackwood; the palisade; the Hold

This is the worst week in the book and it is nine days long and it cost the Hold twenty of
its numbers, and every single thing in it was a consequence of something somebody had
done earlier and forgotten about.

I have put it here, in the side roads, out of its year, for one reason. If it sat where it
belongs, between the hundred and twenty-second day and the hundred and fortieth, it would
read as the story of Tarn Stonewake, who was a stranger to the dead and did nothing wrong
by any law the Hold had. It is not that story. It is the story of a conscription on the
ninety-fifth day, a rite not held on the hundred and twenty-eighth, and a body left in
Brackwood on the hundred and thirtieth, and those three things are on three different roads
and they arrived at the timber together.

Asha Vane wrote no entries for six days. She has told me why and I have put her reason at
the end where she put it.

·  ·  ·

Yeva Holt died on the hundred and twenty-first day in Brackwood, eleven days into
carrying the Ashblade, to Cinder Stalkers, in ground the Hold had pushed back on the
forty-seventh day and had not maintained since.

That is the first thing. Eleven days of work by twenty-two people and four Carvers setting
relic-stone, on the best single entry this Hold ever wrote, and then seventy-four days of
nobody walking out to check it.

Tarn Stonewake took up the blade on the hundred and twenty-second. Quarryman, forty-one,
Steady and Grim, and he had never learned her name. The words said over him were the
words: *never learned their name.*

A stranger has more in the purse than anybody, by fifteen in a hundred, and less in the
other thing by a fifth, and carries no grief at all inside the palisade. He was the most
efficient wielder this Hold ever had. He is also the reason the Hold is standing, and I
have written that in three places in this book, and I will write it here too, because what
follows is going to read like an indictment and is not one.

On the hundred and twenty-eighth day he set out with her unburied.

Nobody stopped him. There was no rite he owed her. He had not known her, he had no bond to
her, and the Hold's law has never required a wielder to hold a Vigil before going out, and
in a hundred and twenty-eight days no wielder had ever needed to be required.

The board took three off Memory and two off Communion and put two on Dominion for it. He
did not see the board that morning. He went at first light and the Hearth was dark.

·  ·  ·

On the hundred and thirtieth day he walked past her in Brackwood.

Her ring was nine days old and six paces across and at the fourth growth, and it was in the
charcoal burners' clearing where the huts are, and he came up the cart track with the Maul
on his shoulder and he saw it and he went round.

I have his account, given once, four days afterwards, in about forty words. He said the
ring was six paces. He said it was standing in the middle of it facing the huts. He said he
had a load of lime on his back and one flask left and he was four hours from the gate,
and that a fourth growth is not a thing a man goes at with a load on and one flask.

He was right. I want that in the same paragraph as the rest of it. Every Warden I have
asked has said he was right, and two of them have added that they would not have gone
round, and both of those two would have died in the clearing and both of them know it.

The board took five off Memory and three off Mercy and two off Communion and one off
Dominion.

He came in at dusk, put the lime in the shed, ate, and went to bed. Asha Vane cut the
entry the next morning and wrote beside it, in the margin, in the fast angry hand she had
by then: *he is not wrong and I would like to hit him.*

Four days later it left the clearing.

·  ·  ·

They do not leave until the third growth. Below that they come to the rim of the bent
grass and stop, and everybody in the Vale has known that since the nineteenth day.

Yeva Holt's Echo was at the fourth growth on the hundred and thirtieth and at the fifth on
the hundred and thirty-second, and the fifth is the one nobody had seen.

What a fifth growth does is come to the palisade.

The Foragers tracked it. There is a map, cut into a board and still in the Hearth, of what
it crossed between the hundred and thirtieth and the hundred and thirty-fourth, and the
map is the reason the Hold understands region decay at all. Brackwood clearing to the
Brack ford. The ford to the low rows. The low rows to the mere road. The mere road to the
fence line. And everything on that line went one state worse in the four days it took her
to walk it, and none of it has ever fully come back, and the low rows are Greying as I
write this and were Quick in the spring.

An Echo does not walk through a country. It walks a country out.

·  ·  ·

And now the ninety-fifth day arrives, thirty-nine days late, at the timber.

Colm Grey pulled Asha Vane out of the Carvers' shed on the morning of her wedding and put
her in a Warden's kit. Minus one, minus four, minus three, plus five, and Dominion at
eleven, the highest this Hold ever reached.

What the Hold lost that morning was not a Warden's worth of anything. It gained a body in
a kit that did not fit. What it lost was a Carver, and the Hold had four, and four is what
it takes to set relic-stone at the rate the palisade was being built.

So from the ninety-fifth day the palisade was built by three.

On the hundred and thirty-fourth day the wall of Ashfall Hold was stone for two thirds of
its length, in courses with relic set at the corners, and timber for the rest, on the
north-east arc, above the mere road.

It came up the mere road.

I do not say that it chose the timber. Asha Vane says it chose the timber and she was
standing on it and she is the third hand and she wrote the entry. Bran Holt's copy, made
the following spring from her board, has a note on it that a thing at the fifth growth is
walking the shortest line from where it died to where it is going, and the shortest line
from the Brackwood clearing to the Hearth comes up the mere road, and the mere road ends
at the north-east arc, and there is no choosing in it at all.

I record what was said. I do not settle it. I will note only that both of them are
describing the same wall and that the wall was timber because of a wedding.

·  ·  ·

Here is what the Hold had on the hundred and thirty-fourth day.

Tarn Stonewake, with the Widow's Maul, which he had taken off the wall on the hundred and
twenty-second day in a gesture the Hold read as arrogance and which was the exact opposite
of arrogance: he took the weapon that punishes hesitation because he did not have any.

One Warden, Orrin Dunn, twenty-six, with a shoulder that had been wrong since the hundred
and ninth and would be wrong for the rest of his life.

Asha Vane, Carver, in a kit, with a Warden's spear she had been carrying for thirty-nine
days and had used twice.

Two Healers, who cannot be called, and were not, and who spent the day in the Memorial
with the children and the four Brackwood sick, and who have never once been criticised for
it by anybody.

Eleven people at the stone two thirds of the wall, with nothing to do, because nothing came
at the stone.

And on the other side of the timber, a thing at the fifth growth carrying the Ashblade,
with Yeva Holt's shoulders and Yeva Holt's eleven days of skill and Colm Grey's habit
underneath it, because she had reclaimed him on the hundred and sixteenth and had carried
his motion into Brackwood with her, and thirteen days of a fed country on top of all of
it.

·  ·  ·

The fight at the timber took a long time and I have four accounts and they agree on
almost nothing except the order of three events.

The first is that it came over. Not through. Over. It put the Ashblade into the timber
twice at head height and used the blade as a step, which is not a thing any Husk or
Stalker or Warden of the barrows has ever done, and which is the single most frightening
sentence in this Chronicle, and which is in all four accounts in almost the same words.

It is not an animal. It has a person's habits and a person's tools and nothing of a person
in it.

The second is that Orrin Dunn went down in the first exchange, on the bad shoulder, inside
the wall, on the cart ground by the mere gate, and that Asha Vane put herself over him with
a spear she could not use, and that neither of them died, and that the reason neither of
them died is that Tarn Stonewake arrived.

The third is that it took him an hour.

An hour. In the yard, with the whole Hold watching from two thirds of a wall they could
not get down from fast enough to help and would not have helped by reaching.

The Maul takes forty-six hundredths of a second from the decision to the landing, going
heavy, and half a second again afterwards in which the world may do as it likes with you.
He committed to that swing, by Asha Vane's count, thirty-one times. He landed nine of
them. He drank four flasks, which is one more than the Hold's ration and was the Pyre's
fourth, and the Healers gave it to him without being asked and wrote it down.

Yeva Holt's Echo had no packing. It had a coat. Nine landings from a post-driver on a body
with a coat on, and it came back eight times.

It went down in the cart ground about an hour after it came over the timber, at the ninth
landing, and the ring that stood up when it opened was six paces of the Hold's own yard,
and there is a ring of grass by the mere gate that is greener than the rest of the yard to
this day, and nobody mows it, and nobody has ever said why not.

·  ·  ·

The Hold reclaimed her at the gate on the hundred and thirty-fifth day.

I have written that as though it were a separate event and on the board it is, and this is
the one place in this book where I think the board is being dishonest about something
rather than merely ignorant of it. The fight was on the hundred and thirty-fourth. The
reclaiming is entered on the hundred and thirty-fifth because Tarn Stonewake did not have
anything left to carry the Ashblade back the twelve paces from the cart ground to the
Hearth, and lay where he was until first light, and Asha Vane would not enter a reclaiming
for a man lying on his face in a yard.

She entered it when he stood up. Plus six, plus three, plus one, plus two. The eleven
growths of Yeva Holt released everything they had been holding in the middle of the
settlement and it came out over thirty people standing in their own yard at dawn, and
Asha Vane's note for that morning is one line and it is the best thing in the third hand:

*It is the same substance. I have always known that and I had never stood in it.*

The pool stood at sixty-eight.

·  ·  ·

They held a Wake on the hundred and thirty-sixth day.

It was the first Wake in the Hold's history, and the second and last was held seventy-four
days later on the two hundred and tenth, and I held that one, and that is a different
chapter.

A Wake takes sixty and then it takes everything else. That is the rite. Sixty is the price
and the clearing is what you have bought, and a Hold cannot Wake its way out of a shortage
because a shortage does not have sixty in it, and by the same arithmetic a Hold cannot
hold a Wake until something has gone badly enough wrong to afford one.

Sixty-eight. They paid sixty and the other eight went with it and the pool was at nothing
for the first time since the sixth day of the Hold.

There is no liturgy. Nobody cuts anything and nobody raises anything and there is no fire.
Thirty people and a cask in the yard and every name spoken once, oldest first, and the
naming that day ran to eight names and took about four minutes, and then people stayed out
there until it got light again.

Tarn Stonewake, who had no bond to any of them and carried no grief inside the palisade
and by every arithmetic the board has should have felt nothing at all, is recorded by two
witnesses as having said Yeva Holt's name at the Wake, which he had not learned when he
took the blade from her.

Asha Vane wrote that down. It is the last thing she wrote about him. She wrote *he said
it correctly* and nothing else, and I have thought about that entry for eleven years.

·  ·  ·

She wrote nothing for six days, from the hundred and thirtieth to the hundred and
thirty-sixth, and the board for that week was reconstructed afterwards from her spear
tallies and from what the Foragers had mapped, which is why the entries are eleven days
late and why three of them are in the wrong order in the original and have been left in
the wrong order by every hand since, including mine.

I asked her once, at the end, why she stopped.

She said that a Teller writes down what happened and that for six days she was one of the
things that was happening, and that she did not trust herself to be both, and that she had
decided it was better to have a gap than a lie.

I have taken the blade myself since. I have written the chapter about it in the first
person and I have said in that chapter that I broke the discipline exactly once.

She was thirty-four in that kit and she was right and I was not.

## The Other Roads

### The Vigil held on the hundred and twenty-eighth

In three of the four Wayfarer copies I have, the hold in question buried its wielder before
its next wielder went out, because in three of those four valleys there is a law requiring
it, and the law in all three cases was written after a week like this one.

Held on the hundred and twenty-eighth, the week does not happen. The Hold pays twenty-eight
of grief for a Burial it can afford, gains six of health for every successor it will ever
have, and the ring in the Brackwood clearing never reaches a third growth, so it never
leaves, so the low rows are Quick to this day, so the fence line is Quick, so the mere road
is Quick, and Orrin Dunn's shoulder heals.

`vigil_held` a Burial · Memory +8 · Mercy +4 · Communion +5 · Dominion 0

Against the week as it happened, which cost minus nineteen, minus nine, minus ten and minus
four across five entries, this single road is worth twenty-seven of Memory and fourteen of
Communion in the difference.

**Pressure:** straight toward **The Wake**, and it is the cheapest road to it in this book.
The whole of the ending that Ashfall eventually got could have been bought for twenty-eight
of grief on an ordinary morning by a man who had no reason to spend it.

### The Echo reclaimed in the clearing on the hundred and thirtieth

Tarn Stonewake goes at a fourth growth with a load of lime on his back and one flask.

I have a copy in which a wielder did exactly this, in a charcoal clearing, four valleys
west, and the entry after it is `wielder_died` and the entry after that is
`successor_taken` and the entry after that is `echo_abandoned` for the same body, and their
board never recovered.

`echo_reclaimed` · Memory +6 · Mercy +3 · Communion +1 · Dominion +2, with a wielder's
death against it at better than even odds.

**Pressure:** toward **The Wake** if it works and toward **Ash Inheritance** if it does
not, because a hold that loses two wielders in ten days starts skipping rites to save
grief and then stops cutting panes to save Carvers.

### Letting it into the Hold and fighting it in the lanes

This is in one copy and the copy is seven pages and the seventh page stops in the middle of
an entry.

The reasoning is not stupid. A fifth growth in an open yard has thirty of reach and an arc
that takes two bodies. In a lane between a workshop and a byre it has neither, and four
people with spears have a wall on each side, and the Hold's Wardens argued for exactly this
on the hundred and thirty-third and were overruled by Tarn Stonewake, who said that the
thing would then be inside, and that he had seen what it did to the low rows in a day, and
that he was not going to let it stand in the middle of a settlement doing that to the
ground the settlement was standing on.

The copy that tried it has no further entries. The last legible line is `echo_raided` and
then a subject and then nothing.

`echo_raided` · Memory -8 · Mercy -6 · Communion -4 · Dominion -5, and then whatever
follows, which in one case out of one is nothing at all.

**Pressure:** toward **The Long Hold**, by the only route that actually leads there. It is
not a wall that makes a Long Hold. It is a hold that has been hurt inside its own lanes
once and has decided never to open anything again.
