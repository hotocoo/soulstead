# Chapter 44: The Long Hold

> *You held the vale by force and buried nobody. The walls stand. Nobody inside remembers
> why they are worth holding.*
> Recorded: the fifth line of the board at Vane Hold, cut deep, still legible

**Hand:** the last Teller of Vane Hold, name illegible, copied whole by Grieve Mourne
**Days:** Vane's own count, day 1 to day 88
**Place:** Vane Hold

What follows is not mine and I have not improved it.

Asha Vane brought out eleven legible leaves and a copy of the board on the ninety-ninth day
of our count. Four more leaves came apart in her hands on the way home and she wrote down
what had been on them as far as she could recall, and I have marked her recollections as
hers and set them apart, because a Teller's memory of a page is not a page.

The hand is unsigned. The first leaf has a name cut at the head of it and the name has gone
to damp, and what is left is four letters of a family name and none of a given one, and the
four letters are consistent with three of the twelve. I have not guessed and I am not going
to.

Their count begins at their day one, as ours does, and runs to their day eighty-eight, and
there are no entries after that and there is no last leaf missing. The book simply stops
with three quarters of a leaf unused.

I have copied it whole, including the parts that are only verbs. Especially those.

·  ·  ·

**Leaf one.**

```
D1. Wall begun. 141 of us. Four Carvers. Quarry sound.
D3. Hrth up. Board up. Five lines ruled, four words, one empty.
D6. First course true. Bran Ember says the corners want relic and
    we have none. Noted for the Smith.
D9. Rite for the founder's mother. Burial, whole, with her shears.
    Mem +8 Mrc +4 Com +5 Dom 0.
D11. Nix Vane took up the blade. Com +2 Dom +1.
```

One hundred and forty-one people and four Carvers. We had twenty-two and one Carver on our
eleventh day. I have read that first leaf more times than I have read any other document
in my life and I have never got past the first line without stopping.

I have tried to reconcile the roll and I cannot, and I am going to say so here rather than
have somebody find the gap later and think I hid it. A hundred and forty-one at the wall's
beginning. Twenty put down in the huts on their forty-sixth and fifty-third. Four wielders
dead outside the ditch. That leaves a hundred and seventeen and Asha Vane counted a
hundred and sixteen twice.

The difference is one. It may be the Teller, if a Teller is standing at a board in that
Hearth and she did not count the board as a chore. It may be a birth nobody entered,
because no board in any valley has a verb for a birth either, and their count ran eighty-eight
days and their calendar ran a great deal longer than that, and in the calendar years
between their eleventh day and their fifty-first a hold of that size would have had
children and buried grandmothers and entered none of it.

One. I record it as one and I leave it open.

**Leaf two.** Their days twelve to twenty-six. Ordinary in the way our second month was
ordinary: two Husks put down at the ditch, a Stalker in the west rows, a stand of their
own Brackwood equivalent going Turning and a working party sent to it and the entry
`region_saved` against it, plus four, plus seven, plus six, plus two, in a hand that is
plainly pleased with itself. They pushed decay back on their nineteenth day. It took
eleven people and two days, because they had eleven people to send.

Their Memory stood at twenty-nine on their twenty-sixth day. Their Communion stood at
nineteen. Their Dominion stood at four.

If the book stopped there it would be a different chapter and nobody would ever have
copied it.

·  ·  ·

**Leaf three** is where it turns and it turns on one entry.

```

D31. Ring at the east ditch, second growth. Oda Brack would not go.
     Warden rota short. No verb for would not go.
D33. Oda Brack fell at the east ditch. Mem +2 Dom -3.

D34. Left the Echo of Nix Vane to grow. Mem -5 Mrc -3 Com -2 Dom -1.
D34. Left the Echo of Oda Brack to grow. Mem -5 Mrc -3 Com -2 Dom -1.
```

Two wielders in five days and both rings left standing, because the second one died going
to the first one.

That is the whole mechanism of a bad month in any valley and it is the same in every copy I
have. A hold does not fail at a wall. It fails at the arithmetic of who can be sent to
fetch whom, and the arithmetic goes wrong in one week and then every week afterwards is
worse by exactly as much as the week before it.

**Leaf four.**

```
D37. Pulled Yeva Brack from her work to fight. Mem -1 Mrc -4 Com -3 Dom +5.
D37. Pulled Ketil Holt from his work to fight. Mem -1 Mrc -4 Com -3 Dom +5.

D39. Yeva Brack took up the blade. Com +2 Dom +1.
D41. Left the Echo of Bran Ember to grow. Mem -5 Mrc -3 Com -2 Dom -1.
```

Four conscriptions in two days and a wielder dead the day after them.

I want to point at what that costs, because our Hold did it once and got eleven of
Dominion out of it and I have written three pages about the shed and the wedding and the
knotted cord. Four conscriptions is twenty of Dominion and sixteen off Mercy and twelve off
Communion in forty-eight hours, and it is also four sets of hands out of a workshop, and
the workshop at Vane Hold was building the wall.

They were still building the wall. That is in the entries. The wall went up all the way
through this.

**Leaf five.** Their days forty-two to forty-nine. Five conscriptions more, which makes
nine, and the Dominion climbing five at a time like a tally of sacks, and a fourth wielder,
and the first of the two culls.

```
D44. Pulled Colm Mourne from his work to fight. Mem -1 Mrc -4 Com -3 Dom +5.
D45. Pulled Asha Rowan from her work to fight. Mem -1 Mrc -4 Com -3 Dom +5.
D46. Put down the sick of the north huts. Eleven. Mrc -11 Dom +22.
D47. Yeva Brack fell. Mem +2 Dom -3.
D47. Ketil Holt took up the blade. Com +2 Dom +1.
D49. Left the Echo of Yeva Brack to grow. Mem -5 Mrc -3 Com -2 Dom -1.
```

Eleven people, entered as one line, in a hand that has not changed its letters at all.

It is the same verb the board gives you for a Stalker in the rows. `Put down.` Minus one of
Mercy and plus two of Dominion, eleven times over, and the board does not have anything
else to offer, because the board has eighteen verbs and it has never had one for this.

I have copied that line exactly and I have copied the next line exactly too, which is about
a wielder, and the two lines are the same size.

·  ·  ·

**Leaf six** is the leaf with the brackets on it.

```
D51. Smith to make brackets, gate, inside face. Eight. Iron.
     Two arms of timber to sit in them.
     Ketil Holt asks what the brackets are for.
     Told: for if.
D52. Brackets set. Gate opens as before.
D53. Put down the sick of the south huts. Nine. Mrc -9 Dom +18.
```

*Told: for if.*

That is three words and they are the worst three words in any document I have copied out of
any valley, and I have copied out four last pages and one unsigned fragment.

Somebody stood at a fire on their fifty-first day and made eight iron brackets for a gate
that was still being opened, and somebody else asked what they were for, and the answer
went on the board, and the gate opened as before on the fifty-second, and on the fifty-third
they killed nine more of their own people.

Asha Vane's recollection, marked as hers: she says one of the four leaves that came apart
had a line on it about the brackets being made out of a plough. I cannot confirm it. She
was holding the leaf when it went and she has never claimed more for it than that she
thinks she read it.

**Leaf seven.** Their days fifty-four to fifty-eight. The last leaf with sentences on it.

```
D54. Ketil Holt fell. Mem +2 Dom -3.
D54. Sila Vane took up the blade. Com +2 Dom +1.
D55. Sila Vane would not go past the ditch. Warden rota is nine
     conscripts and no Wardens. There is no verb for would not go.
     I have written it in words four times now and it is not an entry
     and the board will not carry it.
D56. Ring at the east ditch, fifth growth. Came to the wall.
     The Echo of Nix Vane reached the palisade.
     Mem -8 Mrc -6 Com -4 Dom -5.
D57. Put down. Mrc -1 Dom +2.
D57. Nobody went out to fetch the others.
D58. Timber up. Two arms. Brackets held.
     I asked whether we are going to enter this and was told
     there is no verb for it.
```

There is no verb for it.

Four times on one leaf, in a hand that is still legible and still level, a Teller writes
that the thing the Hold is doing cannot be put on the board.

Our Hold has the same hole in its board. I have written about it twice, once about three
Wayfarers on our twenty-fifth day and once about a road-shrine on our hundred and first,
and both times I have said that the absence of a verb for hospitality has cost this valley
something I cannot measure.

Vane Hold found the other side of the same hole. There is no verb for barring a gate. So the
single most consequential act in the history of that settlement is not an entry. It is a
sentence in the middle of a leaf, and the board did not move for it by one point in any
direction, and the board went on being kept for thirty more days as though nothing had
happened, because by the board's own accounting nothing had.

Thirty entries and no deaths recorded between them.

I have been asked by two Wayfarers and by one of our own Wardens what that means, and there
are two readings and I am not going to settle between them.

The first reading is that they stopped entering the deaths. A Teller who is not going to be
allowed to hold a rite may decide that there is no point in cutting a name he will never be
able to spend, and may keep cutting the one verb that is still true, which is that no rite
was held today either.

The second reading is that nobody died. Nothing came. The clock was not running, because
nobody was outside the wall, and inside a hold where the clock is not running the bread
does not rise and the pot does not cool and a person does not get any closer to the end of
themselves. Thirty days with one verb on them may be thirty days in which the only thing
that happened was that a Teller went to the board in the morning and wrote down the only
true thing available.

Asha Vane holds the second reading. She has held it since the ninety-eighth day of our
count and she held it to the end. She says a hold in which one hundred and sixteen people
are standing at looms and wells and doorways is a hold in which one hundred and sixteen
people died at more or less the same moment, and that the moment is not on the board
because there was nobody left to cut it.

Bran Holt's note on the copy, made the following spring, is: *the board does not lie. It
also does not help.*

·  ·  ·

**The board.**

It is in the stone Hearth on the north wall, out of the weather, cut deep because whoever
cut it cut all their letters deep. Asha Vane copied it on the ninety-eighth day of our
count and I have it here in full and this is the only place in this book I have put it
whole.

```
   Memory against Oblivion        +9
   Mercy against Ruin            -23
   Communion against Isolation   -44
   Dominion against Surrender    +51

   Drifting toward:  The Long Hold
```

Fifty-one of Dominion is the highest figure in any copy anybody has. Our Hold reached
eleven, once, for four days, and I have written three pages about how we got there and what
it cost a Carver's wedding morning.

Minus twenty-three of Mercy is two culls, twenty people, entered as two lines.

Minus forty-four of Communion is nine conscriptions and a gate.

And nine of Memory, which is the figure nobody expects and which I find the hardest of the
four to sit with. Nine is not an Unnaming hold. Nine is a hold that was still cutting
panes. There are panes at Vane Hold, in a range on the south side of the Memorial ground,
and they are good work, and the last four are uncut blanks with names in wax beside them
waiting for pearl that was never going to come because nobody was going to light another
pyre.

Nine is a hold that was still trying, right up to the leaf with no sentences on it.

·  ·  ·

The fifth line is what this chapter is named for and I have put it at the head of the
chapter and I am not going to put it twice.

I will say only that it is cut in the same letters as the four numbers above it, at the
same depth, by the same hand, and that nobody has ever been able to account for the fifth
line on any board in any valley, including this one, including ours, including the blank
one I planed and ruled myself and could have left off.

·  ·  ·

Half a page of mine, and then I am done with them.

I went out to the Vane Road on the evening of the two hundred and tenth day of our count,
with the Wake still going on behind me in our yard, and I stood at their gate, and the gate
is barred from the inside with two arms' worth of timber in eight iron brackets made on
their fifty-first day for if.

The timber is scored.

Asha Vane noted the scoring on the ninety-eighth day and did not count the marks, and I
have always assumed that was a choice and I have never asked her, and she is dead now and I
cannot.

I counted them. There are a great many and they are all recent.

I know what recent means on wood in a hold where the clock is not running. I have thought
about it for the whole of the time since and I have concluded that I do not know what
recent means there, and that the marks are on the inside face, and that they are at the
height a person's hands would be.

That is what I have. I record what was said, and where nothing was said, I record the
wood.
