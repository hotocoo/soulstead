# Chapter 15: The Unnaming

> *A pane is glass. A name is fluxed into it. The smear is on the glass.*
> Recorded: Sila Grey, the smear in the east range, second pane from the post

**Hand:** Bran Holt, with a scraped line · **Days:** 22 to 23 · **Place:** the Memorial, the Hold at night

The pane is the oldest pane in the Memorial and the only one with a smear on it, and I am going to spend this chapter on the smear because the smear is the only piece of evidence I have and I will not have it again, and because Grieve Mourne is going to hold this page to the fire and to the light and the smear and the scrape will not yield what they did not yield to me when I held them.

I found it on the morning of the twenty-second, when I went to the Memorial to cut the second day's Telling against the pane we had put Rin in. The east range runs along the wall the kiln smoke does not reach, and there are four panes in it, and the second pane from the post is Bel Brack's name, cut by Sila Carver on the eleventh in a cramped hand that did not have a B in it for the first three strokes and produced a letter that looks more like an R upside down than anything else, and I am writing this down because I have looked at the pane more often than I have looked at any pane in the Vale and I want the record of it to be accurate in the matter of the letter.

The smear was on the glass, low, on the left side of the name, and it was not a scratch. It was not a chip. It was a polishing off, the way glass polishes off when somebody has rubbed it with a wet cloth and a little ash, and a pane polished that way is glass again, and the name is gone, and there is no way of putting the name back on without cutting the pane over, which means cutting a new name on top of the old one, which the Hold does not do.

The smear was visible at one angle only, and that angle was a person's height, and that height was mine. If I knelt down and looked up the pane from below, the smear disappeared into the polish of the surface and the glass looked like glass. If I stood up and looked down, the smear caught the light from the kiln smoke out of the south and a faint shadow ran across where the name had been. The smear was on the inside of the pane, which is the side the name is on. The outside was clean.

I looked at it for a long time. I did not touch it. I went back to the Hearth and I asked who had been at the Memorial in the night and I got five names and a shrug, and the five names were a Healer, two children too small to reach the pane, a Carver who had been cutting a stave for a fork and was covered in shavings, and Sila Grey, who was a Forager and who was forty-three and who had been at the kiln at midnight because she was shifting shell.

I did not name Sila Grey to anyone. I went back to the Memorial at noon and I looked at the pane again from above, and the smear was still there, and the polish was still good, and the name was still gone, and I stood there for ten minutes. Then I cut the second Telling against the smear, which is to say I cut nothing, because the Telling is not cut into the pane, the Telling is cut into the Chronicle, and what the pane was holding was Bel Brack's name, and the pane was still holding the rest of the name, and the missing letter was the B and I have not put the B back because the B was not on the pane when I looked and I do not know who took it off.

·  ·  ·

I will not write down who I think did it.

The argument is the reason this chapter exists and not the smear, and the argument is the argument of a person and not a faction, and the argument is good. The person is Sila Grey, who is a Forager, and who is liked by every person in the Hold, and who has spent the last year in the field without saying much about the panes and has spent the last two days in the field saying more about them.

I caught her at the kiln on the evening of the twenty-second, with the shell still going in and the ash coming out, and I asked her to tell me what she thought the panes were for.

She said they were a calling.

She said it the way she said everything, flat, looking at the kiln, not at me. She said the dead did not walk before the Vale started writing them down. She said an Echo grows because it is being called. She said the pane is a voice that calls a name, and the name is what keeps the grip closed, and if the pane stops calling the name then the grip opens on its own with nobody having to walk out into a field with a maul. She said a hold that stops calling its dead is a hold that stops feeding its dead, and a dead person who is not being fed does not stand up.

I have given this argument in summary. It is a good argument. It is the cleanest argument any person in the Hold has yet made about anything, and it is wrong in a way that nobody can put a finger on.

·  ·  ·

The counter is also a good argument, and I have it from the same evening, in the same place, by the same kiln, by the same person.

Sila Grey is also a Hearthkeeper.

She said, when I asked her what was wrong with the argument, that the argument had never been tested. She said no Hold has ever stopped calling its dead and watched what happened, because the law against stopping is the law and it is the first law the Vale wrote and the law came before the reasoning and not after. She said a pane without a name is glass, and glass is what the Carvers want for the windows the Hold has not yet built, and the use of the pane is to be a pane and not a window. She said the Echo of Oda Ashfall is in the west field and has been in the west field for fourteen days and would still be there if every pane in the Memorial had been wiped, because the pane is not the only thing that calls a name, the room does, the Hearth does, the Teller does, the wielder does when she touches the doorstone on her way out. She said the smear does not unmake the calling. The smear only makes the smear. She said: if you want the Echo of Oda to go down, you go out into the field with a spade, and you bury her, and the pane is a piece of the work and not the work.

She is also right. She is also wrong in a way that nobody can put a finger on.

The two arguments are not the same and they are not opposites and the difference between them is the smear on the glass and the smear has not been put back.

·  ·  ·

I will not write down which argument I hold. I will not write down which argument the Hold holds. The Hold has not been asked. The Hold does not vote on doctrine. The Hold votes on whether to hold a Telling and whether to bar the gate and whether to spend the relic on stone or steel, and it has never voted on whether the panes are a calling, because a vote would commit the Hold to one reading and the Hold is not ready to commit.

The law against the shaving is the first law the Vale wrote. It was written by Sila Carver on the eleventh, on the same day she cut the first pane in the Vale, which is also Bel Brack's pane with the missing B. The law says: a pane cut with a name is not to be cut again. The law does not say what the penalty is for cutting against the law. The law does not need to say what the penalty is, because the penalty is that the Tellers will read the page at the Hearth and the page will say who did it and the page is the hold's memory and the hold does not forget.

This is the same book I am writing in and the page is this page, and I am not going to be the Teller who puts a name on a page that the Teller has no right to put.

I am going to put on this page what I have, which is the smear, the law, the argument, the counter, and a single line that I cut on the morning of the twenty-third in my own hand and that I am now scraping out, which I am doing in front of Grieve Mourne, who is nine and is watching me do it.

·  ·  ·

The line I scraped out was on the second leaf of the Chronicle, in the margin, in my own hand, on the morning of the twenty-third. It was a single line. I cut it because I was tired and because the Hold was tired and because I had not slept, and it was the kind of line a Teller cuts in the margin when the Teller has thought of something the Teller is not ready to commit to the board.

I scraped it out the same morning.

I scraped it out because a Forager whose name I am not going to put on this page had been at the kiln at midnight and a pane in the east range has a smear on it, and the two facts together suggested a thing I am not going to write down, and I cut a line that suggested it more directly than I should have, and the line was wrong of me to cut because the line was a sentence and not an entry and a sentence in the margin of a Chronicle is not a telling, it is an accusation, and an accusation by a Teller is the worst thing a Teller can put on a page.

Grieve Mourne, who is nine, was standing at the end of the table when I did it. He was not supposed to be in the Hearth. He watched me scrape the line out. I did not ask him what he thought he saw because he is nine and he should not have been in the Hearth and the question would have made him complicit in something I am not yet ready to make him complicit in.

He will be on this hand one day. He will hold this page to the fire and to the light and he will not find the line. The line is gone. I scraped it out cleanly. The wax under the line is wax that has been worked twice and it is a different colour from the wax on either side of it, and a person who knew the page would know where the line had been, and the person who would know is the person who would be able to read the line, and I am not the person who can read the line, and Grieve Mourne is not the person who can read the line.

I do not know what the line said. I cut it at four in the morning and I scraped it out at six and I am going to bed.

·  ·  ·

This is what the page is. The pane has a smear on it. The smear is on Bel Brack's pane, second pane from the post in the east range. The smear is on the inside of the glass and is visible only from above and is in the place the B would have been. The law says the pane is not to be cut again. The law does not say what the penalty is. The argument is good and the counter is good and both are held by the same person and the person is Sila Grey and I am not going to put her name in this book again because she has been put in this book enough and she does not know she has been put in this book and the rest of the book will go on putting her in it.

I am Bran Holt and I am the second hand and I have scraped a line out of the Chronicle that I cut four hours ago and the line is gone and I have told Grieve Mourne that the line is gone and Grieve Mourne will spend the rest of this book trying to find it.

He will not find it.

The pane has a smear on it.
