# Chapter 31: The Ash Orchard

> *Sit down. The ground takes it back.*
> Recorded: the Ash Orchard, north of the Grey Ash, where the trees stand dead upright

**Hand:** Asha Vane · **Days:** 102 to 104 · **Place:** the Grey Ash

Seven people left the Hold on the morning of the one hundred and second and walked north.

Two of them were the surviving Brackwood sick. Colm Brack, who could not hold a cup for a full minute, and Yeva Ember, who could hold a cup but who had not been able to hold a cup with both hands for a season. They were in the yard at first light with their packs on their backs and their packs were not the packs of a Hold that is going north for the day. The packs were the packs of a Hold that is going north and not coming back.

A third was Sila Grey. Sila Grey was not sick. Sila Grey was a Forager who had been at the kiln at midnight more nights than she had not, and Sila Grey was sixty-one, and Sila Grey was the only person in the Hold who had said at the Hearth on the fifty-eighth evening that the panes were a calling and that the calling was what was keeping the Echoes standing, and the Hearth had heard her and the Hearth had re-cut the panes and the Hearth had held the Telling, and Sila Grey had not been the Cull's prisoner and Sila Grey had not been the Hold's prisoner and Sila Grey had been at the kiln.

She had decided anyway. I am going to write the word *anyway* twice because the word is the whole reason she is on this road.

The other four were a Carver, a Warden who had been on the wall and who had come down off the wall, a Healer who had been on for the day and who was going to be on for the day, and a Field-hand whose name is on a pane and whose name is in the board and whose name I am not going to put here because she came back and the coming back is the second half of this chapter.

They walked past the palisade at the north gate and the palisade had no Warden on the north gate because the Warden who should have been on the north gate was the Warden who had come down off the wall, and the wall had nobody on it for an hour and the hour was the hour they needed.

I was at the Smithy. I saw them from the Smithy door. I did not raise the alarm. I did not raise the alarm because Colm Grey was at the Hearth and Colm Grey was the wielder and Colm Grey was deciding what to do about seven people walking north out of his Hold with their packs on their backs and the packs not the packs of a day.

The Ash Orchard is north of the Grey Ash. I have been there once, on the seventy-ninth day, with two Foragers who had not been there either and who had wanted to know what was in it, and I have written the account in another chapter and I am not going to write the account again here.

What I will say here is what is in the Ash Orchard.

The Ash Orchard is a stand of dead trees standing upright in ankle-deep drawn-ash. The trees are not skeletons. The trees are not the trees that grew in the Ash Orchard, because no tree has grown in the Ash Orchard since the Loosing, and the trees in the Ash Orchard are the trees that were there before the Loosing, and the Loosing took them and they did not fall. They stood upright because they were standing when the Loosing took them and the Loosing did not take the standing, and the standing is what is left.

Between the trees are depressions. The depressions are the Yielding. People from every valley have been walking to the Ash Orchard since before there was a Chronicle to record the walking, and the people have been walking to the Ash Orchard to sit down, and the sitting down is the Yielding, and the Yielding is the giving back, and the giving back is the ground taking the hold early and unclosed, and the unclosed is the depression.

The depressions are not graves. The depressions are not marked. The depressions are the shape of a body that sat down and did not get up, and the body sat down and the ground took the body's hold and the body was not a body any more and the body's hold was the ground's hold and the ground did not have the body's hold before the body's hold came and the ground had the body's hold after the body's hold came and the ground was the ground.

I have counted the depressions. There are more than I could count in a day. The rows of trees run north and the depressions run in rows and the rows are the only thing in the Ash Orchard that has been done on purpose, and the on-purpose is the only thing that says the Yielding is a decision and not a happening, and the happening is what the Cull says the Yielding is and the Cull is not entirely wrong.

The Ash Orchard is older than the Hold. The Ash Orchard is older than the Mourne being a fire-mountain. The Ash Orchard is the place the Yielding has been being yielded for as long as the Vale has been the Vale.

·  ·  ·

Sila Grey stood at the edge of the Orchard on the morning of the one hundred and third and she said what the Yielding's case was, and the case was delivered by a Healer who had been at the Hearth for a season and who had decided anyway.

I am going to write the case down because the case is the strongest argument anyone in the Vale has ever made for anything and everyone hates it and I am going to write it down anyway.

She said: the rite is a machine that moves hold from the grip of the dead and into the grip of the living. The machine is the Burial. The machine is the Pyre. The machine is the Telling. The machine is the Monument. The machine is the Wake. Every machine moves hold. Every machine moves hold from the dead and into the living, and the moving is the conversion, and the conversion is the spending, and the spending is the Legacy, and the Legacy is the inheritance.

She said: the spending is a theft.

She said: the dead did not consent to the rite. The dead did not consent to the Legacy. The dead did not consent to the spending of their hold by the living. The dead had hold. The dead had the hold they had when they died. The hold was theirs. The hold was not the Hold's. The hold was not the legacy's. The hold was not the next wielder's purse. The hold was theirs.

She said: the rite is the Hold's way of taking what was theirs and making it ours. The Legacy is the Hold's way of keeping what was theirs. The Wake is the Hold's way of spending what was theirs on a cask in the yard. The Telling is the Hold's way of saying their names in a room they did not ask to be said in. The Monument is the Hold's way of putting a stone on ground they died on and calling the stone a Keeping.

She said: the Yielding is the right answer. The Yielding is the dead person's hold being given back to the ground that the hold came from. The Yielding is not a machine. The Yielding is a giving. The giving is early. The giving is unclosed. The giving is what the hold is owed, and the hold is owed because the hold was always the ground's.

She said: a Yielding person leaves no Echo. There is no grip in the field. There is no purse in the Echo. There is no Echo at the palisade. There is no Echo at the third growth and no Echo at the fifth growth and no Echo at the gate. The Yielding is the answer to the Echo and the Echo is the answer to the rite and the rite is the answer to the Yielding and the answer is no answer.

She said: nobody has to come out here with a maul for us. Nobody has to spend grief on our Keeping. Nobody has to spend a relic on a stone over us. Nobody has to hold a Telling for us. The ground is going to take it. The ground is owed it.

Colm Grey did not stop them.

I am going to write why.

He did not stop them because he could not stop them without killing them. He did not want to kill them. He did not want to kill them because killing seven people who had decided to walk north was the Cull's argument and not his, and the Cull's argument was not the wielder's argument and the wielder was not the Cull.

He did not stop them because the Yielding was the right answer to a question the Vale has not finished asking. He had heard Sila Grey make the case at the Hearth on the fifty-eighth evening. He had heard Bran Holt not answer it. He had heard Bran Holt say *I am not arguing with you, I am not settling it*. He had heard Bran Holt go to bed. He had not heard a single person in the Hold answer the case, and the case was right.

He did not stop them because the Hold was twenty-six people and the seven who had walked north were seven of the twenty-six and the seven had decided and the decision was the Yielding's decision and the Yielding was theirs.

He walked out to the Orchard on the morning of the one hundred and third. He did not have the Pike. He did not have the Maul. He did not have the Ashblade. He did not have a weapon. He stood at the edge of the Orchard and he looked at Sila Grey and Sila Grey looked at him and he did not say anything and Sila Grey did not say anything and the seven of them walked into the Orchard and sat down.

I watched from the path. I have been at the Orchard before and I was not going to be at the Orchard again. I watched from the path. The seven of them sat down between the rows. The depressions were not where they sat. The depressions were where other people had sat. The seven of them sat in new places. The ground took their holds. The seven of them were no longer seven of them. The seven of them were the ground.

This is the Cull's second unforgiven day.

The Cull has been right three times. The Cull has been wrong twice. The Cull is not going to be told it is wrong by a Teller who has held the graver for a hundred and four days. The Field-hand whose name is on a pane and whose name is in the board and whose name I am not going to put here changed her mind.

She was the seventh of the seven. She had walked to the Orchard and she had stood at the edge of the Orchard and she had not sat down. She had stood at the edge of the Orchard for the whole morning while the other six sat down, and she had walked back to the Hold at noon.

She came in the north gate and the north gate had nobody on it because the Warden was in the Orchard, and the Warden was the Warden who had come down off the wall, and the Warden was not coming back, and the wall was going to have a Warden on it for the next shift and the Warden for the next shift was going to be the Warden whose name is on the board.

She walked to the Hearth. She sat down at the table. She did not speak for a long time. She was grey from the knees down and her boots were full of ash and the ash was the ash of the Orchard and the ash was not the ash of the Hold and the ash had a smell that the Hold's ash did not have, and the smell was the smell of the ground taking a hold back, and the smell was what the Hold's ash would smell like if the Hold's ash was the ash the ground took the hold back with.

I asked her what had changed her mind. She did not answer. I asked her if she had decided not to sit down. She did not answer. I asked her if she had decided to sit down again. She did not answer. She sat at the table and she did not speak and she did not move for a long time.

I am going to write what the Hold did with her, which is nothing.

There is no verb for coming back. There is no verb for changing one's mind about the Yielding. There is no verb for sitting down at a table in a Hearth after having walked to the Orchard and not having sat down. There is no verb for being the seventh of seven who did not sit down and who walked home and who is now at the table and whose name is on a pane.

There is no verb.

The Hold did nothing with her. She sat at the table. She had dinner. She went to the byre. She went to the byre for the night. She went to the field in the morning. She went to the field in the morning because the field needed her. The Hold did not vote on her. The Hold did not read the board about her. The board did not move about her.

She is alive in the Hold today. She is in the kitchen. She is forty-eight. She has not walked north since.

I am Asha Vane and I am the third hand and I have written the chapter about seven people walking north and one of them coming back and the other six sitting down and the ground taking their holds and the board moving by eight on three axes, and the chapter has not said what the Hold should have done, and the chapter is not going to say what the Hold should have done, because what the Hold should have done was nothing, and the Hold did nothing, and the nothing was right.

The Ash Orchard is north of the Grey Ash. The Ash Orchard has more depressions than I could count in a day. The Ash Orchard has six more depressions than it had on the morning of the one hundred and second.
