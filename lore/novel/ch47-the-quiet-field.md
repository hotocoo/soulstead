# Chapter 47: The Quiet Field

> *No triumph, no collapse. The work continued. Someone will pick up the blade tomorrow.*
> Recorded: the fifth line, fourth leaf, of an unsigned fragment

**Hand:** unsigned, unplaced, unconverted
**Days:** not convertible
**Place:** a hold whose name was never cut anywhere I have been able to find

Seven leaves, folded twice, used as packing.

A Wayfarer brought me the third part of the Verren copy wrapped against the wet, and the
wrapping was somebody's Chronicle. She did not know whose. She had it from another
Wayfarer who had it from a hold on the north road that used it for the same purpose and had
found it already folded.

I asked her whether there had been more of it. She said there had been more of it once and
that people had used it, because vellum is vellum and it rains.

·  ·  ·

I cannot place this hold and I have tried for eleven years.

The reason I cannot place it is in the document itself and it is the single most important
thing about it, and it took me four of those eleven years to understand that it was a
reason and not an obstacle.

**This Teller did not count board-days.** There is not one board-day anywhere in the seven
leaves. Every entry is dated by the season and the day of the season, in the way a
Field-hand talks and in the way no Teller in any valley I have copied from has ever
written.

*Second spring, ninth day.* *First frost, third day.* *Barley down, fourth day.*

A board-day is a day the world moved, and the world moves only while somebody is outside a
fence, and every Chronicle in every hold I know of counts them because the board counts
them and the board is what a Teller keeps. Which means every Chronicle in every valley can
be laid against every other one: their fourth hundredth is somewhere near our two
hundredth, and a Wayfarer who knows a route can line two boards up and tell you which
death came first.

Not this one. This one is dated to weather.

Two holds can share a season. They cannot share a spring, because a spring in a valley two
ridges over is not the same length as a spring here, and nobody has ever kept a record of
how long any of them were.

So there is no line in these seven leaves that can be matched against any line in any other
copy. Nothing in it can be cross-read. I have laid it against eleven boards and it touches
none of them.

Whoever kept this one was not writing for a Wayfarer. They were writing for the people in
the room.

·  ·  ·

## The fragment, whole, in the letters it was cut in

**Leaf one.** Top third gone to the fold. What is left:

```
    ...ond spring, ninth day.
    Oda Kettle to the mere road, with Bel Rowan. Back by dark.
    Nothing out there. Rows clean.
    Second spring, eleventh day. Ketil Grey at the lambing all
    night, lost two, kept nine.
    Second spring, fourteenth day. Bel Rowan out past the fence
    to the east stand. Put down a Stalker. Mrc -1 Dom +2.
    Second spring, fifteenth day. Rain. Nothing entered.
```

*Rain. Nothing entered.*

Bran Holt would have entered nothing on a day like that and would not have said so. This
hand says so. There are eleven days in these seven leaves where the entry is that there was
no entry, and that is the first thing I liked about it and I liked it before I understood
anything else.

**Leaf two.** A death and two rites.

```
    Second summer, second day. Sila Grey died in the night.
    Seventy-one. Six bonds. Grief 28.
    Second summer, fourth day. Burial, whole, with her spindle
    and the good knife. Mem +8 Mrc +4 Com +5.
    +6 of health to whoever carries the blade after, and to
    everyone after them, for as long as there is anybody.
    Second summer, sixth day. Telling for Sila Grey, given by
    Oda Kettle who sat with her. Mem +8 Mrc +4 Com +5.
    Told it wrong in one place. Ketil Grey corrected it and I
    have put both in.
```

An old woman died in her bed at seventy-one and was buried with her spindle and had her
death told aloud by the person who sat with her, and the telling had a mistake in it, and
the mistake is in the book, and so is the correction.

That is a hold I have never been to, doing the two things I have spent seventy days
insisting are the only things that matter, and doing them without anybody watching, for an
old woman who had six bonds and no wielders among them.

**Leaf three.** The bad season.

```
    Second autumn, first day. East stand gone to Turning.
    Foragers say pale at the root the whole length.
    Second autumn, ninth day. Working party, eleven of us, four
    days. Lye and bone into the stand. Two relic-stones set.
    Second autumn, thirteenth day. Root colour back.
    Pushed the decay back from the east stand.
    Mem +4 Mrc +7 Com +6 Dom +2.
    Second autumn, twenty-second day. Barley short. Half of
    last year.
    Second autumn, twenty-ninth day. Bran Kettle to the north
    road to ask for seed. Refused at two holds. Given at the
    third. Would not say which.
    First frost, second day. Nix Grey born.
    There is no verb. Entered in words. Nine pounds.
```

Eleven people, four days, two relic-stones, and a stand of trees that was going and then
was not.

Our Hold did the same thing to Brackwood and it took twenty-two of us eleven days and I
have called it the best single entry this Hold ever wrote. Theirs took eleven of them four
days and nobody outside that valley has ever known it happened, and the leaf it is on was
used to keep rain off somebody else's book.

*There is no verb. Entered in words. Nine pounds.*

**Leaf four.** The wielder, and the fifth line.

This is the leaf with the ending on it and I am going to give the whole of it and then
stop.

```
    First frost, eleventh day. Bel Rowan has had the blade a
    hundred and forty days by the board and eleven years by the
    weather. She is forty-four. She says her left hand is slow
    and she has said it twice now, and the second time she said
    it to me and not to the Wardens, which I take to mean she
    wants it written.
    First frost, thirteenth day. Called three names.
    Bel Rowan. Oda Kettle. Ketil Grey.
    Bel Rowan declined her own name, which is permitted and
    which I have never seen.
    First frost, fourteenth day. Oda Kettle took up the blade.
    Com +2 Dom +1.
    Bel Rowan handed it to her in the yard with everybody there.
    There is no verb for this. The board will not carry it.
    I have asked twice.
    I have entered the Succession, which is the nearest thing
    the board has, and it is not the same, and I want whoever
    reads this to know that it is not the same.
    Nobody died.
    First frost, fifteenth day. Bel Rowan to the lambing shed.
    Ordinary work, ordinary rota.
    First frost, nineteenth day.
    No triumph, no collapse. The work continued.
    Someone will pick up the blade tomorrow.
```

That last part is not a board line. The fifth line of a board is cut on the wall, in the
Hearth, under four numbers, and nobody has ever been able to account for how it gets there.

This is in the hand. In the middle of a leaf. In the middle of an ordinary week, four days
after a woman with a slow left hand gave a sword to somebody in a yard and went to work in a
shed.

I have handled the leaf under a lamp. It is the same letters, the same depth, the same
breath as the lambing entry above it. It was not added later and it was not added by anyone
else.

Either that Teller wrote down the fifth line of their own board because they had gone in and
read it, in which case their board said it while everybody in the hold was alive and well
and nothing had been resolved.

Or they wrote it because it was true and they had noticed, and the board on the wall said
the same thing afterwards because a board says what is true.

I have no way to test it. There is no one to ask. I record what was said.

·  ·  ·

**Leaves five and six.** Two more deaths and the one that was left.

```
    Deep frost, eighth day. Ketil Grey fell at the east ditch.
    Fifty-four. Nine bonds. Grief 37. Mem +2 Dom -3.
    Deep frost, ninth day. Oda Kettle went out and fetched him.
    Reclaimed the Echo of Ketil Grey.
    Mem +6 Mrc +3 Com +1 Dom +2.
    Deep frost, eleventh day. Monument over him at the ditch,
    relic under it. Mem +8 Mrc +4 Com +5.
    Spent a relic on stone that will outlast you.
    Mem +5 Com +3 Dom +1.
    Thaw, fourth day. Bran Kettle fell in the west rows.
    Thirty-one. Four bonds. Grief 22.
    Thaw, sixth day. Pool at 12. Cannot pay for a Burial.
    Thaw, ninth day. Pool at 12.
    Thaw, fourteenth day. Left the Echo of Bran Kettle to grow.
    Mem -5 Mrc -3 Com -2 Dom -1.
    I have entered it and I want it entered plainly. We did not
    leave him because we chose to. We left him because a Burial
    is twenty and we had twelve, and the only way to have twenty
    is for somebody else to die.
    Thaw, twenty-second day. Pool at 12. Rows clean. Nothing out
    there but him.
```

There it is. That is the whole of it, in six lines, in a hand nobody can name, about a
thirty-one-year-old in the west rows.

A Burial costs twenty of grief. Grief is ten and three for every bond the dead had. Bran
Kettle had four bonds, so he was worth twenty-two, which would have paid for his own
Burial with two to spare.

Except that the pool stood at twelve, because they had spent everything they had on Ketil
Grey a season earlier: a Burial, no, a Monument at forty, and a relic on top of it.

They buried a man properly in the deep frost, and the price of it was that they could not
bury the next one in the thaw.

Nobody in the Vale of Ashfall has ever had to write that sentence, because this Hold has
never once been short at the moment it mattered. We were short on the hundred and
twenty-eighth day and we were short for a different reason: we had a wielder who was a
stranger and no law requiring him. We had the grief. We did not spend it.

They had the will and not the grief. We had the grief and not the will. Both boards took
five off Memory and three off Mercy and two off Communion and one off Dominion, in exactly
the same letters, and there is no verb anywhere in the eighteen that tells the two apart.

**Leaf seven.** Ordinary work, and the tear.

```
    Thaw, twenty-eighth day. Lambing done. Nineteen kept.
    First spring, second day. Oda Kettle to the mere road. Back
    by dark. Nothing out there.
    First spring, third day. Bel Rowan's hand no better. She has
    the rota and the tally now and does it well.
    First spring, fourth day. Seed in. Everybody out. Nothing
    entered, becaus
```

The tear runs across the corner and takes the rest.

·  ·  ·

Half a page of mine, and it is the last half page I will write about somebody else's hold.

I have spent eleven years trying to find out whose this is. I have asked every Wayfarer who
has come to our post. I have sent a description of the hand out along four roads. I have
laid these seven leaves against boards from eleven holds and they touch none of them,
because this Teller counted weather.

There is no name of a hold anywhere in the fragment. There is no name of a valley. The
names in it are the names everybody has: Oda, Bel, Ketil, Sila, Bran, Nix, and the family
names are Kettle and Grey and Rowan and they are on walls in every direction.

Eighteen given names and twelve family names in this world, and we have used them all, over
and over, until a name is not an address any more. I have written about that in the
appendix and it is the only place in this book where I have let myself be bitter.

What I know about them is this. They kept an old woman's Telling and put the mistake in.
They pushed a stand of trees back from Turning with eleven people and four days. They
begged seed off three holds and would not shame the two who refused. They could not afford
to bury a man of thirty-one and they wrote down exactly why. A woman handed a sword to
somebody in a yard and went to work in a shed, and the board had no verb for the best thing
that hold ever did.

And they will not be remembered. Not by me, because I cannot find them, and not by anybody
else, because the rest of their book kept the rain off.

So I did the only thing available.

I have copied all seven leaves into this Chronicle in full, on the seventieth leaf, in my
own hand, with the tear marked and the fold marked and the missing top third marked as
missing. It is now in two places instead of one. If this Hearth burns, there is a copy on
the other shelf, and a Wayfarer is carrying a third.

That is not a rite. There is no rite for a hold with no name and I have looked and the
appendix has the list of what I tried.

It is the nearest thing to a Keeping that a Teller can perform on strangers: I took what
was unspent and I gave it to somebody else to carry.

Their ending is the commonest ending there has ever been. Most holds get it. Ours nearly
got it eleven times in a hundred and fifty-seven days, and every one of those times we were
one body lying out in a field away from it.

There is no monument for it. There was never going to be.

No triumph, no collapse. The work continued.
