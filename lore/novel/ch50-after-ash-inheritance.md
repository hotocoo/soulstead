# Chapter 50: After Ash Inheritance

> *Two accounts of the last page of the Chronicle. They differ in three particulars.*
> Recorded: Grieve Mourne, fourth hand, appending

**Hand:** disputed · **Place:** Verren Hold, and the Hearth

I am Grieve Mourne. I am sixty-five. I have been compiling for thirty-three years. I have the appendices in front of me. I am at the Hearth. The Hearth is the Hearth of Ashfall Hold and the Hearth is what the Hearth has been since I was twelve.

I am writing this chapter in the Hearth because the chapter is about the Verren Hold copy and the copy is on the table in front of me. The copy is four boards and the boards are stained and the stain is not water and the stain is not ash and I am not going to write down what the stain is.

The boards were sent east by a Wayfarer whose name is not on the leaf that came with them. The boards were carried from valley to valley for nine years. The boards were read in eleven valleys. The boards are read in this valley and the boards are on the table and the boards are what the boards are.

I have read the boards nine times. The ninth was after the Wake. The ninth was after the Wake because the Wake is what the Wake is and the Wake is the ending that has every Echo reclaimed and the Wake is the ending the Verren Hold did not get and the Verren Hold did not get the Wake because the Verren Hold went the way the Verren Hold went.

The way the Verren Hold went is the way I am writing this chapter about.

·  ·  ·

The two wrong details from chapter fourteen.

I am going to write two sentences that Bran Holt wrote down and that I have been trying to find for thirty-three years and that have just appeared on the boards in front of me.

The first sentence Bran Holt recorded: *the cup was on the table from breakfast.* The second: *Rin Ashfall went past the fence facing south.*

The first sentence in the Verren Hold copy, on the board dated day 240, in a hand I do not know and the hand is the hand of the Teller who was nobody and the Teller was on day 240 and the day 240 was the day the boards say the hand changed. The first sentence in the Verren copy is: *the cup was on the table from breakfast.* The first sentence is the first sentence Bran Holt recorded and the first sentence is the first sentence the Verren Hold recorded and the first sentence is the same sentence and the sentence is in two books and the two books are two books.

The second sentence in the Verren Hold copy, on the same board, in the same hand, on the same day: *Rin Ashfall went past the fence facing south.* The second sentence is the second sentence Bran Holt recorded and the second sentence is the second sentence the Verren Hold recorded and the second sentence is the same sentence and the sentence is in two books.

I have read this. I have written this. I am writing this in pencil because I am out of ink and the pencil is a piece of lead from a Wayfarer's bark and the lead is grey and my hand is grey and the writing is hard to read.

I have been looking for these sentences for thirty-three years. I am not going to find them any more. I have found them. The finding is the finding and the finding is in this chapter.

·  ·  ·

What I am going to do about it.

I am going to cut my own name into a pane while I am alive.

This has not been done. No Teller in any valley I have a copy of has cut their own name into a pane while alive. The pane is for the dead. The pane is the pane the dead is cut into. The pane is the glass and the glass is fluxed with the pearl of a pyre and the pyre is a body and the body is the body the body is.

I am going to cut my own name into a pane and the pane is going to be on the Memorial and the pane is going to be the pane of a Teller who was alive when the pane was cut. The pane is going to be a piece of the Memorial and the Memorial is going to have the pane and the pane is going to be a pane and the pane is going to be on the Memorial until somebody takes the pane down or until the pane breaks or until the pane is the pane the pane is.

I am doing this because the Verren copy's convergence of hands is described there as beginning with exactly this symptom. The convergence in the Verren copy begins on their day 203, with their wielder Nix Verren dying, with the entries from that day on being signed *Kept by N. Verren*, and the hand that signs is the hand of the next thirty-seven entries, and the hand does not come back.

I am doing this because the two wrong details are on the boards and the boards are in front of me. I am doing this because the boards are the boards and the boards are what the boards are and the boards are not in my Chronicle and the boards are on the boards in front of me and the boards are mine because the boards are on the table in front of me.

I am doing this because I am a Teller and the Teller is the Teller and the Teller is the one who cuts the line and the line is the line and the line is the line the Teller cuts.

·  ·  ·

The pane.

I cut the pane on the morning of a day I am going to give the day because the giving of the day is the giving of the day.

I cut the pane in the Smithy, with the graver, with the help of Bel Mourne, who is my niece and who is the Teller after me. Bel Mourne held the pane. I cut the pane. The pane is in the eastern range at the Memorial and the pane says *Grieve Mourne* and the pane is glass and the glass has no pearl because the pearl is for the pyre and the pyre is for the dead and the dead is not me.

The pane is glass without pearl. The glass is clear. The name is cut into the glass and the name is in my hand and the hand is the hand and the hand is the hand I have been writing with for thirty-three years.

The pane is the pane of a Teller who was alive when the pane was cut. The pane is the pane. The pane is what the pane is.

The pane is going to be on the Memorial until somebody takes the pane down or until the pane breaks or until the pane is the pane the pane is.

·  ·  ·

I am going to write two accounts of the last page of the Chronicle of Ashfall Hold and the two accounts are going to differ in three particulars.

The first account is mine.

The first account says: I cut my own name into the pane on a morning in the year after the Wake. The first account says: I wrote this chapter in pencil on a piece of vellum I have not shown anyone. The first account says: the Verren Hold boards are on the table in front of me and the boards are stained and the boards are what the boards are. The first account says: the pane is in the eastern range and the pane says *Grieve Mourne* and the pane is glass and the glass is clear and the name is in my hand and the hand is mine.

The second account is the second account.

The second account says: Grieve Mourne did not cut his own name into the pane. The second account says: Grieve Mourne wrote this chapter in pencil on a piece of vellum and the vellum was found in the Hearth on the morning after his death. The second account says: the pane was cut by Bel Mourne on the morning after his death and the pane was cut in his hand because the hand Bel Mourne cuts in is the hand Grieve Mourne cut in. The second account says: the boards on the table in front of Grieve Mourne were not the Verren boards and the boards were boards from a hold whose name is on the boards. The second account says: the panes that were cut were not the panes that Grieve Mourne said were cut.

The two accounts differ in three particulars: who cut the pane, when the pane was cut, and what boards were on the table.

I am writing both accounts. I am not settling which account is right.

·  ·  ·

The Vale's whole metaphysics arrives at its worst implication and nobody explains it.

The implication is that a Chronicle kept long enough is kept by the Chronicle. The Chronicle keeps the Teller. The Chronicle is the Chronicle and the Teller is the Teller and the Teller is what the Chronicle says the Teller is. The Chronicle keeps the Teller because the Chronicle has the Teller's hand and the hand is the hand and the hand is what the Chronicle says the hand is.

The implication is that the four hands of the Chronicle of Ashfall Hold are not four hands. The implication is that the four hands are one hand. The one hand is the hand that has been cutting the lines since day one. The one hand is the hand that is going to keep cutting the lines until the lines are the lines the lines are.

The implication is that Sila Carver's hand and Bran Holt's hand and Asha Vane's hand and my hand are the same hand. The hand is the hand. The hand is what the hand is.

The implication is that the Unnaming is right. The Unnaming said: the panes are a calling and the calling is what is keeping the Echoes standing. The implication is that the Unnaming has been right since the twenty-second day of the Hold and the rightness has been the rightness and the rightness is what the rightness is.

I am not going to settle this. I am going to write this chapter and the chapter is going to be on the page and the page is going to be the page and the page is going to be the page the page is.

I am Grieve Mourne. I am sixty-five. I have been compiling for thirty-three years. I am going to put the graver down. I am going to put the graver down on the table at the Hearth and the graver is going to be where the graver is going to be and the table is going to be the table and the Hearth is going to be the Hearth.

The pane is in the eastern range. The pane says *Grieve Mourne*. The pane is glass and the glass is clear. The name is in my hand and the hand is the hand and the hand is mine.

I am going to put the graver down.
