# Chapter 24: The Shaved Panes

> *Eleven panes, smeared. One boy who looks at the Memorial from the wrong angle.*
> Recorded: the smear in every range, east and west, found on the morning of the fifty-fifth

**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 55 to 58 · **Place:** the Memorial

Grieve Mourne found the smears on the morning of the fifty-fifth.

He is nine years old. He is the son of Sila Mourne, who is a Field-hand, and his father is a name I do not have on the pane and have never asked about because the pane is not my business and the Teller's rule about asking extends to the names of people's dead. He is small for his age and he has a habit of being in places he is not supposed to be, which is a habit that has not yet been broken out of him and which I am not going to be the one to break out of him, because the habit is the reason he found the smears.

He was on the south side of the Memorial at first light, looking at the panes from below. The panes are set into the wall at shoulder height for a person of working height, and a person of working height looks at the panes straight on, and straight on the smears do not show, and the smears were polished the way the smear on Bel Brack's pane was polished, with a wet cloth and a little ash, and the polish catches the light from below only when the light is at the right angle, and the right angle is at first light, and first light is when a boy who has slept on the south side of the Memorial because he was not supposed to be there is the only person at the panes.

He came to the Hearth at first light. He had not had breakfast. He had counted the smears.

Eleven of them. Eleven panes, smeared, in every range, in the east and in the west, in the pane for Bel Brack which had already been smeared and which had been smeared again, and in the panes for the dead the Hold has buried since.

I went with him to the Memorial. I brought the lamp and a chair. I stood on the chair and looked down at each pane in turn and the smears were there, on the inside of the glass, polished the way glass is polished, low and to the left of the name where the letter would have been cut, and on the panes with longer names there was more than one smear and the smears were in the same place on each pane and the place was where the first letter would have been cut.

I did not touch the panes. I sent Grieve to the byre for a Healer and I sent the Healer to the door and I stood at the door and I counted who had been at the Memorial in the night.

·  ·  ·

The investigation was not a mystery plot. A mystery plot is a thing for hands that have time to make a thing interesting. The investigation was a Hold of twenty-six people counting who was awake.

The Hold slept. The children slept. The Healers slept because the Healers were on for the day and the day was going to be a long one. The Wardens slept two at a time in the byre loft and the third was on the wall. The Wardens' names are on the board and the names are not the names I am looking for because a Warden on the wall is awake.

The people who were awake were the people who were working at night, and the people who were working at night were the people the Hold had work for at night, and the work at night is the kiln and the kiln shifts and the kiln is at the south of the Hold and the kiln smoke does not reach the panes.

There were five people at the kiln on the night of the fifty-fourth into the fifty-fifth. Their names are on the board. I have them. I am not going to put them here because the names are not on the page yet and the page will not be the page until the page has the names, and the names are about to be on the page in a chapter that is not this one, and the chapter that is not this one will be the chapter that decides what to do about the smears.

I went to the kiln. The kiln had been going all night. The shell was in. The ash was coming out. The five people at the kiln had been at the kiln because the kiln does not stop and the kiln shifts are twelve hours and the night shift is the cold shift and the cold shift is the shift that people take when they do not want to be at the Hearth.

I asked who had been at the Memorial. I got no answers and no denials and a Carver who was covered in shavings and who had been cutting a stave for a fork in the workshop all night and had not been at the Memorial.

I went back to the Memorial. I stood on the chair. I looked at the smears. I looked at them for a long time.

·  ·  ·

What the shaving does, measurably, is nothing to the Echoes.

This is the finding and the finding is the whole reason this chapter is not the mystery plot the Hold would have liked it to be.

The two smears that mattered were on the panes for wielders' kin. Wielders' kin are people whose bonds to a dead wielder are close enough that the dead wielder's Echo is in a region the Echo has not left, and the Echoes of wielders' kin have to do with the Echoes of wielders in a way that has not been measured.

The first smear was on the pane for Yeva Holt's father. Yeva Holt was not a wielder yet. Yeva Holt would be a wielder in fifty-six days. The pane was cut the day Yeva Holt's father was buried, and the pane was smeared on the night of the fifty-fourth, and the pane was smeared at the F. The grave is in the eastern range, in the row, near the row I walk at the turn of the season. The grave is quiet. The pale root at the head of the grave is not spreading. The grave has been quiet since the day the grave was dug and the grave is going to stay quiet, and the pane is not on the grave, and the Echo is in Brackwood, and the Echo is at the third growth, and the Echo is not paying attention to the pane.

The second smear was on the pane for Sila Kettle's mother. Sila Kettle is a child. Sila Kettle is five. The pane was cut the day Sila Kettle's mother was buried, and the pane was smeared on the night of the fifty-fourth, and the pane was smeared at the S. The grave is in the eastern range, in the row, two graves to the east of Yeva Holt's father. The pale root at the head of this grave is spreading. The pale root has been spreading since the smear. The pale root is at the rim of the grave and the rim of the grave is the rim of the ring and the ring has not formed yet and the Echo is not standing in the ring and the ring is not a ring yet and the spreading is a piece of work that is going to need a body in it in three days if it is not stopped.

Two smears. Two panes for wielders' kin. One grave quiet. One grave spreading. Both facts are recorded and neither fact explains the other fact.

I cut a Telling for the spreading grave on the fifty-seventh day. The Telling was not held. The Telling was a thing I cut on the board because the board needed a Telling and the Telling was the only verb that fit, and the verb was not held by the Hold because the Hold does not vote on Tellings and the Teller cuts the verb and the Tellings are held for who they are held for and the Tellings are not held for who they would be held for.

I have not written that last sentence correctly. I have written it as it happened.

·  ·  ·

Grieve Mourne has not been back to the Memorial since the morning of the fifty-fifth.

I have asked him to. He has not gone. He has been at the Hearth, reading the board aloud to himself. He reads the board aloud to himself the way other boys his age read aloud from a book, and the board is the book and the reading is the reading, and the boy is going to be on this hand one day and the boy is going to know the board by heart by the time he is on the hand.

He is nine years old. He is the only person in the Hold who looked at the Memorial from the wrong angle.

He is the only appearance of Grieve Mourne in this book before Chapter 41.

I am putting that on the page because the page is where it belongs and the page is the only place in this book where it can be said that a nine-year-old boy found eleven smears by lying down under a pane at first light and looking up at it, and the boy did not understand what he had found and the boy has not been back, and the boy will be on this hand one day and the boy will read this page and the boy will know what he found and the boy will not go back either.

·  ·  ·

The person who did the smears came to the Hearth on the evening of the fifty-eighth.

It was Sila Grey.

She was at the kiln on the night of the fifty-fourth. She was at the kiln on the night of the fifty-fifth. She was at the kiln on the night of the fifty-sixth. She had been at the kiln every night for a week. She came to the Hearth on the evening of the fifty-eighth and she said that the smears were hers and she did not say it like a confession. She said it like a person who had been holding a thing for a long time and had decided to set it down.

I want the sentence she said recorded.

She said: I did them because I believe the panes are a calling and I believe the calling is what is keeping the Echoes standing and I have done what I have done and I will say what I believe and the Hold will do what the Hold will do.

She did not raise her voice. She did not look at the door. She looked at the table and she said what she said and she sat down.

I am going to spend the rest of this chapter on what she said because what she said is the second half of the argument and the second half is the half that is harder to argue with.

She said: I have done this for a year. I have done it eleven times. I have done it on the panes of people whose graves are quiet and on the panes of people whose graves are not quiet and I have done it on the pane of a person whose grave is spreading and the spreading started after the smear and the spreading is not because of the smear.

She said: I do not know whether the spreading is because of the smear. I do not know whether the quiet is because of the smear. I do not know what the panes are doing. I know what I have done and I know why I have done it and I know the law and I am not asking for the law to be unmade.

She said: the calling is what is keeping the Echoes standing. The Echo grows because it is being called. Stop calling and the grip opens with nobody having to walk out into a field with a maul. The pane is a voice that calls a name. The name is what keeps the hold closed. If the pane stops calling the name, the grip opens on its own.

She said: the Holden graveyards are quiet because the graveyards are full and the Echoes that are not in the graveyards are in the fields because the fields have not been kept, and the keeping of the fields is what closes the Echo's grip on the ground and the closing is what the Echo feeds and the feeding is what the Echo is. The Echo is what the Echo feeds.

She said: I have been at the kiln at midnight for a year because the kiln is the only place I can think. I have done what I have done. I will say what I believe.

The room was quiet.

The room was quiet for a long time.

I am Bran Holt and I am the second hand and I am going to bed.

·  ·  ·

The other roads out of this chapter fork in the chapter after this one and not this one.

There are no Other Roads.

---

Grieve Mourne, compiling. I was nine years old and I lay down under the panes at first light and the smears were there and I have not been back.

I am thirty-two. I am at the Memorial every day. I cut the panes myself. I do not lie down under them anymore because there is no smear to find.

Sila Grey is in the Hold. She is sixty-one. She is at the kiln at midnight more nights than she is not. I have asked her why and she has told me and I have not put her answer in this book.

She told Bran Holt once that she told me about Maren Verren and the doorstone. She told me the same thing twenty years later in the same words. I am not going to write the words.

I am going to write that the words were the same words.

The other roads fork in the next chapter and not this one, and the next chapter is the one where the Hold decides what to do about a person who believed the right thing hard enough to break the law about it, and the chapter is going to be the chapter where the Tellers and the Hearthkeepers and the Cull and the Unnaming are all of them correct and all of them wrong and the chapter is going to end with a Telling and a re-cut and a person who has been right for a year being told she has been wrong by the Hold she has been right in.
