# Chapter 27: The Stranger's Echo

> *It carried a name the Hold did not have. The name was somebody.*
> Recorded: Sila Verren, walked in out of the west on the morning of the sixty-eighth

**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 68 to 70 · **Place:** Wrenfield, the Memorial

It walked in out of the west at first light on the sixty-eighth.

I want to be exact about this because what walked in out of the west is the thing the Hold has not yet seen and the thing the Hold has been arguing about for a season. The Echoes of the Hold are Hold Echoes. They are people we buried or people we failed to bury and they are in the Hold's fields or in the Hold's graveyards and they carry the Hold's gear and they fight with the Hold's weapons. They are ours.

The Echo that walked in out of the west on the morning of the sixty-eighth was not ours.

It was a Cinder Stalker build. It was small and fast and low-packed and it had been built up the way Echoes build up, by feeding on the ground it walked through, but the ground it had walked through was not the Hold's ground. The Echo was carrying a sword on its back and the sword was not the Ashblade and the sword was not the Widow's Maul and the sword was a longer sword than either and the sword was bound with leather that was not Hold leather. The Echo was carrying a flask at its belt and the flask was empty. The Echo was carrying a pack on its back and the pack was buckled shut and the buckles were not Hold buckles.

It walked in past the long grass and through the last fence and stood at the rim of the ring the Hold's Echoes had bent the grass into on the nineteenth day when Maren had brought Rin home, and the ring was grass again because grass grows back, and the stranger stood in the grass and did not move.

The stranger was at the second growth. The stranger was not at the third. The stranger had not left its death site.

The stranger's death site was not in the Vale.

·  ·  ·

This is what drift is.

An Echo grows on the ground where it died. The ground feeds it. The Echo feeds on the ground. The Echo gets bigger and the ground gets worse and the worse the ground gets the more the Echo has to feed and the more the Echo has to feed the bigger the Echo gets. The Echo does not leave its death site until the third growth, and the third growth takes more feeding than the ground around the death site has, and the Echo has to walk to find more ground, and the walking is the leaving, and the leaving is the roaming.

A roaming Echo is looking for food. The food is the hold of the ground. The ground it has fed on is Grey. The ground it walks through is Grey on the side it walked. A roaming Echo at the third growth walks a country out, one decay state per region crossed, and a roaming Echo at the fourth growth crosses valleys.

A roaming Echo that has crossed a valley is drifting. The drifting Echo carries its owner's name. The drifting Echo carries its owner's gear. The drifting Echo carries its owner's skill and the skill is at the level the owner died at and the level is being added to by every ground the Echo has walked through since.

The drifting Echo does not know it is drifting. The drifting Echo is a person's grip, still closed, with nobody inside it, walking because the grip is the walking. The grip is looking for ground that will feed it and the ground that will feed it is any ground with hold in it, and the ground with hold in it is any ground that has not been fed on yet.

The stranger walked in out of the west and the ground to the west of the Hold had been fed on twice in two seasons and the stranger had walked through it on the way in and the ground on the way in was Grey and the stranger had not fed on it because the stranger was not hungry. The stranger was not hungry because the stranger had been fed. The stranger had been fed on ground that was not the Hold's ground, on ground two days' walk west, on ground in a valley the Hold has never been to, on ground in a valley the Wayfarers have copies of.

The name on the stranger's belt was Sila Verren.

I read it three times because I have learned that names on strangers are read three times. The first read is wrong because the hand is not the hand. The second read is wrong because the eye is not the eye. The third read is right because the third read is the only one I trust. The third read said *Sila Verren* and the third read was right and the name was somebody.

·  ·  ·

The Hold argued for two days about what to do with the stranger.

I want the argument described properly because the argument is the argument the Hold has been having about every Echo that has not been ours since the Hold was founded, which is no Echoes, because the Hold has not had a stranger's Echo before, and the argument is the first argument of its kind and the last argument of its kind for a long time.

The Tellers argued to bury it. The Tellers said: a pane in the Memorial is a pane in the Memorial and a pane is a pane and the name on the pane is the name the pane is holding and the holding is what the pane is for. The Tellers said: the Carvers have nine panes of backlog and a backlog is not a reason to deny a name a pane and the backlog is a reason to cut more panes and the cutting is what the Carvers are for.

The Cull argued to put it down at the fence. The Cull said: a thing that carries hold is a thing that carries hold and the carrying is contagion and the contagion is what the Cull is for and the Cull does not care whose name is on the belt.

The Hearthkeepers argued to bury it. The Hearthkeepers said: a Husk is a person who was failed and a stranger's Echo is a stranger's person who was failed and the failing is the same and the rite is the same and the rite is owed.

The Long Hold argued to bar the gate. The Long Hold said: the thing is what the Long Hold is for.

The Unnaming argued to send it on. The Unnaming said: the pane is a calling and the calling is what keeps the Echo standing and the Echo is standing because the Echo is being called and the Echo is not being called by this Hold and the Echo should be called by its own Hold and the sending is the right thing and the sending is what the Open Road is for.

The Open Road argued to read the names of every settlement the Wayfarers had a copy of and to wait for the name that matched.

Maren Verren argued nothing.

·  ·  ·

On the third day she took the Widow's Maul and the spade and a flask and she walked out to where the stranger was standing, and the stranger was at the rim of the old ring and the stranger was at the second growth and the stranger was not moving.

She did not fight it. She did not have to fight it because the stranger was at the second growth and the second growth is not the third growth and the second growth does not leave the death site and the death site was not the Hold's death site and the stranger did not know the Hold's name and the stranger did not know the Hold's gear and the stranger was standing in the grass looking at the long grass the way a person looks at a thing they have been walking past for a long time and have decided to stop in front of.

She dug a hole next to the stranger. The hole was deep. The hole was wide enough for a body and a sword and a flask and a pack and a name on a belt. The stranger did not move while she dug. The stranger was at the second growth and the second growth is what the Echoes do before they leave, and the leaving had not happened, and the Echo was the grip and the grip was the walking and the grip was not going to fight a woman with a spade because the woman with a spade was not the grip's reason for being in the grass.

She put it down. The grip went slack. The grief came out of the stranger's purse all at once, which is what happens when an Echo is reclaimed, and the grief came out into the Hold on the walk back, and the grief was forty-eight of grief and the grief was a stranger's grief and the grief spent on nobody the Hold had lost and the grief sat in the pool and the pool rose and the Hold worked at a fraction for the rest of the week.

The stranger's sword was not bound for the Smithy. The stranger's flask was empty. The stranger's pack was buckled shut. The stranger's name was on the belt and the name was *Sila Verren* and the name was somebody the Hold did not have.

She carried the gear to the Memorial. She laid the gear on the floor. She asked the Carvers to cut a pane.

The Carvers said: we have nine panes of backlog.

She said: cut a pane.

They cut a pane. The pane was glass. The glass was fluxed with pearl from a stranger's pyre, which is a thing the Hold has never done, because the Hold did not have the pyre and the pyre was two days' walk west in a valley the Hold has never been to. The pearl was in the flask. The flask had been at the stranger's belt and the flask had been full of a stranger's water and the flask had been emptied and the flask had been refilled at the stranger's Hold with a stranger's ash and the ash had been steeped and the ash had been settled and the ash had been drawn off clear and the ash had been cut with hearth-water and the water had been the stranger's hearth-water and the hearth was two days' walk west.

The pane was cut. The name on the pane was *Sila Verren*. The name was cut in a hand that was not the Hold's hand and the hand was smaller than the Hold's hand and the cuts were finer and the cuts are in the pane and the pane is in the Memorial and the Memorial is in the Hold and the Hold has a stranger's name on a stranger's pane in a stranger's pearl.

The board moved. Six on Memory. Seven on Mercy. Nine on Communion. Minus one on Dominion.

The numbers are on the board and the numbers are the numbers of a stranger's Echo buried by a Hold that did not know the stranger and the numbers are the largest single movement on Mercy in any verb and the numbers are the entry that crossed a ridge before there were two valleys writing in each other's books.

The Wayfarers carried the entry west on the seventy-first day. The entry was in a bundle under the arm of a Wayfarer whose name I do not have on the page and whose hand I have on a leaf in the bundle. The entry reached a Hold two days' walk west on the seventy-fourth day. The Hold two days' walk west is Verren Hold. Verren Hold's board read what Verren Hold's board read on the seventy-fourth day, and the line on Verren Hold's board was *+6 +7 +9 -1* and *Sila Verren* and the line on Verren Hold's board is the line that came east on a leaf.

What crosses between holds is entries.

I want that sentence to be the sentence this whole chapter is built around.

What crosses between holds is entries. What crosses between holds is not the world. It is not the ground. It is not the people. It is the line, on the board, in the hand, on the leaf, in the bundle, under the arm, on the road, at the post, at the gate, at the table, in the next valley's Chronicle.

The stranger walked in out of the west and the stranger was buried in the Hold and the line on the board was cut in the Hold's hand and the line was carried to the stranger's Hold and the line was cut into the stranger's Hold's board, and the line is the only piece of Sila Verren that is in two valleys, and the line is the only piece of the Vale that is in Verren Hold, and the line is on the page in a Chronicle that has been kept for seventy days by a hand that was not raised to keep it.

Maren did not look at the pane after it was cut. She went back to the byre. She sat down on the bed of a sick person who is no longer sick and she did not move for a long time and she did not say what she had done and she did not say why she had done it.

I am Bran Holt and I am the second hand and I have buried a stranger's Echo in my Hold and the line on the board is the line that crossed a ridge and the line is what the Wayfarers carry and the line is what the Chronicle is for and I am going to bed.
