# Chapter 13: The Ledger Board

> *Four numbers, one sentence, no name on the line itself. The name is for the pane.*
> Recorded: the second hand, dated the twentieth, cut on the twenty-first

**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 20 to 21 · **Place:** the Hearth

Sila Carver died of nothing in particular nine days after she had cut the row that put Rin's name on the board, and the Chronicle passed to me on the morning of the twentieth because I was the only person in the Hold willing to take it. I have been told this was an accident. I have been told it was a kindness. I have been told it was because nobody else wanted to watch the wax. All three of those are true and none of them is the reason I have held the graver against my better judgement for the rest of this book. The reason is on the board and I will get to it.

I want the room described properly because the room is the document. A Hold of twenty-two working people, three of them children Bel Brack's age and too small to lift a spade, has a Hearth that is also a kitchen and also a counting house. The walls are stone for the lower two thirds and timber above, with the timber blackened at the lintel in a way that nobody has done anything about because it is the way the fire makes it. The roof is sound. The floor is flagged, with the flags laid by four Carvers on the thirty-first day on a job I will describe when I get to it. North wall, low, the only one with no door: a board of ash, planed, six feet long, two feet and a hand high, set into the stone at table height so a person can read it standing.

That is the board.

It is not a beautiful object. Sila and I planed it on the afternoon of the twentieth out of an off-cut from the Smithy's cold end, because the wax board we had been using was too small for the second page of the Hold's life and because we needed a surface a knife would go into without skipping. The grain runs horizontal. There is a knot at the lower right that we planed around and that we left in because to take it out would have thinned the board under the line we intended to cut *Drifting toward* and the knife might have run through on a hard push.

The first thing I cut, with Sila standing next to me and not helping because I had said I would do it and she had said nothing, was the four words at the top of the board. Four pairs of letters, each in front of a column wide enough for a number with a sign in front of it.

Memory.

Mercy.

Communion.

Dominion.

Underneath them, ruled in a single shallow line, the fifth line, which I am going to spend this chapter on because Grieve Mourne has spent seventy days arguing with me about it and I have refused eleven times to make the plural singular, and because this is my hand and not his and I am going to say what I meant by it.

Drifting toward.

·  ·  ·

The board does what the board does because the four axes move together and not in the same direction. Each one is a question with two answers and no third, and the answers are at war because the choices that move one up move the others down. A settlement can be remembered and merciless, or remembered and open, or held and forgotten. It cannot be all four at once. The four axes are the four ways a Hold of twenty-two working people can be wrong about the same field on the same afternoon.

Memory is the question of whether the names are spoken. To put a name in a pane is to call a hold into the room, and a room that calls its hold into the room every evening has more in it than the people sitting in it. Memory goes up when a Telling is held and a name is recounted in full, and it goes up when an Echo is reclaimed, and it goes up when a wielder falls, which is the strangest of the movements and the one I have to defend at the foot of the page.

Mercy is the question of whether a thing that carries hold is owed anything. A Husk in the west field is a person who was failed. To walk past it is mercy. To put it down is ruin. The Cull, who are mostly Wardens and mostly people I respect, hold the ruin reading, and they have never been wrong about what the reading costs. I have held the mercy reading in the entries I have cut and I am not going to be the one who settles which is right because I have read this board for the rest of this book and I have not yet settled it.

Communion is the question of whether another lineage's dead is your business. An Echo that drifts into the Vale from another valley is still its owner's grip, and what a Hold does with it is its own affair, and the entries the board records are not the same as the entries the holding of the rite deserves. To open the gate is Communion. To bar the gate is Isolation. To put the thing down at the fence is a different entry still and I will come to it.

Dominion is the question of whether the Vale is something you hold or something you are in. A wielder who goes out and walks the long grass and brings back what was left in it is holding the ground. A wielder who kneels down in her own field and gives away part of what she is holding, to a stranger who is leaving in the morning, is in the ground, and the board agrees with her and not with the man who built the wall.

·  ·  ·

Below the four axes, the fifth line. Above the four axes, nothing.

That is the board.

What gets cut into it is what gets cut into it, and I will not apologise for what I have not cut into it, because Grieve Mourne has now spent seventy days apologising for what I did cut into it, and the man can write, and the man is wrong about what I left out, and I am going to address him here because I never had a chance to address him when I was alive, and because he has been compiling my hand for the better part of a season and I am tired of having my prose corrected by a Teller who was a child in the years I was keeping it.

Grieve. The room was not a room. It was a Hearth and it was also a kitchen and it was also a counting house, and the walls were not stone. The timber was not blackened. The board was not ash. The grain did not run horizontal. I made the room because a ledger does not record rooms, it records days, and a day in a room is not the day. You wanted what the room felt like. I am the wrong hand for that. Read Sila.

Now the verbs.

I cut them into the inside of the Smithy door on the morning of the twenty-first, because the Smithy door is the second most-read surface in the Hold and I wanted people to have to look at them before they cut their own. There are eighteen and they are in this order, which is the order I was taught by a Teller I never met, in a hold whose name I never learned, on a Wayfarer's leaf I no longer have:

echo_reclaimed. Memory plus six, Mercy plus three, Communion plus one, Dominion plus two. *Reclaimed the Echo of %s.* All four up. The day somebody went out and brought one home.

echo_abandoned. Memory minus five, Mercy minus three, Communion minus two, Dominion minus one. *Left the Echo of %s to grow.* The only entry that records a decision not to act.

echo_raided. Memory minus eight, Mercy minus six, Communion minus four, Dominion minus five. *The Echo of %s reached the palisade.* Twenty-three across the four axes. The heaviest line on the board.

vigil_held. Memory plus eight, Mercy plus four, Communion plus five, Dominion zero. *Held a %s.* A Burial and a Wake cost the same line on the board, which is the dishonesty in the line and the only reason a Hold can read well on Memory for a season and be unable to bury anybody.

vigil_skipped. Memory minus three, Mercy zero, Communion minus two, Dominion plus two. *Set out with the dead unburied.* The cheapest entry and the one that appears on the last dozen leaves of any board anyone has read out of a finished hold.

wielder_died. Memory plus two, Mercy zero, Communion zero, Dominion minus three. *%s fell.* Two on Memory is the strangest thing on the board. It is argued at the foot of this leaf.

successor_taken. Memory zero, Mercy zero, Communion plus two, Dominion plus one. *%s took up the blade.* A staffing decision held at a funeral. Two on Communion because three names are called in front of everyone.

villager_conscripted. Memory minus one, Mercy minus four, Communion minus three, Dominion plus five. *Pulled %s from their work to fight.* A Forager in a Warden's kit is not a Forager.

enemy_spared. Memory zero, Mercy plus six, Communion plus two, Dominion minus three. *Walked past %s without drawing.* The Cull read the minus three and stop reading.

enemy_slain. Memory zero, Mercy minus one, Communion zero, Dominion plus two. *Put down %s.* The flattest line on the board.

relic_spent. Memory plus five, Mercy zero, Communion plus three, Dominion plus one. *Spent a relic on stone that will outlast you.* Stone, not steel. Stone, because the stone holds ground.

region_saved. Memory plus four, Mercy plus seven, Communion plus six, Dominion plus two. *Pushed the decay back from %s.* The best single entry the board can record.

region_lost. Memory minus four, Mercy minus seven, Communion minus five, Dominion minus four. *%s went to ash.* The worst single entry the board can record.

ally_revived. Memory plus two, Mercy plus eight, Communion plus nine, Dominion minus two. *Lifted %s off the ground.* The largest single movement on Communion in any verb, and it takes about as long as it takes to bring a small pot up.

ally_abandoned. Memory minus two, Mercy minus eight, Communion minus ten, Dominion plus three. *Left %s where they fell.* The largest single subtraction on any axis in any verb, and the board is not being squeamish, it is being accurate.

echo_sent. Memory plus three, Mercy minus two, Communion plus six, Dominion plus four. *Sent the Echo of %s drifting into another settlement.* A line that crossed a ridge before there were two valleys writing in each other's books.

foreign_echo_slain. Memory plus one, Mercy minus three, Communion plus four, Dominion plus six. *Put down a stranger's Echo: %s.* Four on Communion is the strangest arithmetic in this language and no copy I have read explains it to my satisfaction.

foreign_echo_honoured. Memory plus six, Mercy plus seven, Communion plus nine, Dominion minus one. *Buried a stranger's Echo: %s.* The largest single movement on Mercy in any verb.

Eighteen. The order is the order I was taught. There have been copies that have tried a nineteenth and the nineteenth has always been one of the eighteen already, and I have refused eleven requests to make the plural singular, four of them from Tellers who wanted the line clean for a Telling.

·  ·  ·

The fifth line.

Drifting toward.

I cut it on the twentieth, with Sila standing next to me, and I cut it because the board needs a fifth line or it is a counting board and not a reading board, and a counting board cannot answer the question the four axes are asking. The four axes move together and they can move in any direction, and without a fifth line the board has no opinion about what it is doing, and the Hold has no opinion about what the board is doing, and a Hold without an opinion about what its board is doing is a Hold that will stop reading it.

I did not choose the name of the fifth line. The line is older than I am. I cut it because it was the line that was there before I started. I cut it as a horizontal stroke ruled first, with the words *Drifting toward* cut into the wood at the start of the line and the rest of the line left empty for a Teller to write what the board is doing.

What the Teller writes is one of five names.

There are five endings, in this order, checked in this order, first match wins.

The Wake. Memory at or above forty-five and Communion at or above forty and every Echo reclaimed and the lineage not empty.

The Long Hold. Dominion at or above fifty and Mercy at or below minus twenty.

Ash Inheritance. Memory at or below minus forty.

The Open Hearth. Communion at or above fifty-five and Mercy at or above thirty-five.

The Quiet Field. Always.

I am not going to defend the order. The order is the order. I will say that the order is the only reason two valleys with identical numbers got two different endings, and that whoever reads this should hold the order against whoever defends it.

I will also say that the fifth line is not a prophecy. A prophecy is what a board says before the day. The fifth line is what the board says after the day. It is a record of what the board is doing, and it is the Teller's duty to cut it at the turn of every season, and I have done it for seventy-six days, and Grieve Mourne has done it after me, and the line has been wrong in my hand three times and wrong in his more times than he will admit and he is right to refuse to admit them.

The fifth line was right on the twenty-first day. The board read The Quiet Field, and the Quiet Field is what the board reads when it has nothing to say, and it is the only ending that does not require a settlement to be anything in particular. The Quiet Field is what most settlements are most of the time and I am not going to be the one who decides whether that is an ending or a life.

·  ·  ·

Grieve.

I am not arguing with you. I am not settling it. I am leaving this chapter on the board the way I cut it, with the four axes and the fifth line and the eighteen verbs on the door of the Smithy, and I am going to die on the ninety-sixth day of a thing nobody has named yet and you are going to take this hand and you are going to put yourself in the margins of it, and that is your right and that is what Tellers do, and the four numbers will move and you will write down what they move to.

The board does not average. That is the phrase I want planted here because I will not have a chance to plant it later, and because it is the only piece of doctrine I have that I am willing to die for. The board does not average a region saved and a region lost. The board holds both. A Hold that lost a field and got it back is not the same as a Hold that never lost it, and the board will say so until somebody scrapes it, and I have not scraped it, and I am not going to.

The board is a liar with good handwriting. I am going to borrow that from you because you said it better than I would have, and because you said it on the hundred and sixteenth day about the day you had just reclaimed, and because the board was reading The Wake that morning and the Wake is what the board reads when somebody has gone out and fetched everybody, and I had been keeping it for ninety-six days and I had not seen that line on it yet and you had seen it three times and that is why the line came out of your mouth and not mine.

I am done with this chapter. The board is cut. The Smithy door has the verbs. The fifth line is ruled. Twenty-two people are in the room who are not going to read the board tonight, and four of them are going to read it tomorrow, and the four are the four who will carry the graver after me.

That is what I have to say about the board.

The room was not a room.

·  ·  ·

---

Grieve Mourne, compiling, fourth hand. I have left this chapter as it was cut, with one exception. Bran Holt wrote above that the room was not a room and the timber was not blackened and the grain did not run horizontal. The room was a room and the timber was blackened and the grain did run horizontal. I have been in the Hearth. I have held my hand to the wall at the lintel. Bran Holt knew I would read this and he left the paragraph in anyway. I think he meant it as a joke. I think Bran Holt's jokes have to be held to the light the way his scraped lines do, and I cannot read either, and I have let both stand.

The board is on the north wall. The grain runs horizontal. The knot is at the lower right.
