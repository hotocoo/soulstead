# PART I — THE LOOSING (before and at the founding)

Hand: Sila Carver's first hand, compiled and argued with by Grieve Mourne. Register:
elegiac shading into saga. No fights except one, badly, in chapter 5.

## Chapter 1: The Work of Giants
**Hand:** Grieve Mourne, compiling · **Days:** 210 and before day 1 · **Place:** the Vane Road, then the Vale
- Opens at the end: Grieve, day 210, at the Vane Road ruin with the Wake behind him, looking at a wall nobody living could build. Elegiac. The *where has it gone* figure. Gate still barred from inside.
- States the Teller's rule and the book's method: three earlier hands, villager testimony, the Memorial panes, the Vane Road board, four Wayfarer copies. *I record what was said. I do not settle it.*
- States the book's shape without apology: it is a ledger, not a story, so it runs out of order where the roads run out of order, and every page has a day on it.
- Establishes the Vale physically: ninety-six by seventy-two of walkable ground, the Mourne at the north head, the Kettle, the lake, the scarp, Brackwood, Wrenfield, the Grey Ash.
- Plants: the barred gate; the legible Vane board with four numbers still on it; the phrase *work of giants*; the last line of the book's first page, which chapter 52 answers.
- Do **not** explain hold. Do not name the five endings.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 2: The Mourne's Last Breath
**Hand:** Grieve compiling from Forager lore · **Days:** long before 1 · **Place:** the Grey Ash, the Mourne
- The ashfall as physical event, remembered in Forager craft rather than history: a hand's depth of grey over everything, once.
- The agronomy: ash ground tills like flour, holds water, grows the best barley anyone ever saw, and in the fifth season starves on soil that looks identical. Locked ground. Never supply the modern word.
- The remedy learned hard: ground bone, burnt shell from the mere, lye from the pyres, back into the furrows.
- Introduce the Forager's craft of telling fall-ash from drawn-ash by feel and by what grows. They are the same colour.
- Plants: the lime-kilns at Stonewake; the phrase *the fields are hungry again*; the Forager's test.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 3: The Coming of the Ashfall Family
**Hand:** Sila Carver, first hand · **Days:** 1 to 5 · **Place:** the Hold
- Oda Ashfall, Sila Carver, Bran Holt, Ketil Brack, and eleven others raise the Hearth on day one. That is day one, and the Chronicle counts from there.
- Jobs assigned as jobs, not classes: Field-hand, Smith, Forager, Warden, Healer, Carver. Six, and the argument about who has to be the Warden when there is nothing to fight yet.
- Sila's hand introduced as a physical thing: cramped, letters cut short to save wax, weather recorded with suspicion.
- The Hearth as the only safe fire, and the discovery that nothing in the Hold moves while everyone is inside it, stated as a shrug and not a horror. Chapter 11 will pick it up.
- Plants: the ledger board, blank; the first pane, uncut; the wax.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 4: What the Ash Does to the Dead
**Hand:** Sila Carver · **Days:** 6 to 8 · **Place:** the Hold, Wrenfield
- Oda Ashfall dies of an ordinary thing, badly, on day six. The Hold has no rite because nobody has needed one.
- The body does not rot. It sets. Describe accurately and without wonder: cool, dry, heavier, and a dip worn into the doorstone where she used to stand that is now warmer than the stone around it.
- Day eight, the body stands up and walks to the field and begins the motion of reaping with nothing in its hands.
- Nobody in the Hold has the word Husk yet. They call it *Oda*, and that is the whole horror of the chapter.
- Plants: the doorstone; the reaping motion; Sila's line, *it is not her and it is her hold, and I do not know which of those is worse to write*.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 5: The First Interval
**Hand:** Sila Carver, disputed by two villager testimonies · **Days:** 8 to 9 · **Place:** Wrenfield, the Hold
- They wait. Nine days between the Loosing and the Keeping, invented by accident, because nobody could agree.
- Ketil Brack goes at it with a fence stake and learns what poise is before there is a word for it. He lives. The scene is short, clumsy, and frightening because nobody knows the rules.
- Bran Holt notices the grass around the body going pale at the root. Turning, though nobody calls it that yet.
- The three motions get named in one evening by argument: the Loosing, the Holding, the Keeping. Two testimonies disagree about who said it first. Do not settle it.
- Plants: the fence stake; the pale root; the nine days as the Vale's standing measure of *too long*.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 6: The Invention of the Vigil
**Hand:** Sila Carver · **Day:** 9 · **Place:** the Memorial ground
- Sila Carver digs a hole and puts Oda in it with her gear, whole, on the reasoning that a hold is closed around things and the things should go too.
- It works. The Husk goes down mid-motion. Describe the moment physically: everyone in the Hold feels the spending get cheaper and nobody can say why.
- The Hold works better that afternoon. The first Legacy. The arithmetic gets noticed by Bran Holt, who writes down that twenty of something was spent and six of something gained.
- The Memorial founded as a place, the first pane cut, the glass question raised and deferred to chapter 33.
- The Chronicle is begun because Sila decides that if the rite worked, the reason must be written where the next one can find it.
- Plants: the twenty and the six; the first pane; *we buried her with her knife because the knife was part of the grip*.
**Ledger:** row 1.

# PART II — THE FIRST BLADE

Hand: Sila, then Bran Holt from day 20. Register: saga, tight. This is where the reader
learns combat through bodies.

## Chapter 7: Rin Ashfall
**Hand:** Sila Carver · **Days:** 10 to 11 · **Place:** the Smithy, the Hold
- Rin Ashfall, Oda's daughter, nineteen, Reckless and Quick. Swings first, rolls come cheap, minus eight of something she does not have a word for.
- The Ashblade forged from a plough coulter and cart iron. Sixteen of power, twenty-six of reach, an arc of a body and a half. Ketil Brack makes it in two days and is not proud of it.
- The first Succession, which is not yet a rite: three names, and two of them are needed in the fields, and that is the argument.
- Teach Composure through her body only: the third swing is slower, the fourth is not there, and Ketil tells her to stop and she cannot hear him yet.
- *Taking the blade* enters the language.
- Plants: the coulter's old curve still visible in the blade; Rin's habit of touching the doorstone on the way out.
**Ledger:** row 2.

## Chapter 8: Wrenfield and the Long Grass
**Hand:** Sila Carver · **Day:** 12 · **Place:** Wrenfield
- First expedition. The clock starts when she steps past the fence. One minute out is an hour of the world, and the Hold behind her is not moving at all.
- Two Cinder Stalkers out of tall grass. Thirty-four of health, twelve of damage, sixty-four of speed, a quarter-second tell. They die to one clean swing and they do not come alone.
- Teach commitment: she agrees to a heavy swing, the second Stalker is already inside it, and she takes twelve because a swing is a promise.
- Teach the dodge honestly: eighteen hundredths of nothing-touches-you inside forty-two hundredths of being unable to do anything else. And what happens when she rolls with nothing left: a slow sidestep and no mercy in it.
- She kills both and walks home shaking and says the grass needs cutting.
- Plants: the tall grass as a place things come out of; the shaking hands.
**Ledger:** row 3.

## Chapter 9: The Warden at Stonewake
**Hand:** Sila Carver, with Bran Holt's marginal note · **Day:** 13 · **Place:** Stonewake Scarp
- The barrows above the quarry. Upright things in packed plate that have been standing a long time.
- One Gravewarden. Ninety of health, eighteen of damage, fifty-five of poise, sixty-two hundredths of tell you can watch arrive and cannot afford to misread.
- It says a name while it fights. Not her mother's; someone else's, nobody the Hold knows. This is the chapter that establishes the horror and defers the explanation.
- Rin breaks the packing with the heavy swing and gets in. First relic in the Vale: dense, warm, wrong to hold, shaped like nothing.
- Ketil Brack does not know what to do with it yet. It sits on a shelf for six days.
- Plants: the relic on the shelf; the spoken name, unidentified, which chapter 23 picks up.
**Ledger:** row 4.

## Chapter 10: The Day the Hillside Was Beautiful
**Hand:** Sila Carver · **Day:** 14 · **Place:** Wrenfield
- Rin Ashfall dies. Saga understatement at maximum. Four Stalkers, bad ground, and she was already down to nothing from the second.
- Do not dramatise. Do not give her a speech. Give her one observation about the slope and the grass, then the flat fact.
- The Hold learns what an Echo is by watching one form: the body stays, and around it the grip closes, and on day two it will be stronger than she was.
- Grief arithmetic stated for the first time as arithmetic: ten, and three for every bond, and Rin had five bonds, so twenty-five. Sila writes the number and hates it.
- Plants: the Ashblade lying in the grass; the phrase *the hillside is worth looking at*, which recurs at every wielder's death and is answered in chapter 52.
**Ledger:** row 5.

## Chapter 11: Maren Verren Takes the Maul
**Hand:** Sila Carver · **Day:** 15 · **Place:** the Hold, the Smithy
- The first real Succession. Three candidates: Maren Verren, Rin's wife; Bran Holt, who is needed on the board; Ketil Brack, who is needed at the fire.
- *They shared a roof with the dead.* Spouse: less Composure and more Resolve, and the chapter has to make that one physical fact, not a table. She has less to spend because more of what she had is lying in the long grass, closer to it than she is.
- She refuses the Ashblade. The Widow's Maul out of the quarry sledge: twenty-four of power, a two-body arc, a heavy swing that takes forty-six hundredths of a second to arrive and ends an argument.
- The Hold names the weapon before she can stop them. She lets them.
- The world-clock horror finally lands: the Hold did not move for a day while she decided, and Rin's Echo tiered anyway, because the clock runs on whoever is outside and somebody was.
- Plants: the Maul's name; her habit of not touching the doorstone.
**Ledger:** row 6.

## Chapter 12: The Reclaiming of Rin
**Hand:** Sila Carver, last entry in the first hand · **Day:** 19 · **Place:** Wrenfield, the Memorial
- Four days late. Rin's Echo is tier two: more of her than she had, plus everything the field has fed it.
- The spouse bond made a weapon: she hits harder near it and empties faster near it, and both of those are the same fact.
- The fight is the book's first full set piece. It uses her real build. It is won on the fifth heavy swing and one parry she did not mean to make.
- Reclaiming returns the loadout: the Ashblade comes home. The Hold now has two weapons and one wielder.
- The Monument: forty of grief, Ketil's relic off the shelf set under stone raised over a reclaimed Echo. The ground around it stops going pale. First time anyone sees decay pushed back by an object.
- Sila Carver dies of nothing in particular nine days later and the hand changes. Say so here, flatly, out of order, in one sentence.
**Ledger:** rows 7, 8, 9.

# PART III — THE LEDGER AND THE FOUR READINGS

Hand: Bran Holt, legible, dull, complete, compiled by Grieve who resents him. Register:
clerical and saga alternating. These are the doctrine chapters. Every faction must be
correct.

## Chapter 13: The Ledger Board
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 20 to 21 · **Place:** the Hearth
- Bran Holt takes the hand and immediately makes it worse and better: he records only what he is asked to, and he records it perfectly.
- How an entry is cut: the day, the verb, the subject, four numbers, one sentence. Eighteen verbs and there have only ever been eighteen. List them on the board as a physical thing.
- The four numbers get their names at the turn of the season: Memory against Oblivion, Mercy against Ruin, Communion against Isolation, Dominion against Surrender.
- The last line on the board, *Drifting toward*, and the fact that nobody knows who put it there or what authority it has. It has been right about four other valleys.
- Grieve's compiling voice intrudes for the first time to argue with Bran about what he left out.
- Plants: the board's physical construction; the phrase *the board does not average*.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 14: The Tellers
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Day:** 21 · **Place:** the Memorial
- The first Telling: twenty-five of grief, the death recounted aloud in full by someone who saw it, and what it buys is eight in a hundred more of the thing that comes back when you stop swinging.
- Maren tells Rin's death in front of everyone and gets two details wrong, and Bran writes both the telling and the corrections into the Chronicle and refuses to choose.
- Teller doctrine stated by a person and not a narrator: the bond does not end, it changes shape, and a Hold that carries its dead forward has more in it than the living could manage.
- Their honest weakness shown in the same chapter: the Telling costs grief the fields needed spent on something faster, and Ketil Brack says so.
- Plants: the two wrong details, which chapter 25 and chapter 50 both use.
**Ledger:** row 10.

## Chapter 15: The Unnaming
**Hand:** Bran Holt, with a scraped line · **Days:** 22 to 23 · **Place:** the Memorial, the Hold at night
- Someone shaves a name off a pane. The pane is glass. The shaving leaves a smear you can only see at one angle.
- The theory, argued well, by a Forager nobody dislikes: the dead did not walk before we started writing them down. An Echo grows because it is being called. Stop calling, and the grip opens with nobody having to go out and break it.
- The counter-argument, also good: it has never once been demonstrated to work, and no Legacy has ever come from silence.
- The Hold's law against it, and the fact that the law came first and the reasoning after.
- A line in Bran's hand is scraped out here, and Grieve says he has held the page to the fire and to the light and cannot recover it, and will not guess.
- Plants: the smear; the scraped line; the Forager's name, which must be one of the canon names and must recur in chapters 24, 25 and 50.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 16: The Hearthkeepers and the Cull
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Day:** 24 · **Place:** Wrenfield, the Hold
- Oda Ashfall's Husk. She was buried on day nine, and something still walks the reaping motion in the west field, and no two accounts agree about how that is possible. Record both. Do not settle it.
- Maren walks past it without drawing. Six of Mercy, two of Communion, three off Dominion, and a fortnight of argument.
- Healer doctrine: a Husk is a person who was failed, and what it is owed is a rite and not a blade. Ration the Flask. Quarantine rather than cull.
- Cull doctrine, given its best speech: hold is contagion, it moves through ground, and everything that carries it goes down early and goes down cheap, and that includes the sick, and we will say so out loud.
- The Cull are the reason the Hold survives chapter 39. Establish that here so it lands there.
- Plants: the Flask ration argument, which chapter 34 completes; the Cull's roster.
**Ledger:** row 11.

## Chapter 17: The Long Hold and the Open Road
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 25 to 26 · **Place:** the palisade, the Vane Road
- Three Wayfarers at the crossing post, and a six-character route-mark cut into it meaning *a hold this way is keeping a board you can write on*.
- What Wayfarers actually carry: not maps, not goods, entries. Ledger lines copied out of other valleys' chronicles. They will read you somebody else's day for a meal.
- Open Road doctrine: hold does not recognise a palisade, so a rite should not either.
- The Long Hold named, and where the name comes from, and the fact that Vane Hold's wall is still standing and Vane Hold is not. Defer the detail to chapter 30.
- The Hold lets them in. Bran writes no verb for it, because there is no verb for hospitality, and Grieve points out what the absence of that verb has cost the Vale.
- Plants: the route-mark; Tove Rowan named for the first time as a Wayfarer, a walk-on, no weight yet.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 18: The Gravewardens and the Yielding
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Day:** 27 · **Place:** Stonewake Scarp, the Grey Ash
- The old order's history, assembled from what the barrows contain rather than from any record: they bound relics into their own plate, one at a time, on the theory that a body carrying enough borrowed hold could not be opened.
- They were right. That is the problem. They are still standing.
- Maren kills one. The Hold's word for the enemy and the Hold's word for the order are the same word and characters are visibly uncomfortable about it.
- The Yielding introduced in one scene and given the strongest argument in the book: walk north, sit down, let the ground take it back early and unclosed. They leave no Echo. Nobody in the Vale can answer this and everybody hates it.
- Plants: the barrow that is sealed and has not been opened, for chapter 23; the first name of the Yielding's habit, *going north*.
**Ledger:** row 12.

# PART IV — THE SIDE ROADS

Sixteen chapters, out of chronological order, grouped by road. Every chapter in this part
carries a **The Other Roads** section per `STYLE.md`: two to four branches, each with its
exact ledger movement and its ending pressure, reported in Grieve's compiling voice from
other valleys or from the Hold's own near-misses. This is the part that proves every road
is its own story and every story moves the board.

## Chapter 19: The Unburied Smith
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 29 to 31 · **Place:** the Smithy
- Ketil Brack's apprentice, Bel Brack, dies at the fire. Ketil will not give up the gear for a Pyre, because the gear is the boy's grip and burning it is burning him.
- The Hold needs the Pyre: thirty of grief spent buys a fourth Flask, and Maren is going to the scarp again.
- Maren persuades him. How she does it is the chapter: she does not argue, she works his fire for two days and lets the arithmetic say itself.
- The Pyre. What a pyre actually is, materially: body and gear burned together, and the ash of it steeped, settled three days, drawn off clear, and cut with hearth-water. The Flask is his ash. Say it once, plainly, and let it sit.
**Other Roads:** taking the gear by force (`villager_conscripted`-adjacent Dominion road, report from Verren Hold where the smith was broken open); letting it rot (`vigil_skipped`, and what the Hold's output did); the Unnaming's offer to shave the boy's name instead.
**Ledger:** row 13.

## Chapter 20: Low Kettle Under the Mere
**Hand:** Bran Holt, with a Wayfarer copy · **Days:** 33 to 38 · **Place:** Kettlemere
- Low Kettle: a village drowned before the Hold, whose dead are in the Holding at a depth nobody can walk to. The Vale has no rite for water and has never had one.
- Bel Kettle, a child, was carried up and laid at the Hold before the water closed, and hers is the only Echo from that village the board has ever been able to count.
- Her Echo has been standing in the shallows since before day one. Nobody has gone in. Thirty-eight days.
- Maren goes in. Fighting in water: Composure spends faster, the roll is gone, the Maul is wrong here and she brings it anyway.
- Reclaiming a child's Echo. Handle with restraint and no sentiment. The loadout it returns is a knife and a shoe.
- The chapter must state, flatly, that the board cannot count what no Teller can survey, and that the lake is therefore not on it. Chapter 47 and chapter 52 both need this.
**Other Roads:** draining the mere (`region_lost`, Mercy and Communion collapse, Long Hold pressure); sealing it and cutting no pane (`echo_abandoned` plus an Unnaming argument, Ash Inheritance pressure); the Wayfarer report of a hold that dived for all of them and lost four wielders doing it.
**Ledger:** row 14.

## Chapter 21: The Cull at Brackwood, One
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 41 to 46 · **Place:** Brackwood
- Nine charcoal burners come back from a Greying stand of Brackwood with grey in the seams of their hands and something wrong with their sleep.
- It is not a plague. It is drawn-ash in the lungs, and what it does is loosen the grip early: they are spending hold they are not getting back, and one of them has already stopped being able to hold a cup.
- The Cull's roster is read out at the Hearth. Names, all canon names, people the reader has seen working.
- The Healers' quarantine huts and the cost: nine people out of the labour pool, plus two Healers, on a Hold of twenty-something.
- End the chapter with the decision not yet made and the vote called for the next morning. No cliffhanger language. Just stop.
**Other Roads:** none in this chapter. Say so: *this road forks in the next chapter and not this one.*
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 22: The Cull at Brackwood, Two
**Hand:** Bran Holt, then Asha Vane's later annotation · **Day:** 47 · **Place:** Brackwood, the Hold
- Maren walks into the quarantine and sits down in it. That is the argument and it wins.
- The nine are kept. Six of Mercy, two of Communion, three off Dominion. Four of them die anyway over the following month and are buried, and the Chronicle records that too, in the same voice.
- Then the second half: Brackwood itself is pushed back from Greying, which takes eleven days of Carvers setting relic-stone and Foragers turning lye and bone into the burnt stands. Four, seven, six, two. The best single entry the Hold ever wrote.
- Communion stands at thirty-nine after it. One short of the gate, for six days, and nobody knew.
- Asha Vane annotates this page seventy days later in a different mood and Grieve keeps both.
**Other Roads:** the cull performed (`enemy_slain` road plus `region_lost`, Ruin and Dominion, the exact Long Hold bend, reported from Verren Hold); bringing them inside the palisade untreated (Mercy up, a grief bomb of five bonds each, and the output floor); the Unnaming's variant, in which the sick are unnamed while still alive.
**Ledger:** rows 15, 16.

## Chapter 23: The Gravewarden Who Remembered
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 50 to 53 · **Place:** Stonewake Scarp, the sealed barrow
- The sealed barrow from chapter 18 is open from the inside. What comes out says *Oda Ashfall* and then *Rin*, in order, correctly.
- Its plate contains relics. Some of them are the Hold's dead, taken out of ground the Hold thought it had kept. The Monument at Wrenfield is checked and is intact, which makes it worse, not better: these came from somewhere else.
- Its name, as far as the Hold can establish, was Halde. Chapter 9's unidentified spoken name is resolved here and only here, and resolving it explains nothing.
- Maren does not draw. Eighteen days of Hold argument compressed into one page. The Cull never forgive it.
- This is the entry that first puts **The Wake** on the board, day fifty-three, and the chapter should end on Bran reading that line out and nobody knowing what it means.
**Other Roads:** putting it down for the relic (`enemy_slain`, and the Monument it would have paid for, and the Dominion road); following it back into the barrows (a Wayfarer copy in which a wielder did, and the last four pages of that chronicle are in a hand that spells its own name two ways); the Unnaming's proposal to unname every relic in its plate.
**Ledger:** row 17.

## Chapter 24: The Shaved Panes
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 55 to 58 · **Place:** the Memorial
- Eleven panes are smeared. Grieve, a boy of nine in this chapter, is the one who notices, because he is the only person who looks at the Memorial from the wrong angle.
- Establish Grieve as a child here: a Carver's apprentice with a habit of reading the board aloud to himself. His only appearance before chapter 41.
- The investigation is not a mystery plot. It is a Hold of twenty-six people counting who was awake.
- What the shaving does, measurably: nothing to the Echoes. Two of the eleven are wielders' kin and their graves are quiet, and two are not, and the pale root is spreading at one of the two. Both facts are recorded and neither explains the other.
- End with the Forager from chapter 15 admitting it in a sentence, without defiance, and stating the theory one more time better than before.
**Other Roads:** none here; note that the fork is chapter 25.
**Ledger:** none.

## Chapter 25: The Quiet Doctrine
**Hand:** Bran Holt, then Grieve compiling · **Days:** 59 to 61 · **Place:** the Hold, the Memorial
- The Hold's judgement. No hanging. Maren's ruling: the shaved names are re-cut, and a Telling is held for all eleven at once, and the Forager cuts them.
- The Telling for eleven names: twenty-five of grief and eight of Memory, and the physical scene of a Hold saying eleven names in a row while one person holds the graver.
- The doctrine's best day and worst day in one page: while the panes are being recut, the pale root at the one grave stops. Nobody can say which act did it.
- Grieve's compiling voice takes the chapter's last page to state the honest position: the Unnaming may be right, and if they are, this book is the machine that has been killing the Vale, and he is still writing it.
**Other Roads:** **joining** the Unnaming, in full, as the Ash Inheritance road, reported from Verren Hold: the panes shaved in order of age, the board's Memory falling past minus forty, and the last forty pages of their chronicle written in one hand under a name that is not its own; ignoring the shaving entirely; hanging the Forager (Dominion and Ruin, and what the Hold became for a season in the valley that did).
**Ledger:** row 18.

## Chapter 26: The Long Grass Road
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 63 to 66 · **Place:** Wrenfield, the palisade
- Four Wayfarers want passage through Wrenfield in a Turning season. Tove Rowan is one of them. Second appearance, still a walk-on, and the reader should not yet know she matters.
- The Hold's Wardens are against it. The long grass is a place things come out of and a stranger in it is four more bodies the Hold cannot account for.
- The gate is opened. In the grass, a Stalker takes Tove Rowan down and Maren lifts her off the ground. Two, eight, nine, minus two: the biggest Communion entry in the Hold's history to that point.
- What being lifted off the ground actually is: another body spending its hold to close your grip for you. Establish this properly. It is the mechanism the whole Open Hearth depends on.
- Tove Rowan copies the Hold's board into her own book before she leaves, and that is how Holt Vale comes to have Ashfall's numbers, and why chapter 41 is possible.
**Other Roads:** leaving her down (`ally_abandoned`, minus ten of Communion, and the Wayfarers stop coming, which is the Long Hold's actual mechanism and not a wall at all); turning them away at the post; robbing them (Ruin and Dominion, reported from a valley where the route-marks were cut off the posts).
**Ledger:** row 19.

## Chapter 27: The Stranger's Echo
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 68 to 70 · **Place:** Wrenfield, the Memorial
- An Echo walks into the Vale that nobody in the Vale has ever met. It carries a name the Hold does not have: Sila Verren. A drifted Echo, roamed out of its own valley, still wearing the build it died with.
- Establish drift: an Echo unclaimed long enough and roaming far enough leaves its region and then its valley, and arrives somewhere carrying its owner's name and gear and nothing else.
- The Hold's choice, argued for two days by people with real stakes: it is not ours, it is not owed a pane, and the Carvers have nine panes of backlog.
- Maren buries it. Six, seven, nine, minus one. A stranger's name cut into Ashfall glass, fluxed with pearl from a stranger's ash, in the Hold's own Memorial.
- The Wayfarers carry the entry back east and Verren Hold's board moves because Ashfall's Teller wrote a line. State the mechanism plainly: what crosses between holds is entries, not ground.
**Other Roads:** putting it down (`foreign_echo_slain`, plus one Memory, minus three Mercy, plus four Communion, plus six Dominion, and the fact that this is *also* a Communion gain, which should unsettle the reader); sending it on (`echo_sent`); barring the gate against it and letting it tier at the fence.
**Ledger:** row 20.

## Chapter 28: The Relic Debt
**Hand:** Asha Vane, third hand · **Days:** 86 to 91 · **Place:** the Smithy, Wrenfield
- Out of sequence: this chapter sits after chapter 35 in time and before it in the book, and the Teller says so in one line and does not apologise.
- Colm Grey is wielder. Maren Verren is six days dead in the west field and her Echo is tier three and roaming.
- Ketil Brack's price for rebinding the Reaper Pike is a relic, and the only relic within reach is inside Maren's Echo, which means the Hold's new wielder has to go kill the Hold's old wielder to arm himself.
- Colm is her rival. *Never liked them. Will still carry the blade.* The rival's cold gift: her Echo's pull does not touch him, and he is the only person in the Hold who could do this job without emptying.
- He does it. The relic is spent on stone first, not steel, because Asha Vane argues him into it on the board, and that is why row 25 comes before row 26.
**Other Roads:** binding the relic into the Pike instead (the weapon-art road, and what a blade that remembers Maren's motion does to the person holding it); refusing and going to the scarp for a Gravewarden's relic (Dominion); waiting, and the tier five that waiting produces, which the Hold will see for real in chapter 32.
**Ledger:** rows 25, 26.

## Chapter 29: The Fieldhand's Wedding
**Hand:** Asha Vane, writing about herself in the third person · **Days:** 93 to 95 · **Place:** the Hold
- Asha Vane and Bran Holt's son are to be married on day ninety-four. The Hold has twenty-four working people and a Warden short.
- No combat. This is the settlement chapter, and it must be the best-observed chapter in the book: work, rota, bread, the lye vats, the argument about who covers the mere road.
- Colm Grey conscripts Asha out of the Carvers' shed on the morning of day ninety-five. Minus one, minus four, minus three, plus five. Dominion reaches eleven, the highest the Hold ever goes.
- The Hold's own Teller is now in a Warden's kit, and that is why the third hand is fast and angry and argues with the entries it copies.
- She is married anyway, at the palisade, in the kit, in eleven minutes, and Bran Holt writes the only sentence of his hand that Grieve admits is good.
**Other Roads:** conscripting the groom instead; conscripting neither, and the Warden shortage that follows into chapter 32; the Cull's proposal to conscript the four surviving Brackwood sick, who are of no use in a field anyway.
**Ledger:** row 27.

## Chapter 30: Vane Hold
**Hand:** Asha Vane, who is not related to it and is tired of being asked · **Days:** 97 to 99 · **Place:** the Vane Road
- The excavation chapter. Full elegiac register. The wall, the barred gate, the ledger board still legible with four numbers on it.
- Vane Hold's numbers, read off the board and recorded in the Chronicle exactly: Dominion past fifty, Mercy below minus twenty, and the last line still saying what it said.
- What the inside contains: no Echoes at all, one hundred and sixteen Husks, every one of them in a doorway or at a loom or at a table, and the gate barred from inside by two arms' worth of timber.
- The reconstruction, offered as reconstruction and not fact: they stopped going out, so the clock stopped, so nothing decayed, so nothing came, and they were correct and it did not matter.
- A Burial is held for as many as three people can manage in two days, which is not many. Eight, four, five, zero.
- Plants: the board, which chapter 44 quotes in full; the timber; the phrase *they were not wrong, they were only finished*.
**Other Roads:** stripping it for timber and relics (Dominion, and the Hold that did); barring it back up and cutting no pane; the Wayfarer proposal to make it a road-shrine, which is what chapter 48 does in a different ending.
**Ledger:** row 28.

## Chapter 31: The Ash Orchard
**Hand:** Asha Vane · **Days:** 102 to 104 · **Place:** the Grey Ash
- Seven of the Hold walk north. Two of them are the surviving Brackwood sick; one is a Healer who is not sick and has decided anyway.
- The Ash Orchard: a stand of dead trees standing upright in ankle-deep drawn-ash where people from every valley have been coming to sit down for longer than anyone has been counting. Rows of them. Not skeletons. Depressions.
- The Yielding's case, delivered by the Healer, unanswerable: the rite is a theft, the ground is owed, and we leave no Echo. Nobody has to come out here with a maul for us.
- Colm Grey does not stop them. Zero, six, two, minus three. The Cull's second unforgiven day.
- The one who changes her mind and walks back, and what the Hold does with her, which is nothing, because there is no verb for it.
**Other Roads:** stopping them by force (`villager_conscripted` and Dominion, and the Hold that did it and had to keep doing it); going with them, reported from an unsigned fragment; the Cull's position, which is to kill them on the road before they reach ground that will eat what they are carrying.
**Ledger:** row 29.

## Chapter 32: The Raid of Yeva Holt
**Hand:** Asha Vane, from inside the kit · **Days:** 128 to 136 · **Place:** the palisade, the Hold
- The book's worst week and its midpoint. Out of sequence, and placed here because it belongs to the roads and not the lineage.
- Day 128, a Vigil skipped because Tarn Stonewake is a stranger to the dead and nobody made him. Minus three, zero, minus two, plus two.
- Day 130, Yeva Holt's Echo abandoned in Brackwood. Minus five, minus three, minus two, minus one. It is tier three by then and roaming, and the ground it crosses goes toward Ashen.
- Day 134, tier five at the palisade, carrying the Ashblade, fighting at the skill she had plus everything the Vale has fed it since. Minus eight, minus six, minus four, minus five. The Hold is a Warden short, because of chapter 29.
- The defence. Palisade value, the Wardens who are actually available, Asha in the kit writing nothing for six days, and Tarn Stonewake, a stranger, with the Widow's Maul.
- Day 135, reclaimed at the gate. Day 136, a Wake: sixty of grief, all of it, the whole Hold drinking, and the grief pool at zero for the first time since day six.
**Other Roads:** the Vigil held on day 128 (the entire week does not happen, and the Teller says so and shows the numbers); the Echo reclaimed on day 130 in the field; letting it into the Hold and fighting it in the lanes, reported from a valley that did and whose Chronicle stops mid-entry.
**Ledger:** rows 35, 36, 37, 38, 39.

## Chapter 33: The Carver's Glass
**Hand:** Bran Holt · **Days:** 72 to 74 · **Place:** the Smithy, the Memorial
- The Memorial's material chapter. How a pane is actually made: ash steeped for lye, lye boiled dry in the pot for pot-ash, pot-ash refined again for pearl, pearl into sand at the Smithy's hot end so the glass will run at a heat a wood fire can reach.
- Each pane is fluxed with pearl from the pyre of the person whose name is cut into it. A name written in the substance of the named. State it once. Do not press it.
- The problem: the Hold buries more than it burns, and buried dead yield no pearl, so the Memorial has a backlog of names it cannot make glass for. Nine panes deep in chapter 27, and this chapter is why.
- A relic spent on stone, not steel. Five, zero, three, one.
- Grieve at twelve, apprenticed, cutting his first pane, which is Bel Kettle's, and getting the K wrong.
**Other Roads:** cutting panes in slate instead (Memory holds, the pearl economy collapses, the Flask ration falls); burning everyone by default to keep the glass coming (Pyre-only Hold, and the Burial Legacy the lineage never gets); the Unnaming's cheapest proposal, which is to stop cutting panes and save the pearl for soap.
**Ledger:** row 21.

## Chapter 34: The Ash Flask
**Hand:** Bran Holt, with a Healer's own note · **Days:** 77 to 79 · **Place:** the Hearth, Wrenfield
- What the Flask is, finally and fully: pyre-ash steeped, settled three days, drawn off clear, cut with hearth-water until it will not strip a throat. It is drunk. It burns going down and keeps burning.
- What it does, in the body: gives back hold already spent, briefly, at cost.
- Why three. Not caution. The Healer's note lists four people, by name, who died at the Hearth with their teeth grey, and the dates, and what the fourth one said.
- The Pyre rite raises the ration to four because a pyre yields the ash, and the chapter must make the reader feel the circularity: you drink your dead to keep swinging, and swinging is how you make more of them.
- One Stalker killed on the way home, in one line, because the chapter needs to end on work and not on philosophy.
**Other Roads:** the Cull's ration, which is unlimited and is why they lose Wardens; the Healers' ration, which is one and has been refused twice; the Wayfarer variant, cut with mere-water, which does not burn and does not work as well, and nobody knows why.
**Ledger:** row 22.
