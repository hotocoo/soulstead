# Appendix A: The Ledger of Verbs

> *Eighteen. There have only ever been eighteen. I have looked in eleven copies for a
> nineteenth and found the same eighteen, in the same order, in hands that never met.*
> Recorded: Grieve Mourne, fourth hand, compiling

**Hand:** Grieve Mourne, compiling
**Kept:** loose in the second book at leaf three, unnumbered, between the roll and the first entry

The board in the Hearth has five lines. Four are numbers and the fifth is a name. This
leaf is about how the numbers move.

I have set the verbs down in the order they are cut into the board and not in any order of
my own, because the order is older than this Hold and nobody knows who chose it. A verb is
not an opinion. It is a hole of a particular shape, and an act either goes into it or it
does not go on the board at all. Each one below has its four numbers, the sentence the
Teller writes, what it costs a valley, and one day on which it was used. Where this Hold
never used a verb I have said so and used another valley's copy, and marked it.

· · ·

### 1. `echo_reclaimed`

Memory +6 · Mercy +3 · Communion +1 · Dominion +2
*Reclaimed the Echo of %s.*

All four up, which happens with one other verb and no more. It pays on Memory because a
name comes home and on Dominion because ground is taken back. What it does not show is the
grief. An Echo is a purse, and reclaiming empties it into the settlement on the walk home,
and that is what pays for the rite you hold next. The board records the credit and never
the debt.

**Day 19.** Maren Verren went out at noon and came back in the middle of the afternoon with
two weapons and a dead woman's hands to wash. The pool went from forty-two to eighty-seven
before she reached the gate.

### 2. `echo_abandoned`

Memory -5 · Mercy -3 · Communion -2 · Dominion -1
*Left the Echo of %s to grow.*

Eleven down across the four, none of it steeply, which is the dishonesty in it. A small
entry for the largest available mistake. What it costs is not on this line, it is on the
line four days later, and the board cannot put the two together. It is the only verb that
records a decision not to act.

**Day 130.** Tarn Stonewake came up the cart track in the Brackwood clearing with lime on
his back and one flask left, four hours from the gate, and the ring was six paces and at
fourth growth. He went round. Every Warden I have asked says he was right.

### 3. `echo_raided`

Memory -8 · Mercy -6 · Communion -4 · Dominion -5
*The Echo of %s reached the palisade.*

Twenty-three across the four, the heaviest single entry on the board in either direction.
It is written after the fact, always, because nobody is standing at the board while it
happens. It is also the only entry the whole Hold sees with its own eyes.

**Day 134.** It came up the mere road and put the Ashblade into the timber twice at head
height and used the blade as a step. Nothing else in the Vale has ever done that.

### 4. `vigil_held`

Memory +8 · Mercy +4 · Communion +5 · Dominion 0
*Held a %s.*

The only verb whose subject is a rite and not a person, so the board does not say who it
was for. Dominion does not move: a funeral takes no ground and gives none back. And the
five numbers are the same for a Burial that costs twenty of grief and a Wake that costs
sixty. The board prices the holding of a rite and never the price of it, which is how a
hold can read well on Memory for a season and be unable to bury anybody.

**Day 9.** Oda Ashfall, whole, in the ground, with her knife, because the knife was part of
the grip. The first entry this Hold ever cut.

### 5. `vigil_skipped`

Memory -3 · Mercy 0 · Communion -2 · Dominion +2
*Set out with the dead unburied.*

Mercy does not move. That is the strangest nothing on the board and I have never heard it
defended. Dominion goes up, because a wielder who walks out past a body in the house is
holding ground that day instead of tending it. The cheapest entry available, and the one
that appears thirty times running on the only board anybody has read out of a finished
hold.

**Day 128.** He went out at first light and the Hearth was dark and he did not see the
board. Nobody stopped him. There was no rite he owed her.

### 6. `wielder_died`

Memory +2 · Mercy 0 · Communion 0 · Dominion -3
*%s fell.*

Two words and four numbers, and the flattest line on the board. Mercy and Communion do not
move, which is right: nobody was spared and no gate was opened. Dominion drops three
because the Vale took a body back. The two on Memory is argued at the foot of this leaf.

**Day 209.** Wren Ashfall broke a Gravewarden's packing at the ninth ring and went down on
one knee from the ash and not from the blow.

### 7. `successor_taken`

Memory 0 · Mercy 0 · Communion +2 · Dominion +1
*%s took up the blade.*

Nothing on Memory, which surprises people who expect a Succession to be about remembering.
It is not. It is a staffing decision held at a funeral. Two on Communion because three
names are called in front of everybody and the calling is public. One on Dominion because
the Hold is holding the ground another season.

**Day 209.** Three names were called, because three names are always called. Two were
declined by the people who held them. The third was the Teller.

### 8. `villager_conscripted`

Memory -1 · Mercy -4 · Communion -3 · Dominion +5
*Pulled %s from their work to fight.*

It takes a pair of hands out of the work and puts them in a kit that does not fit, and the
board notices the kit and not the hands. What it costs is never the Warden. It is the trade
that stops. The five is argued at the foot of this leaf.

**Day 95.** Colm Grey pulled Asha Vane out of the Carvers' shed on the morning of her
wedding. The Hold had four Carvers and from that morning it had three, and thirty-nine days
later the north-east arc of the wall was still timber, and a fifth growth came over the
timber.

### 9. `enemy_spared`

Memory 0 · Mercy +6 · Communion +2 · Dominion -3
*Walked past %s without drawing.*

Six is the largest movement on Mercy outside the ally verbs. Two on Communion, because
sparing a thing that carries hold is an argument about whose business the dead are. Three
off Dominion, because you have left something standing on ground you claim. Memory does not
move: the board does not care whether you knew its name.

**Day 53.** A wielder walked past a Gravewarden that had said her mother's name out loud.
The Cull have raised that day at every turn of the season since and they are not wrong to.

### 10. `enemy_slain`

Memory 0 · Mercy -1 · Communion 0 · Dominion +2
*Put down %s.*

The smallest entry on the board and the one cut most often in most valleys. One off, two
on, and nothing else, and the subject may be anything at all: a Stalker in the rows, a Husk
in the ditch, a thing in a barrow, or your own sick. The numbers multiply with the count.

**Day 12.** Two Cinder Stalkers in Wrenfield, entered as one line and doubled. Minus two of
Mercy and plus four of Dominion.

### 11. `relic_spent`

Memory +5 · Mercy 0 · Communion +3 · Dominion +1
*Spent a relic on stone that will outlast you.*

The only sentence on the board that addresses the person cutting it. Mercy is flat, because
a relic was already somebody and spending it is neither kind nor cruel to them. The board
cannot tell a relic set under your own Monument from a relic prised out of somebody else's
corner. Five is five either way. A hold three valleys south did the second thing eleven
times over two seasons, fifty-five of Memory and thirty-three of Communion, and they will
tell you it was the most pious season they ever had, and they are not lying.

**Day 19.** Ketil Brack had carried it on the Smithy shelf for six days and did not know
what to do with it. Sila Carver told him to put it under the stone. He asked why.

### 12. `region_saved`

Memory +4 · Mercy +7 · Communion +6 · Dominion +2
*Pushed the decay back from %s.*

Nineteen across the four, the second heaviest positive entry. Seven on Mercy, because you
have given ground back a thing it was owed. The verb hides the labour completely: one line
for eleven days of the whole Hold, every relic on the shelf, and lime off a scarp that had
killed two wielders by then.

**Day 163.** Wrenfield came back from Gone. Day 152 stands beside it in the same column.
The board does not average them.

### 13. `region_lost`

Memory -4 · Mercy -7 · Communion -5 · Dominion -4
*%s went to ash.*

Twenty down. The difficulty with this line is that nobody does it. Every other negative
verb has a hand behind it. This one is cut for three seasons of nobody walking out, and the
Teller has to choose a day for a thing that had no day, and the day chosen is the day a
Forager came back and said it out loud at the board.

**Day 152.** Wrenfield, all of it, from the last fence to the cut bank, with an eleven-pace
circle of Quick grass in the middle of it and a stone in the middle of that.

### 14. `ally_revived`

Memory +2 · Mercy +8 · Communion +9 · Dominion -2
*Lifted %s off the ground.*

Nine is the largest positive figure on Communion, tied with one other verb. Two off
Dominion, because you stopped holding ground for as long as it took. What the board does not
record is which valley the other body came from. It pays the same for your own and for a
stranger, and it is the only verb of the eighteen that does.

**Day 180.** Orrin Dunn and Tove Rowan were in the same field on the same afternoon. She
went down. He lifted her off the ground. Her account differs from his in three particulars
and both stand.

### 15. `ally_abandoned`

Memory -2 · Mercy -8 · Communion -10 · Dominion +3
*Left %s where they fell.*

The ten and the three are both argued at the foot of this leaf. What belongs here is the
other fact: Ashfall Hold never cut this line, not once in two hundred and ten days and ten
wielders. I have been through the leaves twice. It is one of the two verbs of the eighteen
we never used, and the other is two entries below it.

**Their day 61.** From a copy out of four valleys west, two lines in sequence:
`ally_abandoned`, and on the following day `vigil_held`, a Telling, for the same name. Minus
ten and then plus five. The hold stood five short of where it had been the morning before.

### 16. `echo_sent`

Memory +3 · Mercy -2 · Communion +6 · Dominion +4
*Sent the Echo of %s drifting into another settlement.*

Six on Communion and four on Dominion for an act that is not an act. Nobody sends an Echo.
It goes, because nobody went out to it for long enough, and it keeps going, and then it is
somebody else's business. Two off Mercy is the board being honest about that. Three on
Memory is the board being generous about it. I do not accept the three and I entered it
anyway, because the weights are not mine.

**Day 184.** Nix Ember left the Vale. The board read The Wake that evening while he was
walking in somebody else's barley two ridges east.

### 17. `foreign_echo_slain`

Memory +1 · Mercy -3 · Communion +4 · Dominion +6
*Put down a stranger's Echo: %s.*

Six is the largest figure on Dominion anywhere on the board. The four on Communion is the
line that stops every argument in this Hearth, and it is argued at the foot of this leaf.
This Hold never cut it.

**Their day 118.** From the copy three valleys south: a stranger's Echo came out of the ash
at a ditch line and was put down by two Wardens who could not read the name on its gear.
Their Teller cut the name as it stood on the plate, letter for letter, in a hand he did not
have, and the board took four on Communion for it.

### 18. `foreign_echo_honoured`

Memory +6 · Mercy +7 · Communion +9 · Dominion -1
*Buried a stranger's Echo: %s.*

Twenty-one across the four and the heaviest positive entry on the board. It costs a grave, a
pane, and the pearl out of a pyre you lit for somebody else. The board pays more for a
stranger than for your own: `echo_reclaimed` is twelve across the four and this is
twenty-one, and nobody in this Hold has been able to say why, and the Open Road say it is
obvious. The same verb also carries the reverse, which took me a while to notice. Our own
man buried in another valley came onto our board under this line, for something we did not
do.

**Day 70.** Sila Verren drifted into the Vale out of a valley we had never seen and was
buried in our ground, in our glass, under pearl out of her own ash. Her pane is in the west
range and it is cut better than most of ours.

## The arguments

Four of the weights above are argued at every turn of the season, here and in every copy I
have. I am going to set the arguments down and not settle them.

**Why putting down a stranger's Echo raises Communion by four.**

Communion asks whether another lineage's dead are your business. Put that way, the four is
not strange. It is the answer yes. You went out to a thing that came from a valley you have
never walked in, you dealt with it, you cut its name into your board in a hand you do not
have, a Wayfarer copied the line, and somewhere a Teller who never met you entered it and
moved four numbers he cannot check. That is contact between holds. The board does not ask
whether the contact was kind. Mercy takes three because the thing was owed a rite and did
not get one. Dominion takes six because you now hold ground another valley's dead were
standing on.

So the board will pay a hold toward The Open Hearth for killing somebody else's dead. I
have put this to four Tellers and had four answers. The Open Road's answer is the only one
that does not flinch: the four is right, they say, because the alternative is not mercy, it
is never having gone out at all, and a valley that will not touch another valley's dead
stands at minus forty-four with a hundred and sixteen people at its looms.

I do not find that comfortable and I do not find it wrong. I have entered
`foreign_echo_honoured` twice and `foreign_echo_slain` never, and that is not virtue. Both
of ours arrived in summer, when we had a spade and a pane and four hours.

**Why a wielder falling raises Memory by two.**

Because the death enters the name.

Before the fall a wielder is a person doing a job, and some hands never write the name at
all. *%s fell* is the line that puts a name where the other verbs can reach it. After it
you can cut a reclaiming, hold a Telling, raise a Monument, set a pane. Two is not a reward
for dying. It is a book noticing that it has acquired a subject it did not have.

Nine times in this Chronicle the numbers went two up and three down on one line, and the
three down is the Vale taking a body back and the two up is the board taking one on. Memory
came to a hundred on Day 163 and stood there to the last page, forty-seven days. Nine
entries fell inside those forty-seven days that would have raised it and none of them
could, and three of the nine were wielders falling.

The board is more use to this Hold after somebody dies than before. This is the entry where
that stops being a figure of speech.

**Why leaving somebody where they fell is the worst line available.**

Ten off Communion is the largest single figure on the board in either direction on any of
the four axes. A raid at the palisade takes four off Communion. A region lost takes five.
By the sum, `echo_raided` is worse, twenty-three against seventeen, and by the axis that
matters it is not close.

Communion is the axis that gates The Wake and the Wake's floor is forty. One
`ally_abandoned` is a quarter of that floor. It takes two rites held to put it back, and a
rite costs grief, and the grief for this death is sitting out in a field because nobody
fetched the body.

And then the three on Dominion. The board pays you three for it. You kept your footing, you
held the ground, you came home with the lime. That is the coldest arithmetic in the
eighteen, and I have found the verb on nine of the eleven boards I have copied and not once
without something written beside it in the same hand.

**Why a conscription gives five on Dominion.**

It is the only five on that axis. There is one six, on `foreign_echo_slain`, and this Hold
never cut that, so five is the most Dominion Ashfall Hold ever bought with one line.

Dominion asks whether the Vale is something you hold or something you are in, and a
conscription is the purest available answer: a pair of hands belongs to the holding and not
to the work. The board pays five and takes eight away across the other three, and the eight
does not feel like eight on the morning.

Vane Hold used this verb nine times in nine days, their Day 37 to their Day 45, and their
Dominion came to fifty-one, the highest figure in any copy anybody has. Ashfall Hold used it
once, on Day 95, and reached eleven, the highest we ever went, and held it four days. The
Long Hold needs fifty. The distance between this Hold and that ending is one Carver's
wedding morning, eight more times.

## The verbs that are not there

**There is no verb for hospitality.** Three Wayfarers at our crossing post on Day 25. A
road-shrine proposed at Vane Hold on Day 101 and refused because we had three Carvers and a
wall to finish. Nothing went on the board either time, in either direction. Holt Vale did
the second thing in another form and got an ending for it, and every point of it came
through `foreign_echo_honoured`, which requires a corpse. The board will pay twenty-one for
burying a stranger and nothing at all for feeding one.

**There is no verb for barring a gate.** The single most consequential act in the history of
any settlement anybody has a copy of moved the board by zero, in four directions. Their
Teller wrote it in words four times on one leaf because the board would not carry it. Their
Day 58: *Timber up. Two arms. Brackets held. I asked whether we were going to enter this and
was told there is no verb for it.* Their board went on being kept for thirty more days as
though nothing had happened, and by its own accounting nothing had.

**There is no verb for would not go.** Twice on those leaves, their Day 31 and their Day 55,
in a hand still level. The board has `wielder_died` and `successor_taken` and nothing
between them for a person who takes up a blade and then stands inside a gate holding it.

**There is no verb for a birth.** Nine of the thirty-one people in this Hold on Day 209 were
children and not one of them is anywhere on our board. Vane Hold's roll does not add: a
hundred and forty-one at the wall's beginning, twenty put down in the huts, four wielders
dead outside the ditch, which leaves a hundred and seventeen, and Asha Vane counted a
hundred and sixteen twice and wrote it once. The difference is one. It may be a Teller who
did not count the board as a chore. It may be a birth. Their count ran eighty-eight days and
their calendar ran a great deal longer than that, and a hold of a hundred and forty-one
would have had children in it, and none of that is on the board. I record the one and I
leave it open.

**There is no verb for handing the blade to somebody still alive.** The unsigned fragment,
seven pages, no hold name, found folded into a Wayfarer's copy as packing: a wielder held
the blade a hundred and forty days and then handed it to somebody who was standing up.
`successor_taken` will not carry that, because on every board in every copy
`successor_taken` follows `wielder_died`, and there was no death. So the fragment says it in
a sentence and the board says nothing. That is the commonest ending in the world and there is
no line for the thing that makes it.

**There is no verb for a cull.** Eighteen verbs and not one of them is for putting down your
own sick, so it goes in as `enemy_slain`, one off Mercy and two on Dominion, once for each or
once for eleven. Their Day 46: eleven people, one line. Their Day 53: nine people, one line.
The hand did not change its letters. The board offered nothing else and their Teller used
what was there.

**There is no verb for having looked at something terrible and left it alone.** Three people
cannot bury a hundred and sixteen. Most holds bar the corner and walk home and enter nothing,
and nothing is not a neutral act on a board that only knows what a hand put in it.

**There is no verb for water.** Two rites attempted on Kettlemere and two failures, the
second of them three of a Healer's fingers for nothing at all. The Healers asked for both to
be copied forward with the dates and the fingers intact and nobody opposed the motion. Low
Kettle is under the water and its dead are in the Holding at a depth no Teller could survey,
so they were never entered, so the board never knew about them.

**There is no verb for beginning a second book.** It costs nothing, it moves no number, and
every person in the Hold turned up.

· · ·

I have put these together because they are one fact and not nine.

The board can only be honest about what somebody had a verb for.

It is not a liar. Bran Holt is right about that and I have never found a case against him.
Every number on it was put there by a hand, and every hand entered what it could reach, and
the reaching is the whole of it. Four numbers and a fifth line, and the fifth line names an
ending, and the ending is worked out of eighteen holes of a particular shape.

A gate barred. A stranger fed. A child born. A blade handed over while both of you were
standing up. A village under a lake. A hold with no name cut anywhere. A second book begun on
an afternoon I have refused to date.

None of it moved a number. All of it happened.

Eighteen verbs. There have only ever been eighteen. I have looked.
