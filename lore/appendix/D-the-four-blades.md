# Appendix D: The Four Blades

> *Four. There will not be a fifth while I have the fire, and if there is a
> fifth it will not be a sword, because this Hold does not want another sword.
> It wants another Carver.*
> Recorded: the first page of the Smith's book, Ashfall Smithy, in Ketil Brack's hand

**Hand:** Ketil Brack, Smith, thirty-eight in the first year, writing across a good many summers. The notes in the second hand are Grieve Mourne's, added later and marked where he could mark them.
**Kept:** On the shelf at the cold end of the Smithy, under the tally of hinges. It has been wet twice. The figures at the front were cut for me by Bran Holt because I write slowly, and everything after them is mine.

## What the numbers do to a body

I am going to set the figures down first and then say what each one does, because the Wardens have been handed figures for twenty years and nobody has told them what they are holding.

**Power.** What the stroke is worth when it lands. Nothing else.

**Reach.** How far away you are when you are in it. Compare it against the thing you are going out to meet, and do it at the bench and not in the grass. A Husk is twenty-two. A Stalker is twenty. A Gravewarden is thirty.

**Arc.** How many bodies the stroke crosses if they are standing badly. Under one is one body and it will not be two however they stand. Near two is two, and the head does not care what it hits first.

**Startup.** The time between your deciding and the edge being where it can do work. A tenth of a second sounds like nothing to a man reading it off a page. It is the whole difference between the Ashblade and the Maul. Two smiths in two valleys have written to me asking why the difference is not larger. The answer is that a tenth of a second is the largest thing there is.

**Active.** The part that lands. Short in all four. Nobody has ever complained to me about it.

**Recovery.** The time after the stroke in which you are not able to do anything at all. This is the number that kills people and it is the number nobody asks me about. A fifth of a second is nothing until the moment when it is the only thing there is. Rin Ashfall was inside a fifth of a second when the second Stalker came from her right on the fourteenth day.

**Cost.** What the stroke takes out of the purse. The yard will tell you how many strokes you have and the field will tell you fewer, and the field is right. Maren Verren had nine of the Maul in my yard and four of it inside her wife's ring on the nineteenth day, on the same afternoon, with the same arms. Nobody should be handed a weapon by me without being told that.

**Poise.** What the stroke does to the other thing's packing. This is the column the Wardens never read and it is the one I would put first if the board were mine. You cannot kill a packed thing. You can only open it. Every figure in this book that matters is in this column.

## The Ashblade

Forged in two days out of a plough coulter and a cart's iron, in the first year. I was not proud of it then and I am not proud of it now, and I have left that sentence in through three copyings because the Hold keeps trying to take it out.

I could not hammer the coulter's curve out of it. I had two days. What the curve does is put the edge a hair wider than it should be coming off the downstroke. That is a fault. I would have corrected it if I had been given a week.

On the fourteenth day that hair went in under the jaw of the third Stalker at Wrenfield and killed it. It is still in the blade. The blade has been on my anvil four times since and I have taken nothing off it.

**What the numbers do.** Sixteen of power, which is three light strokes into a Husk and three into a Stalker. Twenty-six of reach, which is four clear of a Husk, six clear of a Stalker, and four short of a Gravewarden.

A tenth of a second of wind-up going light. Nothing else in the Vale is at a tenth except the kitchen knife. Two tenths of recovery, and that is the figure to say aloud to a new wielder: after every light stroke you are furniture for a fifth of a second.

Twelve of cost, which is eight strokes out of a hundred in the yard and fewer in the ring.

Fourteen of poise going light, and this is what I would teach first. Fourteen is over a Stalker's twelve. One light stroke of this blade opens a Stalker, and nothing else in this book does it in one at light. Going heavy is thirty-four of poise, which is two landings into a Gravewarden's fifty-five, at twenty-eight hundredths of decision and thirty-eight afterwards.

**Who carried it, and for how long.** Rin Ashfall, four days, the eleventh to the fourteenth, first of all of them, bond to nobody. Then it lay out in the grass four days. Then it hung on a hook on my wall from the nineteenth day to the hundred and tenth, which is ninety-one days, and I oiled it and I did not offer it to anybody.

Yeva Holt took it down on the hundred and tenth and carried it twelve days, to the hundred and twenty-first, and it came back over the timber in her hands on the hundred and thirty-fourth. Nix Ember carried it seventeen days, the hundred and fifty-sixth to the hundred and seventy-second. Wren Ashfall carried it thirteen, the hundred and ninety-seventh to the two hundred and ninth.

Four wielders, forty-six days between the four of them. Maren Verren had sixty-nine on the post-driver by herself. I have written that down and I am not drawing a conclusion from it. The Hold may draw its own.

> *Grieve Mourne, fourth hand.* Five wielders. I had it from the two hundred and ninth day and I am the only one of us who did not fall. At the ninth ring it came back to my hand while I was already holding it, which no other wielder reported and which I do not understand and am not going to explain. I have left Ketil's forty-six days uncorrected, because the arithmetic in it is his.

**Which bond suits it.** All of them and none of them, which is why it is the blade a successor is handed by default, and why *taking the blade* means succession in every sentence anybody speaks in this valley, including over the two who never carried a sword.

The honest answer off the figures is that it suits a Rival best and a Spouse worst. A Rival carries ten in the hundred more purse, and this blade wants strokes, eight of them in the yard, and a Rival's purse does not go dear standing in the dead one's ring, because the ring holds nothing of his. Nix Ember was a Rival and wanted the blade and said so out loud, which no candidate had done before.

A Spouse carries fifteen in the hundred less, and will spend it faster than she owns it, and gets the bill inside the same minute. Both widows in this lineage carried other things first and this one last.

**What is bound into it.** Nothing. Nothing will be bound into it while I have the fire, and that is not superstition, it is the same reason I give at the end of this book for refusing two.

This is the blade every successor is handed. Put one hand's motion into it and you hand the next person a stranger's habit at the worst hour of their life. Let it stay a sword with a fault in it.

A Field-hand's tool with the head reforged. I did the head and left the shaft alone, because the shaft was already right and a smith who cannot leave a thing alone is a cost to his settlement.

Forty of reach. There is nothing else in this Vale within fourteen of it. Six tenths of arc, which is one body and will be one body however they stand.

This is the weapon of somebody who has decided never to close with anything again. I am not going to dress that up. Colm Grey came to me for it on the eighty-fifth day and told me what he wanted it for in one sentence and the sentence was about distance.

**What the numbers do.** Forty against a Gravewarden's thirty. You are in it and it is not in you, and that is the only place in this Vale where both of those are true at once. Colm Grey put down two Gravewardens and this is how he did it.

Thirteen of power is the second lowest here, four strokes into a Husk and three into a Stalker. Ten of poise going light is under a Stalker's twelve, so a light stroke will not open one, and any Warden taking this into tall grass should be told that at the gate and not afterwards. Twenty-six going heavy is three landings into fifty-five.

Fourteen hundredths of wind-up going light, four more than the sword, and twenty-four of recovery, four more again. It is slower at both ends than a sword and it does not matter, because you should not be inside anything's reach while you are holding it. If you are, the figures in this entry stop describing your afternoon.

**Which bond suits it.** A Rival first. Ten in the hundred more purse, a tenth off Resolve, and the dead one's ring does not pull at him at all. That last part is the whole of it. A man who will not close can be made to close by a ring holding a piece of him, and a Rival has nothing in the ring. Colm Grey stood in Maren Verren's ring on the ninety-first day at his full purse and took her up, and he had disliked her for eleven years about a fence.

A Friend second. Nothing changes about the body, Resolve goes up a twentieth, and the Vigils come cheap. And a man with a shoulder should carry the longest thing in the Vale.

**Who carried it.** Colm Grey, twenty-five days, the eighty-fifth to the hundred and ninth, Rival to Maren Verren, Grim and Steady, killed in the Grey Ash by an Ash Husk while he was out of everything.

Orrin Dunn, Ketil Dunn's son, who had the eastern furlong off Bran Holt, twenty-four days, the hundred and seventy-third to the hundred and ninety-sixth, Friend to Nix Ember, Steady and Grim, killed at our own palisade.

**What is bound into it.** Maren Verren.

Her relic came out of the ring on the ninety-first day and sat on my shelf a long time. I was asked to put it into this Pike before the hundred and seventy-third and I did it, and I want my objection written next to the work, because the objection was correct and I did the work anyway.

A relic does not care what shape you put it in. Steel takes the motion the dead knew. The motion Maren Verren knew is a post-driver's, at twenty-four of reach and forty-six hundredths of commitment, and this thing is forty long and comes off at fourteen. I said at the bench that a man would be handed a habit that finishes a good way short of where his weapon does. They asked anyway. Orrin Dunn had watched her fight in this yard when he was a boy and asked for it himself, and that is the part I could not argue with, and I have never settled whether it was a reason or whether I wanted one.

> *Grieve Mourne.* He carried it twenty-four days and I watched him in the yard for twenty-three of them and wrote down what he did, because I am a Teller and that is what I am for. He stepped in on the trailing side and came round. Every time. I do not know whether he took that from the relic, or from the woman, or from having been nine years old at a fence, and Ketil is not here to be asked and would not have given me a straight answer.
>
> It matters more than anything else in this book. On the two hundred and tenth day the thing in the ninth ring had Wren Ashfall's shoulders and Orrin Dunn's habit, because she had taken him up on the two hundred and third and carried his motion north. It stepped in on the trailing side and came round. I knew where it would be because I had written it down twenty-three times. I went in on the fourth exchange, on the trailing side, and it went down properly.
>
> Ketil Brack's objection was correct. It was also the reason I am alive to copy it.

A post-driver, reforged in a day and a half on the fifteenth and sixteenth days.

I made the post-driver in the first week, for driving palisade posts. A squared head on a long ash haft, and the head was iron, and it was the heaviest thing in the Vale a person could pick up and swing once. When Maren Verren would not have another sword made and took me out to the yard and pointed at it, I told her a post-driver cannot be recovered from. I said that as a smith and not as a Warden, because there were no Wardens then, only bodies with opinions.

I was right. I made it anyway.

I drew the head down. I dished the striking face. And I set the head on the haft with a wedge instead of a pin, so that a wielder can reseat it in the field with a stone and her own two hands.

That wedge is the first piece of design in this Vale that was made because somebody was expected to be out for days. Everything I had built before it was built for a person who would be back by dark. Whoever has this book after me should notice that, because it is the only thing in here I am actually proud of, and it is a wedge.

It has been reset nine times. You can count the seatings in the haft. Four of the nine are Bel Brack's. He was seventeen when he died and he had been seating it since his fourth summer, and a badly seated wedge is worse than a pin, and he never once seated it badly.

**What the numbers do.** Forty-six hundredths of a second from the decision to the landing, going heavy, and fifty-two hundredths afterwards in which the world may do as it likes with you.

I will write out what that means, because the number does not convey it. You commit to the swing while looking at something that is not yet where it will be when the head arrives. You are not permitted to change your mind. Nothing in this Vale has ever taught a person more about their own character. I have watched four people find it out and two of them were unhappy about what they found.

Against that: twenty-four of power, the highest here by eight, which is two strokes into a Husk and two into a Stalker. An arc near two, which takes two bodies if they are standing badly.

And sixty of poise going heavy, against a Gravewarden's fifty-five. One landing. One. There is nothing else in this Vale that opens a bulwark in one and there will not be unless somebody brings me better iron than the scarp has in it.

Twenty-four of reach against a Gravewarden's thirty, which is the other half of the same sentence. You are inside its reach before you are inside your own. Both people who carried this weapon died on Stonewake Scarp to a Gravewarden, in the same fifty feet, and the Hold still sends bodies up there because the lime is under the barrows and the fields want lime. I have thought about that arithmetic more than I have thought about the iron.

**The short edge.** I ground a short edge into the trailing side and I did not ask and I did not explain it. I am not going to explain it here either. It saved her twice in sixty-nine days. She never mentioned it to me and I never asked her.

> *Grieve Mourne.* It saved me on the two hundred and tenth day and I did not have the thing in my hands. I went in on the trailing side because that is the side Ketil Brack ground, on the sixteenth day, without being asked. The habit of going in there has been in this Hold for twenty years and it came out of a piece of work nobody requested and nobody paid for. I have read this page eleven times. I think the reason he never explained it is that he made it for a widow on the second day of her widowhood and could not have said so out loud in that yard in that month.

**Which bond suits it.** A Spouse. I did not know that when I made it and the figures say it plainly now.

A Spouse carries fifteen in the hundred less purse, which sounds like the wrong body for the most expensive stroke in the Vale, and it is not, because this weapon asks fewer strokes than anything else in this book. What a Spouse has is a quarter more Resolve, and the whole of this weapon is not being moved off a commitment you made forty-six hundredths ago. And she hits harder standing in the dead one's ring, which is where she will be standing, because that is where the work is.

Maren Verren. Sixty-nine days, the fifteenth to the eighty-fourth, longer than anybody will hold anything again. Spouse. Steady and Scarred. Hands did not shake, which people mentioned before they mentioned anything else about her. She would take a blow in order to finish a stroke and do it again the next day.

A Stranger second, and the Hold will not like reading that. Fifteen in the hundred more purse, a fifth off Resolve, no grief at all inside the palisade. Tarn Stonewake took it off the wall on the hundred and twenty-second day and the Hold read that as arrogance and it was the exact opposite: he took the weapon that punishes hesitation because he had none to punish. On the hundred and thirty-fourth day, on cart ground, inside our own gate, against a thing with no packing on it but a dead woman's coat, he committed this swing thirty-one times in about an hour and landed nine of them and drank four flasks. Nineteen days, the hundred and twenty-second to the hundred and fortieth. Killed by a Gravewarden on Stonewake Scarp, in the same fifty feet as the woman the thing was made for.

**What is bound into it.** Nothing.

The first relic in this Vale sat on my shelf six days, from the thirteenth to the nineteenth. I could have put it in this head. Sila Carver told me to put it under a stone instead. I asked her why. She gave me half an answer and said she had no evidence for the other half, and said it anyway, and I did it.

It is at Wrenfield, holding eleven paces of Quick grass in the middle of Gone ground.

I am glad of it. On the nineteenth day this weapon was five days old and I did not yet know what it did.

It has hung on the wall above the Hearth since the hundred and fortieth day. The wedge in it is the one I cut.

A kitchen tool. It came in on a cart. I have re-edged it eleven times, I did not make it, I have never made another like it, and I would not.

It is in this book because Sable Kettle carried nothing else, and because of a thing about days that I have put at the end of the entry.

**What the numbers do.** Six hundredths of a second from the decision to the edge. Six. Nothing else in this Vale is inside eighteen.

Eight of cost, the cheapest stroke there is, which is twelve strokes out of a hundred in the yard. Fourteen hundredths of recovery, the shortest there is.

What all of that buys is the only thing it buys, and it is enough: she never had to commit to anything longer than a quarter of a second. The Wardens spent a week trying to make her take a real weapon. They were reading the power column. The power column says nine, which is six strokes into a Husk and four into a Stalker, and it is the worst figure in this book, and it was not the figure she was living on.

Six of poise going light, which will not open anything in one. Sixteen going heavy, which is four landings into a Gravewarden's fifty-five, and four landings at eighteen hundredths of wind-up each is a long afternoon. On the hundred and forty-seventh day she went up the scarp and took up Tarn Stonewake with this thing and it took her forty minutes. Forty. Somebody at the gate counted, which is why the Chronicle has the figure, and no Warden in this Hold has ever heard it read out without going quiet.

Eighteen of reach against a Husk's twenty-two and a Stalker's twenty. She was inside both of them at all times. There was no version of her fighting that happened at a distance, and there was no afternoon of hers that could have been survived by anybody slower.

**Which bond suits it.** A Friend. Nothing changes about the body, Resolve goes up a twentieth, and the Vigils come cheap, and the Vigils are the point of it. A Friend pays four in five. Nobody knows why. It was first noticed on the hundred and forty-first day, when she buried Tarn Stonewake and the board came up short of what the arithmetic said it should.

**Who carried it.** Sable Kettle. Fifteen days, the hundred and forty-first to the hundred and fifty-fifth. Friend. Quick and Tender. Twenty-four years old. Killed by Cinder Stalkers in Wrenfield as it went Gone.

Fifteen days with a kitchen tool. Rin Ashfall had four days with a sword. Yeva Holt had twelve with the same sword.

> *Grieve Mourne.* Wren Ashfall had thirteen with the same sword again. That makes three, and the sentence the Hold says now is exact and I have checked it against the board twice: she lived longer than three people who carried better. I would rather it were not exact.

**What is bound into it.** Nothing. I would not know where to put it.

## The refusal

I have been asked four times to put two relics into one blade. I said no four times. I am writing the reason down here so nobody has to ask me a fifth.

There are two reasons and the first one is the iron.

A relic goes into a bar at welding heat and nowhere else. To put in a second you must bring the same bar back to welding heat, and the first binding is sitting in that heat, and it will not survive being asked twice. I tried it in the yard on a billet with nothing anybody knew in either piece, because I was not going to find this out on a weapon somebody was going to carry past the fence. It came out of the fire with a fault in it you could see from the door. I put the fault on the shelf. It is still there. Anybody who wants to argue with me can pick it up first.

The second reason is the hand.

Steel takes a motion. A hand can learn a motion. A hand cannot learn two motions that finish in different places and then choose between them inside a quarter of a second. Give a wielder a blade that remembers two people and you have not given her twice as much. You have given her a hesitation, and you have put it in the one place where hesitation is fatal, which is inside the tell.

The Wardens will tell you that six tenths of a second is the longest warning anything in this valley gives you, and that people die inside it because six tenths is long enough to decide twice. I make the things they die holding. I am not going to build the second decision into the iron.

That is a smith's reason and not a Teller's. It is the only kind I have.

There is no verb on the board for any of this. There is one for setting a relic under stone and there is not one for putting it into steel, and I have asked about that twice and been told the board has eighteen verbs and has always had eighteen. So none of the work in this book is on the board. Four blades, and the only entry any of them ever earned was the day somebody took one up.

> *Grieve Mourne, on the day I began the second book.* Four blades and nine graves. I have copied this page as it stands, including the sentence about the billet. The billet is still on the shelf. I have picked it up. He was right about the fault.
