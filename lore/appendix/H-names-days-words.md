## 3. The Words

The glossary, in the Vale's own voice, with no modern equivalents supplied.

**Hold.** The grip a life had on the world. The habits, the gear, the dip in the doorstone. What survives a person. Three postures: Kept by the dead (Echo), unspent (grief), Given to the living (Legacy).

**Echo.** The grip walking. The dead person's grip closed with nobody inside. Tier one is the second day unclaimed. A tier every two world-days. At tier three leaves the death site and roams. At tier five comes to the palisade. Always called by the name of the person.

**Husk.** A villager whose hold was never taken up. Walks the last chore. Husks in fields reap. Husks in the Hearth set a table.

**Grief.** Hold that has been released and not yet given. Sits in the pool like standing water. Suppresses work at the ratio of one to two hundred. The well-loved cost more.

**Composure.** How much of your own hold you can spend before your grip opens. Set by where you are standing and who you are standing near. Higher at the Memorial. Lower near the Echo of someone who loved you.

**Poise.** Borrowed hold worn on the body. Heavy armour is heavy because it is packed. Break the packing and the wearer is briefly only a person.

**The Vigil.** A machine that moves hold from one posture to another. Five: Burial, Pyre, Telling, Monument, Wake.

**The Legacy.** What a Vigil gives. On every wielder who comes after, forever, and cannot be lost.

**Relic.** A dense knuckle of matter, warm, wrong to hold. The grief of a reclaimed Echo, hardened in the moment of release. Bound into steel, the steel remembers a motion. Set under stone, the stone holds ground.

**Pearl.** Pot-ash refined again. Finer white powder. Goes into the sand at the Smithy's hot end. Makes the glass run at a heat a wood fire can reach. The flux in the pane-cut.

**Lye.** Pyre-ash steeped in water for three days. Drawn off clear. Soap is what the soap-makers make with it. Pot-ash is what the Smithy makes with it.

**Fall-ash.** Ash that fell from the Mourne before the Hold. A hand's depth of grey over everything. Grows the best barley anyone has seen. Locks in the fifth season. Fixed with bone, burnt shell, and lye from the pyres.

**Drawn-ash.** What ground becomes where hold has been drawn out and never put back. An Echo feeds on it. Same colour as fall-ash. Foragers tell them apart by feel and by what grows.

**Locked ground.** The fourth and fifth season after a fall-ash. The ground's own goodness has bound itself past the roots' reach. Fixed with bone, burnt shell, lye.

**Drift.** The board's reading. *Drifting toward* plus the name of an ending. Checked at the turn of every season.

**The board.** Six feet of planed ash on the north wall of the Hearth. Four axes. One fifth line. Eighteen verbs. Five endings.

**The panes.** The name-glass at the Memorial. Each pane fluxed with pearl from the pyre of the named. A pane is a name written in the substance of the named.

**The Readings.** The four axes. Memory against Oblivion, Mercy against Ruin, Communion against Isolation, Dominion against Surrender.

**The Interval.** The days between the Loosing and the Keeping. Closed and unclaimed. Its length is the argument.

**The Loosing.** The person leaves. Nobody does this; it happens.

**The Holding.** The interval. The hold is closed and nobody's. Dangerous. Its length is the argued measure of *too long*, which in Ashfall Hold was nine days because Oda fell on day six and was Kept on day nine.

**The Keeping.** The rite. The hold is opened and shared out.

**Going north.** Walking into the Ash Orchard and sitting down. What the Yielding does. The strongest argument in the book.

**Route-mark.** Six characters cut into a post at a crossing. Means a hold this way is keeping a board you can write on.

**Tell.** The window between deciding and arriving. A Gravewarden's tell is six tenths of a second. A Stalker's tell is a quarter of a second. A Husk's tell is forty-five hundredths.

**Tier.** Two world-days unclaimed, a tier. The Echo's level. Five tiers is at the palisade.

**The ring.** The bent grass around an Echo. Pales the root at the rim. Three paces on day one, eleven by the fourth. A reclaiming stands the ring back up. A Monument's stone holds its own eleven paces.

**The Wake.** The fifth Vigil. The whole Hold drinks. Clears grief. The hardest ending to reach.

**The Quiet Field.** The ending that always matches. What the board reads when nothing else does. The commonest ending in the world.

**Drift toward The Wake.** The board's saying. Housekeeping report. Not a promise. Not a plan.

This is the Words. The Words are what the Words are for.
