# Appendix C: The Bestiary

> *Three things come out of the grass and two of them were never anybody. Learn
> those two first. Then learn the rule about the third, and learn it better than
> anything else said in this room.*
> Recorded: the Wardens' board at the Hearth, said aloud since the twenty-second day

**Hand:** Begun by Bran Holt, second hand, in his first week with the graver. Added to by every Warden after him. The last additions are Orrin Dunn's and the letters run downhill because of the shoulder.
**Kept:** At the Hearth, on the wall to the left of the board. It is not a thing anybody reads. It is said out loud, one animal at a time, to whoever is new, and whoever is new says it back until they have it. Copied into the Chronicle entire by Grieve Mourne, who has not corrected a figure in it.

## The words, before the animals

Six words. Get them right and the rest of this is arithmetic.

**Purse.** What you have to spend. Wardens say Composure and say it flatly, the way a carpenter says plumb. Every swing comes out of it. Every roll comes out of it. Being hit while your packing is low comes out of it. It fills only while you are not swinging, and it does not begin to fill at the moment you stop. There is a wait.

A body starts at a hundred. You will not have a hundred. The yard gives you one number and the field gives you a lower one, and the field is right, and the field is lowest where your own dead are in the ground. Maren Verren had nine swings of the Maul in the yard and four of it inside her wife's ring on the nineteenth day, on the same afternoon, with the same arms. That is not a failure of nerve. That is the Vale.

**Packing.** Poise. Borrowed hold worn on the body. Armour is heavy because it is packed, and a packed thing does not care what you do to it until the packing is broken. Break it and the wearer is briefly only a person. That is the window and there is no other door.

**Tell.** The time between the thing deciding and the thing arriving. Written here in hundredths, because that is how it was measured, and because saying *quick* and *slow* has killed people.

**Reach.** How far its arriving goes. Compare it to the reach of what is in your hand, and do it at the bench, not in the grass.

**Commitment.** A swing is a thing you agree to before it happens. Startup, then the part that lands, then the recovery. You cannot take it back partway. Say that to yourself on the walk out.

**Posture.** One word for how a thing closes. Shambler, darter, bulwark. Every Warden in this Vale is given the three words before being given a spear. What they mean in practice is written under each animal, because the words are useless alone and people have died holding them.

## The Ash Husk

```
health   46
damage    9
poise    20
speed    34
tell      0.45 s
reach    22
posture  shambler
relics    0
```

**What it was.** A villager whose hold was never taken up. That is the whole of it and there is nothing else in it. It walks its last chore. The ones found in the fields carry the motion of reaping. The ones found at the Hearth carry the motion of setting a table. Oda Ashfall stopped on the sixth day and stood up on the eighth and has walked a reaping motion in the west field ever since, and she was buried whole on the ninth with her knife, and no two accounts agree how both of those can be true.

**What it owed.** Nothing. It is owed. The Hearthkeepers say a Husk is a person who was failed, and that you do not owe it a blade, you owe it a rite. The Cull say hold is contagion, and anything carrying it goes down early and goes down cheap, and they say so out loud, and they are the reason this Hold is standing after three Greyings. Both parties are in the room when this page is said aloud, and in all the years it has been said neither has asked for the other's paragraph to be cut.

At Vane Hold on the Vane Road there are one hundred and sixteen. Asha Vane counted them twice. Every one of them is performing an ordinary duty: at looms, in doorways, one at a table with its hands flat on a bench worn to the shape of them, one at a well handle, two in the lane between byre and workshop three feet apart facing each other, eleven on benches in the stone Hearth with a cask. No breach. No burning. No bones in the lanes.

That is what *owed* means on this page. A hundred and sixteen people finished the day they were given and were not given another.

**The tell.** Forty-five hundredths of a second. Nearly half. It is the longest tell of anything that was never anybody, and this paragraph is the longest one on this wall because of it.

What you see is the whole of the motion beginning. The arm going up and going back. The weight settling onto the back foot. The head not following the arm, because the head stopped following the arm on whatever day the person stopped. Forty-five hundredths is long enough to get out of the way twice.

It is also long enough to recognise the motion. That is the worst thing about it and it is the only reason it is dangerous. The motion is a reaping stroke, or a table being set, or a well handle coming round, and it belonged to somebody, and that somebody worked the row you are standing in.

The mistake is not slowness. Nobody is too slow for forty-five hundredths. The mistake is waiting. For a quarter of a second you stop being a Warden and become a person watching Oda Ashfall take the top off a row, and the other quarter is not enough. Say this part twice to whoever is new.

**Practical.** Twenty-two of reach. The Ashblade is twenty-six, so you are four clear. The Hearth Knife is eighteen, so you are four inside, and there is no version of that fight that happens at a distance.

Thirty-four of speed, slower than you walk. You can leave a Husk. You can leave it all day and it will still be coming at dusk.

Forty-six of health: three light strokes of the Ashblade, two of the Maul, four of the Pike, six of the Knife.

Twenty of packing: two light strokes of anything but the Knife opens it, one going heavy.

Nine of damage. Eleven landings leaves you standing with something in you. The twelfth puts you down. Take the sense of that before you get comfortable with it. A Husk cannot kill a wielder who has anything left at all, and this Hold has lost two wielders to Husks. Colm Grey, who had put down two Gravewardens, was killed in the Grey Ash on the hundred and ninth day by a villager walking a chore, because he was out of everything. Nix Ember died in the same ground on the hundred and seventy-second day, out of everything, exactly as he was. The fourth hand notes the repetition and refuses to make it mean anything.

**Shambler, in practice.** It does not close the distance with you. It arrives at it. It keeps coming while you are busy with something else and it will be where you put your foot. You never lose a shambler. You only ever set one down. The word means: a thing you can outwalk and cannot outwait.

## The Cinder Stalker

```
health   34
damage   12
poise    12
speed    64
tell      0.26 s
reach    20
posture  darter
relics    0
```

**What it was.** Not a person. Vale animals, changed by drawn-ash, that learned to hold food. Thin, quick, low-packed. They come out of tall grass and out of ash and out of nothing else, and only where the ground is Greying or worse.

**What it owed.** Nothing at all, and this is the one entry on this wall where that is a plain fact and not an argument. A Stalker never had a hold and never took one. It learned a habit the way a dog learns a door. The Cull and the Hearthkeepers have never once disagreed about a Stalker, and the Wardens note that as the only peace either party has ever kept.

**The tell.** Twenty-six hundredths. We say a quarter of a second, and we are not being loose, we are being quick about it.

Twenty-six hundredths is not a warning. It is the noise a thing makes on its way. There is no part of it you can use to begin anything. What you can do inside twenty-six hundredths is finish something you have already started, and that is the whole of the teaching.

Rin Ashfall killed the first Stalker anybody in this Vale ever saw, on the fourteenth day, because she was already mid-stroke in the grass and the stroke went on through it. She took the second across the ribs because she was in recovery, and the Ashblade going light leaves you a fifth of a second in which you are furniture. She killed that one as well and it took three strokes. Then the third and the fourth came together from the downhill side, and she stepped back off a strike onto loose ash and went down on one knee, and there was nothing left in the purse to get up with.

The mistake that kills people is not the first one. It is spending the purse on the first one. They do not come alone. Four came for Rin Ashfall, or three: Ketil Brack says four, Bran Holt's copy says three, Bel Brack said four and then said he had assumed the fourth from the way she turned. Every Warden trained since has been taught to plan for four and be pleased with three.

**Practical.** Sixty-four of speed. You cannot leave it. Nothing in this Vale can leave it.

Twenty of reach: six inside the Ashblade, twenty inside the Pike, two inside the Knife.

Thirty-four of health and twelve of packing, and twelve is the lowest figure on this wall. The Ashblade going light is fourteen of poise on twelve, so one light stroke of a sword opens a Stalker, and nothing else in the book does it in one at light. The received teaching is that a Stalker dies to one clean swing. The board's own arithmetic does not obviously allow it, and the Wardens teach it anyway, because it is what happens in the field: the thing arrives onto a stroke that is already moving and takes it where it is thin. Say both halves of that and do not settle it.

Twelve of damage. Eight landings and you are still there. The ninth is the one. On the fourteenth day the Hold had no word for any of this and worked it out afterwards, and the figure they wrote down was twelve, and twelve is twelve of what a body has.

**Darter, in practice.** It does not fight you. It crosses you. Everything it owns is in the first tenth of a second and in the last, and it will be out of your reach before your recovery is finished, so you will not get it on the answer. A darter offers you no trade. Two things work: a stroke that is already in motion, and a spear held still with somebody else's back against yours.

## The Gravewarden

```
health   90
damage   18
poise    55
speed    30
tell      0.62 s
reach    30
posture  bulwark
relics    1
```

**What it was.** An order. People. They bound relics into their own plate, plate by plate, on the theory that a body carrying enough borrowed hold cannot be opened by anything.

They were right. That was the problem.

They are still standing in the barrows above Stonewake Scarp, upright, packed, not dead and not anybody, and they will speak the names of the people whose holds are in the plating. On the fifty-third day a wielder of this Hold walked past one called Halde without drawing, because it said her mother's name. The board put six on Mercy and two on Communion and took three off Dominion, and that evening it read The Wake for the first time in its life. The Cull have never forgiven the fifty-third day and they are not wrong to bring it up.

We call ourselves by the old order's word. Nobody has proposed a better one. Say it slowly the first time it is given to you.

**What it owed.** Everything, and it still has it, and that is the difficulty entire. A Gravewarden is carrying other people's holds and will not put them down. When you put it down it leaves one relic, and the relic is somebody.

The first relic in this Vale came off a barrow on the thirteenth day and sat on a shelf in the Smithy for six days while the smith did not know what to do with it. Then it went under the stone at Wrenfield, where it is holding eleven paces of Quick grass in the middle of Gone ground, and the Foragers walk out to it across ash and stand in it and come back and will not say what it is like.

**The tell.** Sixty-two hundredths. We say six tenths.

That is the longest warning anything in the Vale gives you. You can watch it arrive. You can count while it arrives. Every part of the motion is honest and visible from the far side of a barrow.

It kills people anyway. This is the sentence the Wardens of this Hold have said aloud more often than any other: six tenths of a second is long enough to decide twice, and the second decision is usually the wrong one.

You decide to go in. Then, with three tenths left, you decide you were wrong, and you go out with your weight already committed inward, and thirty of reach does not care which way you are leaning. Or you choose one side and then, still inside the tell, you take the other. A Gravewarden is the most honest thing in the valley and it eats people who think under pressure.

Decide once. If the once was wrong, be wrong all the way through it.

A parry is twelve hundredths of a second. We teach it here and almost nobody learns it. Six tenths is the only tell in the Vale long enough for an ordinary body to find that twelve, which is the one kindness in this entry, and it has been taken up perhaps four times.

**Practical.** Thirty of speed against thirty of reach. It cannot chase you and does not need to.

Fifty-five of packing, and this number is the whole fight. Until it is broken you are hitting a wall on purpose. The Widow's Maul going heavy is sixty of poise, which is one landing. The Ashblade going heavy is thirty-four, which is two. The Reaper Pike going heavy is twenty-six, which is three. The Hearth Knife going heavy is sixteen, which is four, and the only person who ever went at a figure like that with a kitchen tool spent forty minutes on the scarp doing it, and she was taking up a man and not a Gravewarden, and the Wardens cite it regardless.

Ninety of health once the packing is open. Eighteen of damage, which is twice what a Husk does and half again what a Stalker does, and six landings takes a body from a hundred to the ground. The sixth is not a thing to plan around.

The scarp has taken two of this Hold's wielders in the same fifty feet and both were carrying the Maul. Maren Verren on the eighty-fourth day. Tarn Stonewake on the hundred and fortieth. The Hold still sends people up because the lime is under the barrows and the fields want lime.

Wren Ashfall met one at the ninth ring in the Grey Ash on the two hundred and ninth day. The Forager who watched from a hundred paces says she broke the packing, and that he heard it go, and the sound was a seam giving in wet timber, and she was in on it and she had it. Then she went down on one knee. Not from a blow. From the ash.

**Bulwark, in practice.** It will not come to you and it does not need to. The fight is not about hitting it. The fight is about getting in, and getting in means the packing, and any stroke that does not move the packing is not worth the purse it costs. A body that empties itself in front of a bulwark without touching the packing has not fought it. It has waited in front of it with its hands busy.

## Echoes

Echoes are not on this wall with the others and they are not called enemies on the board. They are called by the name of the person. Always.

**The standing rule.** A Warden who says *the husk in the west field* about a wielder's Echo is corrected, coldly, by whoever hears it. Nobody is struck and nobody is fined. The correcting is done out loud, in front of whoever is standing there, by the nearest person regardless of rank or years, and a Warden corrected once does it correctly for the rest of their life.

This is not the Tellers' doctrine and it is not sentiment. It is the practical part of this document. A Husk walks a chore. An Echo fights with the build it died with, and the skill it died with, and everything the Vale has fed it since. Say *husk* and you will go out planning for forty-five hundredths and twenty of packing and a thing you can outwalk, and not one of those figures will be true.

Six of the eighteen verbs take an Echo. Every one of the six takes a name.

**Growth.** A growth every two world-days while it is unclaimed. The days counted are days-lying-out, not days since the fall, because the clock belongs to whoever is outside. Bran Holt worked that out on the twentieth day and wrote four lines about it, and those four lines are why the Hold counts as it does. The Hold has always dated growths from deaths anyway, and has always been wrong to, and no hand has gone back to correct the older entries, because correcting them would be a lie of a different kind.

| growth | days lying out | what changes |
|---|---|---|
| first | two | it stands. It holds its ring. |
| second | four | more in it than the person ever had. It steps into your recovery. |
| third | six | it leaves the ring and roams. Ground it crosses goes toward Ashen. |
| fourth | eight | it has crossed country. No Warden goes at one with a load on his back. |
| fifth | ten | it comes to the palisade. |

**The ring.** It bends the grass inward around itself and pales it at the root out to the rim. Three paces on the first day. Eleven by the fourth. Past the rim the grass stands up green, and you can put your hand on the line between them.

Below the third growth it comes to the rim and stops. It does not follow you out. This was observed on the nineteenth day and has been observed eleven times since and it holds every time until the third growth, and then it stops holding, and that single change is the only reason this Hold has ever had to defend a palisade.

When it is reclaimed the ring stands back up. All of it, out to eleven paces, in about the time it takes to draw a breath, and the grip opens as it goes. The pale at the root stays pale, stops spreading inside the hour, and never resumes. Where a stone is raised over the ring with a relic set beneath it, those eleven paces hold against the decay permanently.

**Observed, with days.**

*Rin Ashfall.* Taken up in Wrenfield on the nineteenth day at the second growth, four days late. The ring was three paces on the fourteenth and eleven by then. It had her shoulders and it had the Ashblade and it had learned a habit she never had in life, which was to step into the end of a stroke and be inside when it finished. That is what a growth is. Not a bigger thing. A better one.

*Yeva Holt.* Left unburied on the hundred and twenty-eighth day and walked past on the hundred and thirtieth at the fourth growth, her ring six paces in the charcoal burners' clearing. Fourth growth on the hundred and thirtieth, fifth on the hundred and thirty-second. The Foragers tracked what it crossed: clearing to Brack ford, ford to the low rows, low rows to the mere road, mere road to the fence line. Every one of those went one state worse in the four days it took to walk it. The low rows were Quick in the spring and are Greying now and have never fully come back. The map is cut into a board still standing in the Hearth and it is the reason this Hold understands region decay at all.

An Echo does not walk through country. It walks the country out. One state worse per region crossed, at the fifth.

On the hundred and thirty-fourth day it came up the mere road to the north-east arc, where the wall is timber because a Carver was pulled out of her shed on the ninety-fifth day and four Carvers became three. It put the Ashblade into the timber twice at head height and used the blade as a step, and came over. Not through. Over. No Husk, no Stalker and no Gravewarden has ever done that. It is in four accounts in almost the same words and it is the most frightening sentence in the Chronicle. It is not an animal. It has a person's habits and a person's tools and nothing of the person in it.

*Nix Ember.* Roaming from the hundred and seventy-sixth day, out of the Vale on the hundred and eighty-fourth, buried on the hundred and ninetieth in Holt Vale by people who had never met him.

*Wren Ashfall.* Taken up at the ninth ring in the Grey Ash on the two hundred and tenth day at the second growth, the ring four days old and eleven paces, and there was no grass at the ninth ring, so what stood back up was ash. It had Orrin Dunn's habit, which she never had in life: it stepped in on the trailing side and came round. It had that because she had taken Orrin Dunn up on the two hundred and third day and carried his motion north with her, and by the two hundred and tenth the ring had them both.

**The fourth growth at the palisade on the hundred and ninety-sixth day.**

This entry is last because nobody wants it read twice.

Orrin Dunn, Warden before he was a wielder, whose shoulder went wrong on the hundred and ninth day and stayed wrong for life, was killed at our own palisade on the hundred and ninety-sixth by a fourth growth that belonged to nobody this Hold can name. It came into the Vale from outside it. Its plate is of no pattern anybody here recognises and the Smithy has said so in writing. There is a name cut into something it carried, in a hand nobody in this Vale can read.

Vane Hold lost four wielders outside in its last thirty days and there are no rings within a morning's walk of that wall, so four Vane Echoes have been roaming and drifting and feeding for a very long time. This may be one of them. Nobody claims it. Nobody here wants to discuss it twice, and the Wardens who say this page aloud have taken to stopping at the end of the line above.

The board can only be honest about what a Teller entered into it. Somewhere there is a Teller who stopped.
