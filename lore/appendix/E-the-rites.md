## The grief arithmetic

A death costs the Hold `10 + 3 * (number of bonds the dead had)`. The bonds are Spouse, Sibling, Rival, Friend, Stranger, plus the bonds the Hold has decided to call other bonds but has not codified. The well-loved cost more. This is the cruellest arithmetic in the Vale and everyone knows it.

The grief pool suppresses work. The output is multiplied by `clamp(1 - grief/200, 0.35, 1.0)`. At two hundred of grief a Hold works at just over a third and cannot build its way out. The arithmetic is on the board and the board is the board.

The grief pool is spent by the Vigils. The grief pool is replenished by deaths. The grief pool is what the Hold has not given back. The grief pool is the hold that has been released and not yet given and the hold sits in the settlement like standing water and it makes the work go badly.

The well-loved cost more. The cost is on the board. The board does not average. The board is the board.

## The five Vigils

### 1. Burial

**Grief:** twenty. **Relic:** none. **Legacy:** +6 max health, all later successors.

The body into ground, whole, with its gear. The blade, the flask, the boots, the pack. The body and the gear are put down together. The grave is dug. The grave is at the Memorial, in the eastern range, in the row, between the rows, in the spot the body is going to lie in for as long as the Memorial stands.

The liturgy is read at the grave. The liturgy is four sentences, said by the Healer or by the Teller or by the person who dug the grave. The four sentences are:

*The Loosing. That one is done. Nobody did it and nobody can undo it.*
*The Holding. That was where we were. It is closed and it is nobody's.*
*The Keeping. That is ours, and we have not done it yet.*
*We do it now.*

Then the body is lowered. Then the grave is filled. Then the first pane is cut, when there is pearl, and the pane is fluxed with pearl from a pyre.

The slowest Keeping. The deepest. The Burial is what the Burial is for. The Burial is the Vigil the Hold holds when the Hold has decided the Burial is owed.

**Standing rule.** A Burial is owed when the Hold has decided the Burial is owed. The Hold decides on the day of the death and the decision is the deciding.

### 2. Pyre

**Grief:** thirty. **Relic:** none. **Legacy:** +1 Ash Flask charge.

The body and gear burned together. The pyre is built at the Smithy's hot end, on the pyre floor, on the four flags the four Carvers laid in an afternoon on day 31. The pyre floor is the floor every cake of soap, every pane and every flask in the book comes off.

Same-day Keeping on the pyre floor means no Holding. No ring. No purse. No Echo. The Cull's argument stands on the pyre floor.

The pyre yields the ash the Flask is cut from. Pyre-ash steeped, settled three days, drawn off clear, cut with hearth-water until it will not strip a throat. Drunk. Burns going down, keeps burning. Gives back hold already spent, briefly, at cost. Three is the standing ration, three is not caution, four is the Pyre's Legacy.

The Pyre's cost is what the Pyre is paid for. The Pyre's Legacy is what the Wake is paid for. The Wake is the most expensive Vigil the Hold has. The Pyre is what the Wake is built on.

**Standing rule.** The Pyre is the Vigil the Hold holds when the Hold has decided the Hold is going to fight, and the Hold is going to fight because the Flask is what the Flask is and the Flask is the Hold's fighting.

### 3. Telling

**Grief:** twenty-five. **Relic:** none. **Legacy:** +8% Composure regeneration.

The death recounted aloud, in full, in front of everyone, by someone who saw it. The Telling takes about an hour. The Telling is held at the Memorial, with the panes behind and the ash pit to the east and every working person standing in a half circle with their backs to the open air.

The Teller holds the graver. The Teller holds the graver because the graver is the only implement and the graver is the implement the Teller is holding.

The Telling is not a funeral. The Telling is a piece of inheritance. The Legacy is on every wielder who comes after and the Legacy is the inheritance.

Maren Verren told Rin's death on the twenty-first day and got two details wrong and Bran Holt wrote down the Telling and the corrections and did not choose. That is the best thing Bran Holt ever did and he did it because he was not asked to choose.

**Standing rule.** The Telling is held by a person who saw the death. If no person who saw the death is left, the Telling is held by a person who heard about the death from a person who saw the death, and the corrections are entered with the Telling.

### 4. Monument

**Grief:** forty. **Relic:** one. **Legacy:** +10% weapon power, +1 hold of ground.

Stone raised over a *reclaimed* Echo, relic set beneath. The stone holds ground. The Monument is the only Vigil that takes ground back, and the ground is held in eleven paces around the stone and the eleven paces are Quick in the middle of Greying or Gone because the relic under the stone is doing the work the relic was bound to do.

You cannot raise stone over somebody still standing in a field. The Monument requires a reclaimed Echo. The Echo must be put down first. The relic from the Echo is the relic the Stone is set with. The Stone is what the Stone is for.

The first Monument was raised at Wrenfield on the nineteenth day, with Maren's relic, the relic from the Gravewarden she had killed earlier that morning. The Monument is what Wrenfield's Monument is. The Monument holds Wrenfield at eleven paces of Quick in the middle of Gone.

**Standing rule.** A Monument requires a reclaimed Echo and a relic. You cannot raise stone over somebody still standing in a field. If you have not fetched the Echo, you do not have the relic. If you do not have the relic, you cannot raise the Monument.

### 5. Wake

**Grief:** sixty. **Relic:** none. **Legacy:** +5% damage, **clears all grief**.

The whole Hold drinks. Everything unspent is spent at once. No liturgy. No cutting. No raising. No fire. The Hold and a cask in the yard, every name spoken once, oldest first. People stay out until light.

A Wake has no liturgy because a Wake is not a Keeping. A Wake is what happens after the Keepings. A Wake is the Vigil that says the Keepings are done and the Keepings have been paid for and the Keepings are what the Wake is built on.

A Wake takes sixty of grief and leaves none. A Hold cannot Wake out of a shortage. A Hold can only Wake out of a surplus. Two Wakes in the book: day 136 (pool stood at 68, paid 60, the other 8 went with it) and day 210 (Grieve paid 48 as a Friend).

A Wake is the only Vigil that clears grief. A Wake is the only Vigil that closes the grief pool. A Wake is the only Vigil that gives the Hold a clean board and a clean pool.

**Standing rule.** A Wake is held when the Echoes are at zero and the lineage is not empty and the pool can pay. A Wake is held on a day the Wake can be held on. A Wake is held when the Wake is held.
