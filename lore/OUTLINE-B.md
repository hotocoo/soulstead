# PART V — THE LINEAGE

Eight chapters, chronological, saga register at its tightest. Genealogy is the structure.
Each chapter is one wielder: who they were before, the bond that shaped them, the blade,
the reign, the death, the Succession that followed. No Other Roads sections in this part.
The point of Part V is that the roads are behind them now and the line only goes forward.

Every chapter in this part must obey the same four-beat shape and must not feel like it:
**the Succession, the reign's work, the death, the board.**

## Chapter 35: Colm Grey, Rival
**Hand:** Asha Vane · **Days:** 84 to 85, with the reign to 109 summarised forward · **Place:** Stonewake Scarp, the Hold
- Day 84. Maren Verren has held the blade for sixty-nine days, longer than anyone will again, and she dies on the scarp to a Gravewarden at eighteen of damage and thirty of reach, in the same place Rin took the Vale's first relic.
- Her death written with maximum restraint. She says something about the quarry face. Two, zero, zero, minus three.
- The Succession: three names. Colm Grey, who disliked her for eleven years about a fence; Yeva Holt, who is needed; Sable Kettle, who is nineteen and cooking.
- *Never liked them. Will still carry the blade.* The rival's arithmetic: ten in a hundred more to spend, a tenth less of whatever Resolve is, and her Echo's pull does not touch him at all. The Hold chooses him for exactly that reason and he knows it.
- The Reaper Pike: forty of reach, a one-body arc, the weapon of a man who has decided never to be close to anything again. He reforges a Field-hand's tool to get it.
- The reign summarised forward in saga catalogue: what he did, where, and the fact that he never once went into Wrenfield.
**Ledger:** rows 23, 24.

## Chapter 36: Yeva Holt, Sibling
**Hand:** Asha Vane · **Days:** 109 to 116 · **Place:** the Grey Ash, the Hold
- Day 109. Colm Grey, who killed two Gravewardens and a tier three, is killed by an Ash Husk in the Grey Ash, at nine of damage, because he was out of everything. Saga irony, stated without comment.
- The Succession. Yeva Holt is called his sibling on the board. Two testimonies say half-sister, one says it was never true and the Hold wanted the Legacy. Grieve records all three and does not settle it.
- *Blood of the fallen.* What the sibling bond buys: reclaiming his Echo grants a Legacy outright, no grief, no rite. The Hold chooses her for the free Legacy and everyone can do that arithmetic.
- She takes the Ashblade, which has been on a hook since Rin, and the Hold notices.
- Day 116, she reclaims Colm on the ash flat. The free Legacy, and the fact that the board reads **The Wake** afterwards for the first time in sixty-three days, and Asha Vane writes *the board is a liar with good handwriting* in the margin.
- Memory clamps at one hundred here. Note the clamp in clerical voice. It never moves again for the rest of the book, and that is its own kind of ending.
**Ledger:** rows 30, 31, 32.

## Chapter 37: Tarn Stonewake, Stranger
**Hand:** Asha Vane · **Days:** 121 to 130 · **Place:** Brackwood, the Hold
- Day 121. Yeva Holt, eleven days in, dies to Cinder Stalkers in Brackwood, in ground the Hold pushed back on day 47 and has not maintained since.
- The Succession. Nobody left who knew her well. Tarn Stonewake, quarryman, forty-one, Steady and Grim, who never learned her name. *Never learned their name.*
- The stranger's arithmetic, honestly stated: fifteen in a hundred more to spend, a fifth less Resolve, and no grief penalty at all inside the palisade. He is the most efficient wielder the Hold ever has and the Hold likes him least.
- He takes the Widow's Maul off the wall, which is a decision the Hold reads as arrogance and is actually the opposite: he chose the weapon that punishes hesitation because he has none.
- Day 128, he sets out with Yeva unburied, because nobody made him and there was no rite he owed her. Day 130, he walks past her Echo in Brackwood. Narrate both as lived scenes, on ordinary evenings, with no sense of consequence attached.
- **The ledger entries for days 128 and 130 do NOT belong to this chapter.** They are recorded in Chapter 32, which owns that whole week as one unit. End this chapter with one clerical line pointing the reader to the side-road account where the entries are, and do not retell the raid.
**Ledger:** rows 33, 34 only (Day 121 `wielder_died` Yeva Holt; Day 122 `successor_taken` Tarn Stonewake). Standing after: 100/73/82/6, drifting toward The Open Hearth.

## Chapter 38: Sable Kettle, Friend
**Hand:** Asha Vane, last entries in the third hand · **Days:** 140 to 147 · **Place:** Stonewake Scarp, the Hold
- Day 140. Tarn Stonewake dies on the scarp, like Maren, to a Gravewarden, and Asha Vane writes two lines about how the scarp has now taken two wielders in the same fifty feet and the Hold still sends people there because the lime is there.
- The Succession: Sable Kettle, twenty-four, Quick and Tender, who drank with him most nights and is the only person who did. *Drank with the dead most nights.*
- The friend's arithmetic: nothing changes about her body, and her Vigils are cheap. The Hold does not understand yet that this is the most valuable bond on the board. Chapter 43 is paid for by it.
- She carries the Hearth Knife and nothing else. Nine of power, eighteen of reach, six hundredths of a second from decision to edge. The Wardens try for a week to make her take a real weapon.
- How she survives: she is never committed to anything for longer than a quarter of a second, and the whole chapter is a demonstration that the Vale's combat rewards the opposite of what it looks like it rewards.
- Day 147, she reclaims Tarn, with a kitchen knife, on the scarp, and it takes her forty minutes.
- Asha Vane's hand ends here. Say how, in one sentence, without ceremony.
**Ledger:** rows 40, 41, 42.

## Chapter 39: Nix Ember and the Greying of Wrenfield
**Hand:** Grieve Mourne, fourth hand, first entries · **Days:** 148 to 156 · **Place:** Wrenfield, the Hold
- Grieve takes the hand on day 140 and his first real entry is the worst one in the book's second half.
- Day 152. Wrenfield goes Gone. Minus four, minus seven, minus five, minus four. The cause is not dramatic: three seasons of nobody maintaining what chapter 22 proved could be maintained, plus a roaming Echo's crossings, plus the Hold's labour going to the palisade instead of the furrows after chapter 32.
- What Gone looks like on ground the reader has walked through in five chapters. Full elegiac passage here, earned.
- The Cull are proved right in public and are not gracious about it. This is the chapter where their argument is at its strongest and the Hold is at its least able to answer.
- Day 155, Sable Kettle dies in what is left of Wrenfield, carrying a kitchen knife, at fifteen days into a season with no barley.
- The Succession: Nix Ember, Reckless and Scarred, her rival, who wanted the blade and said so, which no candidate has ever done before.
**Ledger:** rows 43, 44, 45.

## Chapter 40: Orrin Dunn and the Pushing Back
**Hand:** Grieve Mourne · **Days:** 157 to 173 · **Place:** Wrenfield, the Grey Ash
- Nix Ember's sixteen days: the most aggressive reign the Hold has, all of it aimed at one thing, which is getting Wrenfield back.
- Day 163. Wrenfield pushed back from Gone. Plus four, plus seven, plus six, plus two. It takes the whole Hold, eleven days, every relic on the shelf, and the lime from the scarp that killed two wielders.
- The board does not average day 152 and day 163. Both stand. Grieve writes a page on what that means about ledgers and about people.
- Day 168, Sable Kettle reclaimed with the Ashblade by the woman who disliked her.
- Day 172, Nix Ember dies in the Grey Ash to Ash Husks, in the same ground as Colm Grey, out of everything, exactly as he was. Grieve notes the repetition and refuses to make it mean anything.
- The Succession: Orrin Dunn, Ketil Dunn's son, Steady and Grim, her friend. He takes the Reaper Pike, rebound with Maren Verren's relic from chapter 28, and the chapter's last image is a man holding a weapon that remembers a motion he watched a woman make when he was a boy.
**Ledger:** rows 46, 47, 48, 49.

## Chapter 41: Tove Rowan, and the Two Boards
**Hand:** Grieve Mourne · **Days:** 176 to 190 · **Place:** Wrenfield, Holt Vale, the crossing post
- The two-lineage chapter. Holt Vale's wielder is Tove Rowan, the Wayfarer from chapters 17 and 26, who stopped walking, married into a hold two ridges east, and took their blade on their day one hundred and sixty.
- Establish how two holds share a season without sharing ground: a route-mark on a post, a Teller at each end, and lines copied both ways. Four lineages at most. What crosses is entries.
- Day 180. Orrin Dunn and Tove Rowan are in the same field on the same afternoon, and she goes down, and he lifts her off the ground. Plus two, plus eight, plus nine, minus two. Both boards move. Both Tellers copy it.
- Day 176, Nix Ember's Echo starts roaming. Day 184 it leaves the Vale. `echo_sent`: plus three, minus two, plus six, plus four, and the board reads **The Wake** while Nix Ember is walking in somebody else's barley. Grieve writes the sentence about what a ledger can be honest about.
- Day 190. Holt Vale buries Nix Ember. Their wielder cuts an Ashfall name into Holt glass. Plus six, plus seven, plus nine, minus one, on Ashfall's board, for something Ashfall did not do.
- Mercy stands at ninety. Communion is clamped at one hundred. Grieve states the position plainly: the Vale is now bigger than the Vale.
**Ledger:** rows 50, 51, 52.

## Chapter 42: Wren Ashfall, Spouse
**Hand:** Grieve Mourne · **Days:** 196 to 203 · **Place:** the palisade, the Grey Ash
- Day 196. Orrin Dunn dies at the palisade against a tier four that belonged to nobody the Hold can name: an Echo drifted in from a valley whose chronicle nobody has a copy of. It has a name and the Hold cannot read the hand it is written in.
- Grieve records this and it frightens him more than any other entry, because the board can only be honest about what a Teller entered, and somewhere there is a Teller who stopped.
- The Succession: Wren Ashfall, Orrin's wife, Scarred and Tender, and the last of the founding family's name in the Hold. The book's second widow, one hundred and eighty-two days after the first.
- She takes the Ashblade. The chapter should make the reader feel the object's whole history in one paragraph and then stop.
- Her twelve days: she goes north, into the Grey Ash, on the argument that the Hold has never once cleared it and every Echo the Vale has lost has gone that way.
- Day 203, she reclaims Orrin. Plus six, plus three, plus one, plus two. Echoes at zero, and the board reads The Wake, and this time there is a person in the Hold who has decided to make it true.
**Ledger:** rows 53, 54, 55.

# PART VI — THE FIVE LAST PAGES

Five chapters, one per ending, each fully realised, each true. Per `CANON.md` section 13
they belong to five different holds, so all five can stand in one book without
contradiction. Chapter 43 is Ashfall's own and is the book's climax. The other four are
documents Grieve has copied, and each must be written in a different hand and a different
mood, and Grieve annotates each one at its close in no more than half a page.

## Chapter 43: The Wake
**Hand:** Grieve Mourne · **Days:** 204 to 210 · **Place:** the Grey Ash, the Memorial, the Hearth
- Day 209. Wren Ashfall dies in the Grey Ash to a Gravewarden, six days into a clearance nobody asked her to attempt, with the Grey Ash at Ashen and falling.
- The Succession, and there is almost nobody left to call: Grieve Mourne, Teller, thirty-two, Grim and Steady, who drank with her, which makes him Friend, which makes his Vigils cheap. *Drank with the dead most nights.*
- The Teller takes the blade. He has never carried anything. The chapter must be honest about how badly he does it: he wins by attrition, poise, and the fact that a Gravewarden's tell is six tenths of a second long and he has spent twenty years reading things slowly.
- Day 210. He reclaims Wren Ashfall, which brings the standing Echoes to zero for the eleventh and last time.
- The Wake: sixty of grief, the whole Hold, everything unspent spent at once. Plus eight, plus four, plus five, zero. Memory one hundred, Mercy one hundred, Communion one hundred, Dominion one.
- The board locks. *The Wake.* Every name spoken, every Echo laid down.
- And then the last page of the chapter, which is the book's real ending: Grieve walks to Kettlemere and stands in the shallows where Bel Kettle's Echo used to be, and writes that the lake was never on the board, and that the Vale has declared itself finished over a village still in the Holding, and that he is not going to settle it.
**Ledger:** rows 56, 57, 58, 59.

## Chapter 44: The Long Hold
**Hand:** Vane Hold's last Teller, name illegible, copied by Grieve · **Days:** Vane's own count, ending at their day 88 · **Place:** Vane Hold
- The document Grieve carried off the Vane Road board and the eleven pages still legible inside the gate.
- Voice: clerical, deteriorating. It starts as a competent ledger and becomes a list. The last four pages have no sentences, only verbs and numbers.
- The history it records: a hold that lost four wielders in thirty days, conscripted nine people, culled its own sick twice, and then stopped opening the gate. Dominion fifty-one. Mercy minus twenty-three.
- The reasoning, which is correct: the clock runs only while somebody is outside. Nobody outside, nothing decays, nothing comes. They solved it.
- What they did not solve: one hundred and sixteen people who could not be Kept, because a Keeping needs grief spent and grief is only spent on rites and rites need somebody to hold them and everyone was inside doing nothing forever.
- The ending text quoted exactly as the board carried it: *You held the vale by force and buried nobody. The walls stand. Nobody inside remembers why they are worth holding.*
- Grieve's half page: the timber on the gate was cut from inside, and he counted the marks, and there were a great many of them and they were all recent.

## Chapter 45: Ash Inheritance
**Hand:** Verren Hold, four hands becoming one · **Days:** Verren's day 1 to their day 240 · **Place:** Verren Hold, two valleys east
- The Wayfarer copy in which the Unnaming won. The most formally ambitious chapter in the book.
- Verren Hold took the road chapter 25 refused: the panes shaved in order of age, oldest first, on the theory that the oldest names are calling the longest.
- It appears to work for sixty days. Record that honestly. Their Memory falls and their grief falls with it and their output rises and for two seasons Verren Hold is the most prosperous settlement in any chronicle anybody has.
- Then the arithmetic: no names, no Tellings, no Monuments, no Legacies. Every successor starts at the floor. Their wielders die faster and faster and their Memory passes minus forty on their day 191.
- The formal move: over the last forty pages the four hands converge into one, and the one is not any of them, and it signs entries with the name of a wielder who died on their day 203, and the entries are competent, and the board keeps being kept.
- Ending text quoted exactly: *The lineage forgot itself. Your own Echo walks the fields now, and the villagers call it by your name because it is the only name they have left.*
- Grieve's half page: he has read this copy nine times. He notes that the handwriting in the last forty pages is better than the handwriting before it. He does not settle what that means.

## Chapter 46: The Open Hearth
**Hand:** Tove Rowan, Teller and wielder of Holt Vale · **Days:** their day 160 to their day 246 · **Place:** Holt Vale
- The concurrent ending, from the other side of chapter 41. Warmest chapter in the book, and it must not be sentimental. Communion is a logistics achievement, not a feeling.
- Tove Rowan's own account of being lifted off the ground in Wrenfield on Ashfall's day 180, which differs from Grieve's account in three particulars. Both stand.
- How Holt Vale got there: they buried everything that arrived. Eleven foreign Echoes in eighty days, from four valleys, including Nix Ember of Ashfall. Their Memorial is a third strangers by pane count.
- The cost, stated plainly: their own lineage is thinner, their Legacies fewer, their wielders weaker than Ashfall's, and they have been raided twice by Echoes they chose not to chase.
- The mechanism made concrete: route-marks on eleven posts, four Tellers copying both ways, and a road that now has traffic on it because a hold at the end of it is known to bury strangers.
- Ending text quoted exactly: *Strangers' Echoes were buried beside your own. Other settlements send travellers now. The valley is bigger than one lineage.*
- Grieve's half page: Ashfall reached the same Communion number and got a different ending, because The Long Hold and The Wake are checked before The Open Hearth, and the board's order of questions decided which of two identical valleys got which name. He finds this intolerable and records it.

## Chapter 47: The Quiet Field
**Hand:** unsigned · **Days:** an unnumbered fragment · **Place:** a hold whose name was never cut anywhere
- The commonest ending in the world, and the chapter that must be better than the four before it.
- Seven pages, unsigned, no hold name, found folded into a Wayfarer's copy as packing. Somebody's complete chronicle, or the middle of one.
- It records: four deaths, three Vigils, one Echo reclaimed and one abandoned, a region that went to Turning and came back, a wedding, a bad season, a good one, and a wielder who held the blade for a hundred and forty days and then handed it to somebody else alive, which is not a verb the board has.
- No triumph. No collapse. No faction wins. Nobody is remembered outside those seven pages and the seven pages have no name on them.
- Ending text quoted exactly: *No triumph, no collapse. The work continued. Someone will pick up the blade tomorrow.*
- Grieve's half page, and it should be the most moving thing in the book: he has tried for eleven years to identify this hold. He cannot. He has copied the fragment into the Chronicle in full so that it is in two places instead of one, and that is the only rite available for a hold with no name, and he performed it.

# PART VII — BEYOND

Five chapters. Each takes one ending forward past its last page. Register shifts toward
elegiac and, in the final chapter, toward the clerical. No new lore may be invented here
that contradicts Part VI; everything is consequence.

## Chapter 48: After the Wake
**Hand:** Grieve Mourne · **Days:** 211 to 400-odd, undated in places · **Place:** Ashfall Hold, the Vane Road
- What a Hold does after its board locks. The answer is: the work continues, which is the same answer as The Quiet Field, and Grieve notices.
- The Memorial becomes a road-shrine. Panes for anyone, from anywhere, and the Hold's pearl surplus becomes its trade.
- The Grey Ash, which killed Colm Grey, Nix Ember and Wren Ashfall, is cleared over four seasons by nobody remarkable.
- The lineage's Legacies listed in full, as inheritance, and what a successor in year three starts with compared to Rin Ashfall on day eleven. The genre's central inversion stated once, flatly, without triumph: they are stronger because those people died.
- Kettlemere, unresolved. A rite for water is attempted twice and fails twice. The Vale still has no verb for it.
- Grieve hands the graver to an apprentice and does not stop writing.

## Chapter 49: After the Long Hold
**Hand:** a hold two generations later, copied by nobody · **Place:** the Vane Road, Ashfall's ruin
- The loop chapter, and it closes chapter 1 exactly.
- Written from a settlement that has never heard of Ashfall Hold, excavating a barred gate on a road, reading four numbers off a board, and using the Old English figure: work of giants, nobody now living could build it, nobody now living knows why they would.
- The wall they are looking at is Ashfall's palisade in one reading and Vane's in another, and the chapter must be constructed so that both readings hold and neither is confirmed.
- Their Teller writes the same rule in the same words, having never read it: *I record what was said. I do not settle it.*
- The chapter's argument: The Long Hold is not an ending, it is a phase in a cycle, and the cycle is the only thing in the Vale that never decays.

## Chapter 50: After Ash Inheritance
**Hand:** disputed · **Place:** Verren Hold, and the Hearth
- The unreliable-narrator payoff, and it must be earned and not a trick.
- Grieve Mourne, old, compiling the appendices, finds the two wrong details from chapter 14 reproduced exactly in his own hand in an entry he does not remember writing.
- He checks the Verren copy. The convergence of hands in chapter 45 is described there as beginning with exactly this symptom.
- What he does about it is the chapter: he does not panic, he does not confess, he cuts his own name into a pane while alive, which no Teller has done, and he writes down the date so that whoever reads it can check whether the hand changes after.
- The chapter must not confirm whether Grieve is himself. Two accounts of the last page of the Chronicle exist, in the same hand, and they differ in three particulars.
- The Vale's whole metaphysics arrives at its worst implication and nobody explains it.

## Chapter 51: After the Open Hearth
**Hand:** Tove Rowan, then four Tellers, then no attribution at all · **Place:** eleven valleys
- The network chapter. What happens when enough holds bury enough strangers.
- Route-marks at every crossing. Tellers who have never met copying each other's entries within a season. A shared drift that no single hold can move alone, and the first argument in history conducted between two boards.
- The async mechanism made lore: an Echo written onto a folded page and carried out, arriving in a valley whose people never met its owner, and being buried by them, and the entry walking home a season later in somebody else's hand.
- The cost, honestly: a cruelty in one valley now moves the numbers in four, and the first hold to work that out on purpose is described, and it is not a villain, and it is not stopped.
- Last movement: the attributions stop. The entries continue. Whether that is The Open Hearth succeeding or Ash Inheritance arriving at scale is left exactly as open as it should be.

## Chapter 52: The Second Book
**Hand:** Grieve Mourne, and one other · **Place:** the Hearth
- The last chapter. Clerical, short, and it must land like a ledger line.
- The Chronicle is full. There is a physical limit: wax, vellum, the shelf, the pearl. A second book is begun, and the beginning of a second book is a rite the Vale has no name for and performs anyway.
- What is copied forward and what is not. The arguments about what to copy are the Four Readings one last time, in miniature, in one room, over an afternoon, and each of them wins something.
- The Legacies carry. The names carry. The grief does not, because a Wake spent it. The board is rewritten with the same four numbers at the top and a blank *Drifting toward*.
- Answer chapter 1's last line, exactly, in Grieve's hand or in the other hand, and do not say which.
- Final lines of the book must contain, somewhere, the two phrases the novel has been carrying since chapter 10 and chapter 6: the hillside, and the knife that was part of the grip. Do not underline them.

# APPENDICES

In-world documents, not reference tables with a costume on. Each is 1,500 to 3,000 words.
Numbers must match `CANON.md` and `LEDGER.md` exactly. Written in the clerical register,
with the Dark Souls principle: description over exposition, and an object's history told
as though the reader already knows what the object is.

## Appendix A: The Ledger of Verbs
`lore/appendix/A-ledger-of-verbs.md`
All eighteen verbs, in board order. For each: the exact four numbers, the exact sentence
the Teller writes, and one paragraph of in-world commentary on what the verb has cost the
Vale, with at least one dated example from the Chronicle. Include the arithmetic
arguments: why `foreign_echo_slain` raises Communion and why that unsettles everyone, why
`wielder_died` raises Memory, why `ally_abandoned` is the single worst entry available at
minus ten Communion, and why there is no verb for hospitality, for handing the blade to
someone alive, or for whatever it is that Kettlemere needs.

## Appendix B: The Five Last Pages, Resolved
`lore/appendix/B-the-five-last-pages.md`
The board's own procedure, written as a Teller's working note. The five endings in
checking order, the exact floors and ceilings, the gate on The Wake, and the fact that
order of questions decides between identical valleys (chapter 46's grievance). Worked
examples: Ashfall day 53, Ashfall day 184, Vane day 88, Verren day 191, Holt day 246. A
short honest section on what the board cannot know, using Kettlemere and the illegible
tier four from chapter 42.

## Appendix C: The Bestiary
`lore/appendix/C-bestiary.md`
Ash Husk, Cinder Stalker, Gravewarden, and Echoes by tier. Exact stats per `CANON.md`
section 6. Written as a Warden's training document: how to read each tell, what the
posture words mean in practice (shambler, darter, bulwark), what each thing was before,
and what it is owed. Include the Echo tier table with days, and the two observed facts
about tier three roaming and tier five raiding, and the Hold's standing rule that an Echo
is always called by the name of the person.

## Appendix D: The Four Blades
`lore/appendix/D-the-four-blades.md`
Ashblade, Reaper Pike, Widow's Maul, Hearth Knife. Exact frame data and costs per
`CANON.md` section 7, presented as a Smith's book: what the numbers mean to a body, which
bond suits which weapon, which wielder carried it and for how long, what relic is bound
into it now, and the motion each one remembers. Include Ketil Brack's notes on binding and
his refusal to bind two relics into one blade.

## Appendix E: The Rites
`lore/appendix/E-the-rites.md`
Burial, Pyre, Telling, Monument, Wake. Exact grief costs, relic requirements and Legacies
per `CANON.md` section 8. Written as liturgy plus working notes: what is said, what is
done, who may hold it, the grief arithmetic (ten and three per bond), the output formula
in the Vale's own words, and the standing rules (no Monument without a reclaimed Echo and
a relic, a Wake spends everything). Add the Hold's list of rites attempted and failed,
including the two for water.

## Appendix F: Bonds, Traits, Jobs
`lore/appendix/F-bonds-traits-jobs.md`
The five bonds with exact multipliers and the line said at a Succession; the six traits
with exact numbers and descriptions; the six jobs and what losing each one costs a Hold of
twenty-six. Written as the Hearth's Succession procedure, with the uncomfortable parts
left in: that a Stranger is the most efficient choice, that a Sibling is chosen for a free
Legacy, that a Friend makes the ending affordable, and that everyone at the Succession can
do this arithmetic while the body is still in the room.

## Appendix G: The Gazetteer
`lore/appendix/G-gazetteer.md`
Ashfall Hold and its six buildings; Wrenfield, the Grey Ash, Stonewake Scarp, Kettlemere,
Brackwood, the Vane Road. The eight tile substances. The five decay states with their
names (Quick, Turning, Greying, Ashen, Gone) and what each looks, sounds and smells like.
Encounters come out of tall grass and ash and nothing else, and a Forager explains why.
Include the fall-ash and drawn-ash test, the lime-kilns, the pearl chain, and the ninety-six
by seventy-two figure explained as a day's walk out and back.

## Appendix H: Names, Days, and Words
`lore/appendix/H-names-days-words.md`
Three documents in one file.
1. **The Naming.** The eighteen given names and twelve family names, with invented
   in-world etymology and the plain statement that the Vale has run out of names and
   reuses them, and what that does to a Memorial.
2. **The Count.** The timeline, day 1 to day 210, every dated event in the novel in one
   column, with the wielder holding the blade and the board's drift beside it. Then the
   dated events of Vane, Verren and Holt as far as the copies allow.
3. **The Words.** Glossary: hold, the Loosing, the Holding, the Keeping, Echo, Husk,
   Vigil, Legacy, grief, Composure, poise, tell, relic, pearl, lye, fall-ash, drawn-ash,
   locked ground, drift, route-mark, the board, the panes, the Readings, the Interval,
   going north. Each entry one to four sentences, in the Vale's own voice, with no modern
   equivalents supplied.
